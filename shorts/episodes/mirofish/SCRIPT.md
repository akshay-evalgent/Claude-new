# MiroFish: "He hoped for 1,000 stars."

Vertical short, 1080x1920, 60 fps, 48.0 s, loops on itself.
Repo: https://github.com/666ghj/MiroFish (AGPL-3.0). Live demo: https://666ghj.github.io/mirofish-demo/

## The angle

The tech is abstract (multi-agent social simulation), so the hook is the human story:
a college senior hoped for 1,000 stars, his next project got 83,686 and ¥30M from a
billionaire. Then the video earns that hype by showing the real product working, step by
step, in its own UI. It turns to the strangest real demo (a 260-year-old novel's lost
ending), does an honest sarcastic reality check using the project's own words, and ends
on a joke that uses the product's idea against the viewer: a swarm predicting whether
you'll follow.

Real footage carries the facts (the GitHub page, the live demo, the 1791 book, stock B-roll
for people). Coded animation carries the ideas (the swarm, god mode, a thousand futures).

1. He hoped for 1,000 stars. His next repo got 83,686.

**Description**
> A college senior hoped his project would get 1,000 GitHub stars. His next one, MiroFish, got 83,686 and reportedly ¥30M in backing from Shanda founder Chen Tianqiao.
>
> MiroFish turns a document (news, a policy draft, a report, even a novel) into a simulated world of AI agents with personalities and memory. They post and argue on simulated Twitter- and Reddit-style platforms, you drop in a "what if", and it writes a prediction report you can question.
>
> Repo: https://github.com/666ghj/MiroFish
> Live demo: https://666ghj.github.io/mirofish-demo/
> Simulation engine: OASIS by CAMEL-AI, https://github.com/camel-ai/oasis
>
> UI footage is from the public MiroFish demo. The swarm, god-mode and "will you follow" sequences are illustrations, not MiroFish output. Dream of the Red Chamber pages: National Library of China scan of the 1791 edition (public domain), via the Internet Archive. Fish: Wikimedia Commons (public domain). Other B-roll: Coverr.
