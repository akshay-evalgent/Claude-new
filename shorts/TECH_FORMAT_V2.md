# Tech Shorts that work on YouTube right now (research, Nov 16 2026)

## What failed and why

Our tech video used the Momentum Motivation formula: a **faceless** story with narration over borrowed clips. The data shows that tech viewers don't reward faceless recaps. Almost every tech channel that wins today has a **person on screen** with a point of view, usually in a funny way.

## The data

I pulled the Shorts tabs of about 50 English tech channels (up to 60 recent Shorts each) and compared the **median** views per Short, a channel's typical result rather than its lucky hits.

| Channel | Subs | Median views per Short | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alberta Tech** | 501K | **984K** (recent 1.1M); 30 of 60 over 1M | **Tech news and office life acted out as comedy skits** |
| Mrwhosetheboss | 23M | 10M | Gadget superlatives on camera (a huge, established channel) |
| ByteByteGo | 1.4M | 382K | Animated system-design explainers (an established brand) |
| Matty McTech | 1.5M | 256K | Face + green-screen tech tricks ("Google secrets they don't want you to know") |
| ThePrimeagen | 550K | 209K | Programmer hot takes on camera |
| Tech.Explain | 361K | 172K | Person in a workshop answering curiosity questions ("Does a full USB weigh more than an empty one?") |
| Matt's Computer Services | 362K | 160K | Real PC repair footage with captions |
| Signal 404 (faceless) | 20K | 49K (recent 84K) | Text story about tech oddities (our 7-second template) |
| Github Awesome (faceless) | 64K | 23K | One repo per Short |
| Faceless AI-tool/news channels (about 15 checked) | 10K–600K | **0.1K–8K** | Narrated AI news, tool lists, tutorials (what we tried) |

**Key finding:** Alberta Tech covers **the same stories we researched**, but as 30-second skits. Examples:
- *"The half second delay that saved the internet"*: the xz backdoor, 414K views
- *"How the Jensen Huang affair started"*: 1.5M
- *"CEO finds out tokens cost money"*: 8.2M
- *"Re-hiring employees replaced by AI"*: 2.8M

What makes those work is the delivery: a relatable character instead of a narrator.

## The recommended format: "Tech news, acted out" (20–40s)

**Structure** (from Alberta Tech's top 60):

| Part | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Premise title** | on screen from frame 1 | A white box at the top with black text, written as a situation: "CEO finds out tokens cost money", "If devs gave accurate estimates", "Your co-worker who 'knows how to code'" |
| **Cold open** | 0–2s | Start mid-scene with the funniest or most absurd line. No intro, no logo. |
| **Escalation** | 2–25s | 3–5 back-and-forth lines. Each one makes the situation more absurd and, if it's news, slips in **one real fact**. |
| **Punchline / twist** | last 3–5s | The line people quote in the comments. Cut straight away, with no outro, so the loop restarts naturally. |

**How to film it:**
- **Cast:** one person can play every role. Change one prop for each character (glasses, hoodie, blazer, mug) and cut between them. A friend or co-worker makes it better.
- **Location:** a real office, desk or laptop. It must look like a real workplace.
- **Equipment:** a phone, vertical 4K/30, a cheap clip-on mic, and natural light from a window.
- **Captions:** the dialogue burned in at the bottom, plus the premise title box at the top. I can add both automatically.
- **Workload:** 3–5 skits per filming session, at about 10 minutes each.

**Titles:**
- Write the title as the premise itself: "CEO finds out…", "Your co-worker who…", "If X was Y", "X in 2023 vs 2026", "The entire X situation in 30s".
- Short, with no emojis or one at most.
- Use the searchable name when there is one: Claude, ChatGPT, Nvidia, iPhone.

**Why it suits YouTube's rules:** it's 100% original, with your face, voice, script and footage. That's the strongest possible position for monetisation, and you can add sponsors later. Alberta Tech does paid skits "with HubSpot", "feat AWS" and "with Codex".

## 10 scripts, ready to film

These are based on current events. **Check each fact against its source on the day you post**, because some of these stories are only days old. They're all written with generic characters, and none of them makes claims about a real person's private life.

### 1. "The job interview that cost $6.3 billion"
Binance hack. Source: [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/03/binance-hack-details-linkedin/), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/02/nx-s1-6023424/fbi-says-north-korean-hackers-are-behind-the-theft-of-6-3b-from-binance)

> **RECRUITER** (too friendly): "Love your profile! Quick technical test, just download this little file."
>
> **ENGINEER:** "Sure! …Should it be asking for admin access?"
>
> **RECRUITER:** "Totally normal. Culture fit."
>
> *(cut to)* **BOSS** (staring at laptop): "Why are we missing six point three billion dollars?"
>
> **ENGINEER:** "…I think I got the job."

*Pinned comment:* "Would you have clicked it? Be honest 👇"

### 2. "AI demo on stage vs AI demo in rehearsal"
OpenAI DevDay demo stall. Source: [Futurism](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/openai-agent-fails-live-demo)

> **REHEARSAL** (presenter): "Book me a table." **AI:** "Done. I also did your taxes."
>
> **ON STAGE, 3,000 people watching:** "Book me a table." **AI:** "…" **PRESENTER** (smiling hard): "It's having a slow morning."
>
> **AI** (whispering): "I'm nervous."

### 3. "Your vibe-coded app on launch day"
Apps built with Lovable leaking data. Source: [Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/lovable-apps-expose-sensitive-user-data-researchers-find)

> **FOUNDER:** "Built it in a weekend! No engineers!"
>
> **FRIEND:** "Why can I see everyone's passport?"
>
> **FOUNDER:** "That's… the social feature."
>
> **FRIEND:** "And everyone's private messages?"
>
> **FOUNDER:** "Community."

*On-screen fact:* "Researchers scanned 159,041 vibe-coded apps. Many were leaking data."

### 4. "When your $300 machine wants a subscription"
Indie dev builds their own app. Source: [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50470488)

> **MACHINE** (robot voice): "Please subscribe to continue stitching."
>
> **DEV** (cracks knuckles): "No."
>
> *(montage: typing, Rust logo, coffee)*
>
> **MACHINE:** "…Stitching."
>
> **DEV:** "Open source now, by the way." *(posts it: 740 upvotes)*

### 5. "Your tools in 2026"
Apps adding AI without asking. Source: HN, Nov 3: "Postman silently auto-installs AI plugins".

> **DEV** opens an app. **APP:** "I installed AI for you." **DEV:** "I didn't ask." **APP:** "And a second AI to supervise the first one." **DEV** opens the calculator. **CALCULATOR:** "Would you like to chat about this number?"

### 6. "RIP Sora"
Sora shut down. Source: [OpenAI help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001152-what-to-know-about-the-sora-discontinuation)

> A funeral, deadpan. **MOURNER:** "Sora made me a video of a dog riding a shark." **SECOND MOURNER:** "Sora made me eleven fingers." **PRIEST:** "The app left us in April. The API in September." Everyone looks at their phones: "…What do we use now?"

### 7. "The iPhone that folds (and the crease that cracks)"
The iPhone Duo. Source: [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/electronics-computers/cell-phones/apple-iphone-duo-review-a5520585020/)

> **FRIEND:** "Two thousand dollars?" **OWNER:** "It folds!" *(folds it very slowly, wincing)* **FRIEND:** "Why are you doing it like a bomb?" **OWNER:** "Because of the crease." **FRIEND:** "What happens at the crease?" **OWNER:** *(silent stare)*

### 8. "AI cracked a 370-year-old code in 44 minutes"
The Urquhart cipher. Source: [Vals AI](https://www.vals.ai/blogs/fable-solves-cyphral-distich)

> **HISTORIAN, 1653** (quill): "No one shall ever read my secret." **AI, 2026:** "Done. 44 minutes." **HISTORIAN:** "What does it say?" **AI:** "'O God uphold King Charles the Second'." **HISTORIAN:** "…It sounded cooler encrypted."

### 9. "Why your town voted against the data center"
Data-centre ballot votes. Source: [TechStartups](https://techstartups.com/2026/11/04/top-tech-news-roundup-for-today-november-4-2026-googles-15b-ai-bet-metas-e53b-bond-demand-canvas-restructuring-and-spacexs-5g-push/). **Verify the 38-jurisdiction number first.**

> **AI COMPANY:** "We'd like to build a small data center." **TOWN:** "How small?" **AI COMPANY:** "It'll use as much power as you." **TOWN:** "As me?" **AI COMPANY:** "As your whole town." **TOWN:** *(already voting no)*

### 10. "The code that landed on the Moon"
A tribute, not a joke. It works as a change of pace. Source: [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2026/margaret-hamilton-computing-pioneer-dies-1007)

> **CONTROL ROOM:** "Alarm 1202!" **ENGINEER:** "Abort?" **CALM VOICE:** "No. The software drops what doesn't matter and keeps the landing." *(beat)* **Text on screen:** "Written by a team led by Margaret Hamilton. She died this September, at 90."

**Write these jokes your own way and don't copy anyone's lines.** These ten are original, but the premise style is common. Make the characters yours: one recurring CEO, one recurring "AI".

## If you won't appear on camera

The best faceless options in the data are much weaker:
- **Tech-oddity text stories**, as Signal 404 does (recent median 84K). It's the 7-second template we already have, with topics like "The iPhone that bent in your pocket" or "Gold is in a RAM stick".
- **Animated explainers**, as ByteByteGo does (382K, but an established brand).

Expect about 5–10× fewer views than the on-camera skit format.
