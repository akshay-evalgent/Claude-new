## Titanic: the calm man with the flask

**File:** `titanic_baker.mp4` · **Cover:** `titanic_baker_cover.jpg`

**The detail:** at the stern, right next to Jack and Rose, a man in a white jacket calmly drinks from a flask.
He was real: **Charles Joughin, the Titanic's chief baker**. The video covers what he did that night:
- He sent his 13 bakers up to the lifeboats with bread.
- He refused his own lifeboat command and helped women and children into it.
- He had half a glass of liqueur in his cabin.
- He threw about 50 deck chairs into the sea for people to float on.
- He rode the stern down "like an elevator" and was the last survivor to leave the ship.
- He claimed two hours in the freezing water, and said he hardly felt the cold.
- He was saved at dawn.

Jack's own line from the film ("water that cold… hits you like a thousand knives…") sets up the contrast.

## Jurassic Park: the glass of water

**File:** `jurassic_water_cup.mp4` · **Cover:** `jurassic_water_cup_cover.jpg`

**The detail:** the most famous warning in the film, the rippling glass of water, was one of its hardest shots.
- **The idea** came from Spielberg, whose car mirror was shaking to Earth, Wind & Fire.
- **The mirror** was easy: a little vibrating motor.
- **The water** beat everyone, until Michael Lantieri (Special Dinosaur Effects) found the right note on a guitar one night.
- **On set,** guitar strings ran through the car, and a crew member lay on the floor plucking them on cue.
- **Bonus:** the giant T-rex wasn't waterproof. It soaked up the fake rain, got too heavy and shook on its own, so the crew dried it with blow dryers between takes.

Lantieri tells the key part himself, from Universal's own featurette. The reveal comes at the very end and loops back to the cup.

## Jaws: Quint's USS Indianapolis story is real

**File:** `jaws_indianapolis.mp4` (about 111 s) · **Cover:** `jaws_indianapolis_cover.jpg` (Quint, with "HIS STORY / WAS REAL")

**Layout:** the picture fills the middle 66%, with captions in the bottom band.

**The detail:** Quint's famous late-night speech describes a real 1945 disaster, and the true story goes further than the speech.

**Story beats:**
1. **The ship:** the USS Indianapolis secretly carried parts of the Hiroshima bomb.
2. **The sinking:** four days later two torpedoes sank it in 12 minutes. About 900 men went into the water.
3. **No rescue:** the Navy didn't notice the ship was missing. The men spent four days without food or water, and then the sharks came.
4. **A real survivor** (National Geographic): "you hear a blood-curdling scream… the shark had taken him under."
5. **Found by accident:** a patrol plane spotted them.
6. **Doubt beat:** Quint gets things wrong. It was about 900 men, not 1,100, and July 30, not June 29. Heat and thirst probably killed more men than the sharks.
7. **The captain:** Charles McVay was court-martialed and carried the blame.
8. **The kid:** an 11-year-old Jaws fan, Hunter Scott, made it his school project and took it to Congress. Congress cleared McVay in 2000.
9. **The wreck:** found in 2017, 18,000 ft down.
