# Monetisation: staying eligible, and earning beyond ads

## The short answer

YouTube's rule can't be "bypassed" safely. Human reviewers judge the **whole channel**: its main theme, its most-viewed videos, its newest videos and its watch time. If they decide a channel is template content, **every** video loses ads, not just the templated ones. Disguising a template (random colours, swapped fonts, reworded copies) is exactly what they look for, and it puts the channel at risk.

There's a better route. Make the format **genuinely original**, which also tends to perform better, and earn from more than ads.

## What the policy actually targets (clarified July 16, 2026)

It covers three kinds of "inauthentic content" that can't earn ads:
1. **Generic, repetitive or template-based content.** YouTube's example: videos that are easy to mass-produce with templates or AI and vary little from one to the next.
2. **Off-putting or distressing content** made to manipulate emotions for views. YouTube's example: an animal in distress that then gets rescued.
3. **AI personas of real people** discussing sensitive topics (finance, legal, health).

YouTube said its **reused-content policy** (clips, commentary, reactions, compilations) is **unchanged**. Clip-based videos are fine when they add **significant original commentary, education or creative value**. YouTube hasn't published how much is "too much".

## How our current formats measure up

| Format | Risk | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **7-second text loop** (`textstory.py`, the Fatal-second style) | **High** as the channel's main format | Same layout, same music, one reused clip and no voice in every video. That's the definition of type 1. |
| Emotional stories involving **animals in danger** (highway dog, Kabang) | **High** | Close to YouTube's own type-2 example. Drop them or reframe them. |
| **Narrated recap** (`render.py`, the Momentum style) | **Medium** | The script, narration and editing are original. The footage is reused, and the AI voice and identical structure add risk. |
| **Breakdown** (`breakdown.py`, new) | **Low** | Built to meet the reused-content test. See below. |

## The new format: "Breakdown" (15–40 seconds)

The sample is `edits_bd/01_hamilton_breakdown.json` (30 seconds, 7 segments). Every video must have all of these:

1. **An original, researched script**, written fresh for each story. Never reuse sentences between videos.
2. **4–8 segments from at least 2 sources.** The real moment, plus **evidence we made**: a screen recording of the headline, post, repo, chart or data. Our own recordings are original footage.
3. **Narration.** **Your own recorded voice** is the strongest authenticity signal: record it on your phone and I'll do the rest (`"voiceover": "your_voice.wav"`). The AI voice (`"tts"`) is only for drafting and timing the edit.
4. **Editorial additions:** highlight boxes on the key detail, push-ins, captions that change with each beat, and real audio at the key moment.
5. **"Why it matters":** your own takeaway or analysis at the end. This is what turns a clip into commentary or education.
6. **Sources on screen, with links in the description.** That's what journalism looks like.
7. **The story decides the structure.** Length, number of shots, pacing and whether real audio is used all change from video to video.

**How to use the old formats:**
- Keep the **7-second text loop as a small side series** (about 20% of uploads at most). Make sure your **most-viewed and newest videos are Breakdowns** before you apply for the Partner Program, because those are the videos reviewers look at.
- Retire the animal-in-danger stories.

## Revenue: don't rely on Shorts ads

Shorts ads pay very little. Tech and AI Shorts earn roughly $0.15–0.25 per 1,000 views (industry estimates). Use Shorts for **reach**, and earn from these:

| Stream | When | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Long-form videos** (6–12 min) | Start now, weekly | "This week in tech stories": 4–5 Breakdowns expanded with more context and your take. Long-form ads pay many times more than Shorts. Point each Short at the long video with YouTube's "related video" link. |
| **Sponsorships** | From about 10K subscribers | Dev tools, AI apps and SaaS products pay for one honest line or a dedicated Breakdown. Disclose it with YouTube's paid-promotion box. This is usually the biggest earner in tech. |
| **Affiliates** | Now | Tools, hosting and gadgets mentioned in stories. Shorts descriptions and comments **don't have clickable links**, so put one link page on your channel profile and in long-form descriptions. YouTube Shopping covers products. |
| **Newsletter** ("Repo of the week", "5 tech stories") | Now | You own this audience. It earns from sponsors and is safe from algorithm changes. Link it from your channel profile. |
| **Channel memberships and Super Thanks** | Once eligible | Early access, polls on the next story, a members' Discord. |
| **Digital products** | Later | Curated tool lists, templates, a course on the topic your audience asks about most. |

**Partner Program entry requirements** (as of 2023; check the current numbers in YouTube Studio):
- **Ads:** 1,000 subscribers, plus either 10M Shorts views in 90 days or 4,000 long-form watch hours in 12 months.
- **Fan funding** (memberships, Super Thanks) has a lower tier: 500 subscribers and 3M Shorts views.

## Weekly workflow

1. I research and fact-check 5–7 stories and write the scripts.
2. **You record the narration on your phone** (about 2 minutes total) and upload it to Drive.
3. I cut the Breakdowns (with my own screen recordings and evidence clips), the long-form compilation and the upload kits.
4. Post 1 Breakdown a day, 1 long-form video a week, and 1–2 text loops a week as a side series.

*Note: YouTube doesn't publish its thresholds, and nothing guarantees approval. This plan follows the written policy and keeps the channel defensible if it gets reviewed.*
