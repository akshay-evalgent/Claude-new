# What actually gets millions of views "around creators and virality" on Shorts

Research date: Nov 22, 2026. All numbers are public YouTube view counts (YouTube rounds large numbers).

## The short answer

- **Explaining creators doesn't work on Shorts.** In this sample, Shorts that explain creators (facts, stories, growth advice, comparisons) typically get between 59 and about 1,000 views. Our Spike Files videos belong to this family. Zero views is the normal result for the type, not a failure of the editing.
- **Showing them does.** The one format in this space that reliably gets millions, including on brand-new channels, is the silent nostalgia edit: **"Then vs Now" / "Evolution of …"**. One photo at a time, a big year on screen, music, no voice.
- **It is not guaranteed.** About a third of new channels in this format still only get 1–2K views per Short. Subject choice decides it, not editing quality.

For the 12 newer channels where I could see every upload, I looked at their **first 10 Shorts**:

| Outcome in the first 10 uploads | Channels |
|---|---|

Middle case: about 27K views per Short across the first ten.

### What it looks like

- **One photo at a time**, centred on a black background (the photo is usually square or 4:5).
- **A huge year label** on the photo: white for the old photo, **green for the "now" photo**.
- **No narration, no captions, no logo.** Music only.
- The same subject appears as "then", and a second or two later as "now". Then the next subject.
- **Length: 80–150 seconds** (median 123 s in a sample of 56). Far longer than our 37-second videos. Viewers stay because the next reveal is always one or two seconds away.
- I could not check the music from here (YouTube blocks playback on this machine). See "What I need from you".

### The "script"

There is no spoken script. The script is the order of the photos:

| Step | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Frame 1 | The most recognisable subject, old photo, year in white | Recognition is the hook. No text needed |
| 1–2 s later | The same subject now, year in green | First payoff inside 3 seconds |
| Repeat | 10–40 subjects, one "then → now" pair every 2–4 seconds | A new curiosity gap opens and closes constantly |
| Order | Strongest subject first, second strongest last | Keeps people to the end and feeds replays |
| End | No call to action. The video just ends, or loops | Nothing tells the viewer it's over |

**Tone:** quiet, emotional, nostalgic. The feeling is "time flies" or "I feel old". Comments write themselves.

### Which subjects carry the views

Inside the 24 dedicated channels:

| Subject | Shorts | Typical views | Share over 1M |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kid YouTubers (Nastya, Diana, Vlad & Niki…) | 108 | 1.3M | 57% |
| "Popular YouTubers" (often still kid creators) | 58 | 838K | 48% |
| Bands and singers | 52 | 492K | 36% |
| "Evolution of [one celebrity]" | 105 | 201K | 20% |
| People memes / internet legends | 304 | 142K | 21% |
| Animal memes | 274 | 96K | 10% |
| Movie / TV casts | 250 | 51K | 8% |
| Named adult creators (MrBeast, Speed, PewDiePie…) | 14 | 28K | 7% |

Two honest points:
- **The biggest numbers come from kid YouTubers, watched by children.** I recommend against building on that (see below).
- **Adult creators are the weakest subject so far** (28K typical, though single hits reach 8–16M, such as "evolution of MrBeast" at 11.9M). The format's strength is nostalgia, and nostalgia needs years of distance.

The weak channels in this format look identical to the strong ones. The difference is the subject.

## What not to copy

| What some of these channels do | Why I'd avoid it |
|---|---|
| Build on photos of child YouTubers and their families | It's content about real children, for an audience of children. YouTube treats that as "made for kids": comments off and limited ads |
| AI-generated "now" photos presented as real | It's fabricated. The honest version uses real, dated photos |
| AI-generated fake footage of real creators and athletes (the "When X did Y" leader) | It impersonates real people |
| The same title posted 20 times | That is the mass-produced pattern YouTube's monetisation rules target. Views do not guarantee ad revenue |
| "Now" photos of private people who became memes as children | Only use people who have gone public about it as adults |

## Recommendation

**Run the Then vs Now format on internet-famous subjects**, which keeps our idea (things and people that went viral) but uses the mechanic that is proven.

First ten videos, most evidence first:

| # | Video | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Legendary Internet Memes (Then vs Now) | People memes: 142K typical, 21% over 1M. Only adults who have spoken publicly about it |
| 2 | The Most Legendary Cat & Dog Memes (Then vs Now) | The subject behind Love Nostalgia's growth. Real photos only |
| 3 | Evolution of YouTube (2005–2026) | The site itself by year. No people; two Shorts on this got 2.3M and 2.5M |
| 4 | OG YouTubers (Then and Now) | The first generation of creators: enough years of distance for nostalgia |
| 5 | Evolution of MrBeast (2012–2026) | Proven single hits (11.9M, 16.6M for his logo) |
| 6 | Vine Stars (Then and Now) | Internet nostalgia, adult subjects |
| 7 | Viral Video Stars (Then and Now) | Same |
| 8 | Evolution of IShowSpeed (2016–2026) | Tests a current adult creator |
| 9 | Popular Streamers (Then and Now) | Tests a group of adult creators |
| 10 | First Video vs Now: The Biggest YouTubers | Our own twist: each card also shows subscribers then and now |

**Our own addition (untested):** a second small label under the year with the subscriber count that year. It ties in the growth idea and gives each video researched, original information, which also helps with monetisation rules. If it hurts watch time, we drop it.

**Posting rhythm:** the leaders post 4–6 a week. Ten videos gives a fair test.

**How to judge it:** after ten uploads, the typical new channel here is at about 27K per Short. If we are stuck near 1–2K, the subjects are wrong, not the format.

## Limits of this research

- It covers what YouTube search returned for about 300 topic queries and 100 view-sorted queries. Channels outside those results were not measured.
- Only the latest 120–150 Shorts per channel were pulled, so very old uploads of big channels are missing.
- I could not watch or hear any video. Visual notes come from the four still frames YouTube serves per Short.
- Title and subject comparisons are associations within this sample.
