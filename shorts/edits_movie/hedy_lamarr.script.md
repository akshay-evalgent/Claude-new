# Hedy Lamarr: Hollywood's most beautiful woman invented the idea in your Bluetooth (draft script)

**Tone:**
- Glamour, then betrayal, then vindication.
- No nudity: the Ecstasy film is shown only as a poster or headline.
- Never say "she invented WiFi" (see the doubt beat).

## Script (about 100 s; REAL = her own voice, from the 1990 Forbes phone tapes via PBS / Bombshell)

1. **Hook:** Hollywood called her the most beautiful woman in the world. But she was secretly an inventor, and the idea she patented is inside your Bluetooth.
2. Her name was Hedwig Kiesler, from Vienna. At eighteen, she starred in a film so daring it was banned in several countries.
3. That same year she married Fritz Mandl, an arms dealer who supplied Mussolini. He reportedly tried to buy up every copy of her film.
4. By her account, he kept her like a prisoner. She sat silently through his business dinners, listening to men talk about weapons.
5. In 1937, she escaped. Accounts differ, but by her own telling she slipped out disguised as her maid.
6. She talked her way onto the ocean liner carrying MGM boss Louis B. Mayer, and stepped off with a contract and a new name: Hedy Lamarr.
7. Hollywood sold her as "the most beautiful woman in the world."
8. But at night, she invented. When World War Two started, she wanted to stop enemies jamming the radio signals that guided torpedoes.
9. With composer George Antheil, she came up with an idea: make the signal hop between frequencies, synced like two player pianos.
10. In 1942, they got a patent: number 2,292,387, a "Secret Communication System."
11. The Navy shelved it, and suggested she'd help more by selling war bonds. So she did. By one account, she raised seven million dollars in a single night.
12. She never made a cent from her invention. Twenty years later, Navy ships were using frequency hopping.
13. **REAL (her voice, 1990):** "I guess they just take and forget about a person." (or the nearest line on tape)
14. **Doubt:** To be fair, she didn't invent WiFi, and she wasn't the first to imagine frequency hopping. But her patent showed how to make it work, decades before anyone used it.
15. In 1997, she finally got an award for it. Her reaction: "It's about time."
16. She died in 2000. In 2014, she entered the National Inventors Hall of Fame.
17. **Loop:** So next time your Bluetooth connects, remember: Hollywood's most beautiful woman thought of it first.
