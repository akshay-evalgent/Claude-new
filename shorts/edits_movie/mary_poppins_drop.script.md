# Mary Poppins: the crew member who dropped Julie Andrews (draft script)

**The ask:** a female lead, with a story centred on an ordinary crew member on set (the "spot boy" role).

**Detail picked with the movie-detail-short rubric:** 13 of 14.
- **That's real?** 1: a real accident behind a famous scene.
- **Visible:** 2: the flying scenes.
- **Unknown:** 2.
- **Stakes:** 2: she says she almost died.
- **Escalation:** 2. The fall → "Is she down yet?" → she swears → she only had the role because Hollywood rejected her → the Oscar → her thank-you to Jack Warner → on her next film a crew helicopter keeps knocking her down.
- **Proof:** 2: her own interviews, the Golden Globes speech, the Oscar footage.
- **Relatable:** 2: an underdog's revenge.

**Runner-up:** The Wizard of Oz. Margaret Hamilton was burned on set when the fire effect went wrong. Sources disagree on which scene it was and whether it was her or her stunt double, so it's weaker on proof.

## Script (about 100 s; real audio marked FILM)

1. **Hook:** If you pay attention to Mary Poppins, you'll notice she always floats down so gently. In real life, Julie Andrews crashed.
2. To fly, she hung from thin wires in a painful harness, high above the set.
3. Up in the back, a crew member held her wire, and let her down gently.
4. On one of the last days of filming, she was exhausted, and she felt the wire slip.
5. Then she dropped to the stage, like a ton of bricks.
6. There was a long silence. And then, from the back, the crew member called out: "Is she down yet?"
7. Mary Poppins, the most proper nanny in the world, answered with a stream of swear words.
8. **FILM:** "Practically perfect in every way."
9. But the craziest part is how she got this role at all.
10. On Broadway, she was the star of My Fair Lady. But for the movie, studio boss Jack Warner said she wasn't famous enough, and gave her role to Audrey Hepburn.
11. So Walt Disney made her Mary Poppins instead.
12. That year, Julie Andrews won the Oscar for Best Actress. Audrey Hepburn wasn't even nominated.
13. And at the Golden Globes, she thanked one man in particular.
14. **FILM (Golden Globes 1965):** "…the man who made all this possible… Mr. Jack Warner."
15. And on her very next film, The Sound of Music, a camera helicopter kept knocking her flat into the grass, every single take.
16. **FILM (Julie Andrews herself, telling the helicopter story).**
17. **Doubt:** The fall is Julie's own story, told decades later. The crew never told theirs.
18. **Loop:** So next time Mary Poppins floats down so gently, remember: she landed like a ton of bricks.
