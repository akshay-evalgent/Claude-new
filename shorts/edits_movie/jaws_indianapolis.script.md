# Jaws: Quint's USS Indianapolis story is real (draft script)

**Detail picked with the movie-detail-short rubric:** 13 of 14.
- **That's real?** 2: the ship, the sinking and the sharks.
- **Visible:** 2: the famous speech.
- **Unknown:** 1. Older fans know it; most younger viewers don't.
- **Stakes:** 2: about 900 men in the water.
- **Escalation:** 2. The bomb parts → torpedoed, sinks in 12 minutes → nobody notices → 4 days and sharks → found by accident → captain court-martialed → a kid who saw *Jaws* clears his name → the wreck found 18,000 ft down.
- **Proof:** 2: Navy photos, survivor interviews and wreck footage.
- **Relatable:** 2: an 11-year-old's school project changed history.

**Runner-up:** in *Catch Me If You Can*, the real Frank Abagnale plays the French officer who arrests Leo, but a 2020 book claims much of his story was made up.

## Script (about 100 s; film lines marked FILM)

1. **Hook:** If you pay attention to Jaws, you'll notice the scariest story in the movie isn't about the shark. And it really happened.
2. Late at night, Quint tells Brody and Hooper why he hates sharks.
3. **FILM:** Quint: "Japanese submarine slammed two torpedoes into our side, Chief."
4. The ship he's talking about, the USS Indianapolis, was real.
5. In July 1945, it secretly delivered parts of the atomic bomb that would be dropped on Hiroshima.
6. Four days later, a Japanese submarine hit it with two torpedoes. It sank in twelve minutes.
7. Of almost twelve hundred men on board, around nine hundred made it into the water.
8. And nobody came. For days, the Navy didn't even notice the ship was missing.
9. They floated in the open ocean, with no food or water. And then came the sharks.
10. **FILM:** Quint: "Eleven hundred men went into the water. Three hundred and sixteen men come out. The sharks took the rest."
11. **Doubt:** Quint gets some details wrong. He says June 29th; it was really July 30th. And historians think heat, thirst and exhaustion killed even more men than the sharks.
12. But the sharks were real. Survivors said they attacked day and night. It's often called the worst shark attack in history.
13. After four days, a patrol plane spotted them completely by accident. Only about three hundred sixteen men survived.
14. Then the Navy court-martialed the captain, Charles McVay. He carried the blame for the rest of his life.
15. Decades later, an eleven-year-old Jaws fan named Hunter Scott heard this speech and wanted to know if it was real.
16. He made it his school history project, tracked down survivors, and took it all the way to Congress.
17. In 2000, Congress cleared Captain McVay's name. A movie scene helped fix history.
18. And in 2017, the ship was finally found, eighteen thousand feet down.
19. **Loop:** So next time you watch Jaws, listen closely to Quint's story. It really happened.
