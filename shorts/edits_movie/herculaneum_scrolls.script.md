# A 21-year-old read a 2,000-year-old burnt scroll with AI (true story, draft script)

**Tone:** wonder and underdog.
- **Who reads what:** say "AI found the ink; scholars read the Greek". Don't say "AI read it" outright.
- **Hopes:** lost classics are stated as a hope, not a fact.

## Script (about 100 s; REAL marks on-camera voices, used if found on tape)

1. **Hook:** These lumps of charcoal are books, two thousand years old. And a twenty-one-year-old student just read one... with AI.
2. In 79 AD, Vesuvius buried a Roman villa, thought to belong to Julius Caesar's father-in-law. Inside was a library. The heat turned its scrolls into charcoal.
3. They were found in the 1750s. But touch one, and it crumbles. Early attempts to open them destroyed many: some were sliced in half, others tore apart on an unrolling machine.
4. For twenty years, a Kentucky professor, Brent Seales, tried to read them without opening them: scan them with X-rays, then unroll them on a computer.
5. But the ink is made of carbon, just like the burnt page. On an X-ray, it's invisible.
6. So in 2023, a prize of over a million dollars was offered to anyone who could read them.
7. A twenty-one-year-old computer science student from Nebraska, Luke Farritor, trained an AI to spot the faintest texture of ink.
8. One night, walking home from a party, he checked his phone, and saw three Greek letters. In his words, he "completely freaked out."
9. Then a whole word: porphyras. "Purple." The first word read from inside one of these scrolls in two thousand years.
10. **REAL (Luke):** his line about the moment, if on tape.
11. In Berlin, an Egyptian student, Youssef Nader, found the same word on his own.
12. Together with Julian Schilliger, they revealed more than two thousand letters, and won seven hundred thousand dollars.
13. And what was the ancient text about? Pleasure. Food, music... and the taste of capers.
14. **Doubt:** Some scholars worried: was the AI just seeing patterns that weren't there?
15. Then, in June 2026, sharper scans showed the ink directly. It matched the AI's reading letter for letter. That same month, for the first time, a whole scroll was read from beginning to end.
16. **REAL (Seales or NBC report):** a line about reading them all.
17. There are still hundreds of unopened scrolls. Scholars hope some hold lost classics nobody has read in two thousand years.
18. **Loop:** So these lumps of charcoal... are a library. And we're finally reading it.
