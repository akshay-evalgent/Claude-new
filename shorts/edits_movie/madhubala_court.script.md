# Madhubala: he said he loved her, in a courtroom, while testifying against her (draft script)

**Tone:** respectful and romantic-tragic.
- **Hook fix:** not "never spoke again". They kept filming together, and he visited her once more.
- **The court quote:** "reportedly". No transcript exists.
- **The break-up:** both versions are given with attribution (her sister's, and his own).
- **Left out:** the actress Mumtaz's claim, Kishore Kumar's marriage gossip, and the London suicide claims.
- **Health:** kept brief ("a hole in her heart").
- **Footage:** the black-and-white originals of Tarana (1951), Naya Daur (1957) and Mughal-e-Azam (1960) are public domain in India. The 2004 colour version isn't, so use it sparingly.

## Script (about 100 s)

1. **Hook:** He was India's biggest star, and he loved her. Then he stood up in a courtroom, testified against her family... and told the court he loved her.
2. She was born Mumtaz Jehan, on Valentine's Day, 1933. By nine, she was acting in films to support her family.
3. The world came to know her as Madhubala. An American magazine called her "the biggest star in the world, and she's not in Beverly Hills."
4. In 1951, on the film Tarana, she fell in love with her co-star, Dilip Kumar. For most of the fifties, they were Bollywood's secret couple, and they planned to marry.
5. Then came Naya Daur. When her father refused to let her travel for an outdoor shoot, the producer replaced her and took the family to court.
6. Dilip Kumar testified... for the producer, against her family.
7. And in the witness box, he reportedly said he loved her, and always would.
8. It ended them. Her sister later said he asked Madhubala to leave her father. Dilip Kumar wrote that he blamed her father's attempt to turn the marriage into a business deal.
9. But they still had one film left to finish together: Mughal-e-Azam.
10. And here's the part you can see on screen. The most romantic scene in Indian cinema, the feather between their lips, was shot when they had completely stopped even greeting each other. Those are his own words.
11. **FILM (Mughal-e-Azam, the feather scene).**
12. Her biographer says that for her prison scenes, she wore real iron chains.
13. What almost nobody knew: Madhubala had a hole in her heart, kept secret for years. In London, doctors told her she had about two years.
14. He married in 1966. He wrote that he saw her one last time: frail, her smile "such an effort."
15. She died in 1969, nine days after her thirty-sixth birthday.
16. Her sister said: "She could never forget him."
17. **Loop:** So next time you watch that feather scene, remember: they weren't even speaking.
