# The Cecil Hotel: the guests who complained about the water (draft script)

**Format:** a true-story Short using the same engine and layout as the movie-detail Shorts (picture in the middle 66%, captions in the bottom band). Real news footage carries the story, with real audio moments where they help.

**Tone rules:** this story involves a real young woman who died.
- **No gore and no ghost hype.** No "cursed hotel" framing as fact.
- **The coroner's finding is stated plainly.**
- **The internet-sleuth angle is told as the cautionary part** of the story, not cheered on.
- **The elevator footage is used only briefly,** as news coverage, and never mocked or called "creepy".
- **The ending is the hotel's real fate:** homes for people who had none.

## Script (about 85 s)

1. **Hook:** In February 2013, guests at a Los Angeles hotel started complaining. The water pressure was low, and the water tasted strange.
2. So a maintenance worker went up to the roof, and found the hatch of the main water tank open.
3. Inside was a missing guest: Elisa Lam, a twenty-one-year-old student from Vancouver.
4. She had vanished almost three weeks earlier.
5. To find her, police had released her last video, from the hotel's elevator. It spread around the world.
6. And people noticed where she had stayed: the Cecil Hotel.
7. It opened in 1924 as a grand hotel. Over the decades, it became one of the most notorious buildings in Los Angeles.
8. The serial killer known as the Night Stalker is said to have lived there in 1985, for fourteen dollars a night.
9. In 1991, an Austrian serial killer, working as a journalist, checked in too.
10. So when Elisa was found, the internet decided it had to be something dark. Ghosts. Cover-ups. Murder.
11. Online sleuths even named a suspect: a heavy-metal musician who had stayed there a year earlier.
12. He was in Mexico, recording an album, and he could prove it. Years later, he still gets hate messages.
13. The real answer was much sadder. Elisa had bipolar disorder, and the coroner ruled her death an accidental drowning.
14. In 2021, the Cecil reopened. Not as a hotel, but as six hundred affordable homes for people with nowhere else to go.
15. **Loop:** So the hotel the internet called haunted, now gives people a home. Remember Elisa kindly.
