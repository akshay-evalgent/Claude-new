# Tech Shorts: research and content plan (Nov 2026)

This plan uses the same text-story format as before: a 7-second loop, a 55–75 word story on screen, and one real moment. It points that format at **products, tools, repos, companies, startups and tech people**. The goal is Shorts people **save** (useful), **send** (relatable), **comment on** (opinion) and **follow** (a series).

**Takeaways:**
1. **Specific beats generic.** Named things (GTA5, Discord, Claude, Gmail) beat "tech tips".
2. **Curiosity and surprise drive views:** "why does X…", "the X nobody knows", superlatives.
3. **Stories about a person who did something** beat stories about a company.
4. **Useful stuff gets saved:** repos, websites, hacks. These have a lower ceiling but a steady floor, and saves feed recommendations.
5. **Motivational founder quotes over b-roll don't work.** Avoid them.

## 2. Why people save, send, comment and follow tech Shorts

| Action | What triggers it | How we use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Save** | "I'll need this later": a tool, repo, website or trick | Name the thing in the story and highlight it in yellow. End with **"Save this for later"**. Show the real tool on screen so people can screenshot it. |
| **Send** | "My friend needs to see this", or an in-joke | Call out a specific person: "Send this to the friend who still pays for X", "…to your developer friend", "…to someone who hates subscriptions". |
| **Comment** | An opinion, a side to take, or "I know more" | One binary question in the pinned comment: "Claude Code or Cursor?", "Would you trust it?", "Which subscription would you kill first?". The tech audience comments far more than the emotional one. |
| **Follow** | Expecting more of the same | Run **named series** with a number in the text, e.g. "Repo of the day #12". The fixed template already looks like a series. |
| **Rewatch** | 18–22 seconds of reading over a 7-second loop | Same as the emotional channel. Replays count as views (since Mar 2025). |

## 3. Six series (content pillars)

1. **Built different** (indie and underdog builders): one person beats a company, a subscription or the odds. Examples: ZigZag, the bird frame, OpenClaw, Wordle, Flappy Bird. *Clip:* the creator's own demo or our screen recording.
2. **Repo of the day** (saves): one trending repo explained as a mini story ("Someone got tired of X, so…"). Pick from the GitHub monthly trending list and the HN front page. *Clip:* our own recording of the repo or demo page.
3. **The code that…** (legends and history): Margaret Hamilton, the xz backdoor, the WannaCry kill switch, left-pad, Linus's "just a hobby", JavaScript written in 10 days. *Clip:* NASA footage (public domain), or a recording of **the original post or email** as evidence.
4. **Before they were giants** (company origins): Nvidia 30 days from bankruptcy (rescued by Sega's $5M), Brian Acton turned down by Facebook then paid $19B, Airbnb's cereal boxes, Slack born from a failed game, Instagram's 13 employees and $1B. *Clip:* archive keynote or interview clips (you download them).
5. **This week in tech** (news-jacking, posted within 24–72h): Starship's catch, OpenAI's demo fail, the iPhone Duo crease, Binance's LinkedIn hack. *Clip:* official livestream or news clip (you download it). These are fast and search-driven.
6. **Why does tech…** (curiosity, Fireship style): why 0.1 + 0.2 = 0.30000000000000004, why the Zen of Python is hidden in `import this`, why the save icon is a floppy disk. *Clip:* our own recording of the browser console or terminal, the most original footage there is.

**Where clips come from (safest first):**
1. Our own screen recordings of repos, sites, terminals, consoles, original posts and star charts. I can make these myself with zero rights issues.
2. NASA and US government footage, which is public domain.
3. Demo videos from open-source projects (license plus credit).
4. Official company or news clips you download. These carry a Content ID risk, the same as the emotional channel.

## 4. Title rules for tech (different from the emotional channel)

- **Put the searchable name in the title:** Claude Code, Cursor, OpenAI, Nvidia, GitHub, Linux, iPhone.
- **One ALL-CAPS power word is fine here:** Fireship, Lewis and Mrwhosetheboss all use them ("WORST", "BRIGHTEST", "OLDEST"). The emotional niche penalised caps, but the tech niche doesn't.
- **Keep it short:** under 40 characters of core text, with one or two emojis at the end.
- **Curiosity formats that keep winning:**
  - "Why does X…?"
  - "The X nobody talks about"
  - "This random programmer…"
  - "$X vs $Y"
  - "The OLDEST/WORST/FASTEST…"
- **Never claim something you can't source.** Tech commenters fact-check fast, and one wrong number kills trust.

## 5. Risks to manage

- **YouTube's July 2026 monetisation clarification:** "generic, repetitive or template-based" content can't earn ad revenue. To stay safe:
  - Every story should be original, researched and sourced.
  - Use varied real footage, with real audio where it adds something.
  - Don't reuse wording from one Short to the next.
- **Allegations:** for the Nvidia spy story and the Character.AI lawsuits, state only confirmed facts and attribute them ("the NYT reported…"). Never present an allegation as fact.
- **Ad revenue is low:** tech and AI Shorts earn about $0.15–0.25 per 1,000 views. The value is growth and audience; the money comes later through sponsors and affiliates (tools and repos are natural fits).
