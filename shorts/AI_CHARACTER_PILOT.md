# AI character from Google Flow: verdict and pilot plan

## Verdict: it can work, with one big condition

**Evidence it can work.** Channels built around a **recurring AI-made character** do get consistent views (median views per Short, last 60 Shorts, checked Nov 16):
- Theo and Tia: 275K
- Oops IRL: 120K
- Finn and Poppy: 110K
- Mio Comedy Factory: 68K

**The catch.** Those are almost all **visual slapstick with no dialogue** (foam, pools, sight gags). AI characters with dialogue did much worse (Grubbo: 2K, Zukko Daiko: 3K). I found **no AI-character channel doing dialogue-based tech comedy** with good numbers. That means:
- There's no proof yet that it works.
- Nobody else owns that space yet.

**The condition: don't fake a human.** Don't use a photoreal "person" that viewers think is a real tech worker:
- Tech viewers spot AI faces fast, and "AI slop" comments kill reach.
- YouTube requires the "altered or synthetic content" label for realistic AI people.
- A fake human playing a real employee feels deceptive.

## The fix: make the AI-ness the joke

**Use a stylized, openly-AI character:** "**Botley, the AI intern**" (pick your own name). It's a small office robot with an LED face, working at a tech company and reacting to tech news. Advantages:
1. **The premise is self-aware.** An AI living through AI news ("Botley learns tokens cost money") turns the AI look into the joke.
2. **No lip-sync problem.** An LED-screen face doesn't need lips, so I can add **one consistent voice** with our own TTS. AI video tools tend to change a voice between generations, and a robot avoids that problem.
3. **Consistency.** Stylized characters stay recognisable across generations far better than photoreal humans.
4. **Policy.** It's a fictional, obviously non-human character with original scripts and real facts, so it's not the "AI persona of a real person" YouTube bans. Still tick **"Altered or synthetic content: Yes"** to be safe.

**The rest of each video** (which I make): real news screenshots and screen recordings of the actual headline or post, the premise box at the top, word-by-word captions and sound effects. The Flow clips cover the character's beats. The real evidence is what makes it original and credible.

## How to generate in Flow

1. **Make one reference image of Botley first** (in Flow, or any image tool) and **reuse it as the reference for every clip**, with the same wording each time:

   > *"A small friendly office robot, white rounded plastic body, a black rectangular LED screen face showing simple cyan pixel eyes and mouth, a blue lanyard with an ID badge reading 'INTERN', sitting at a modern open-plan tech office desk with a laptop, warm daylight, shallow depth of field, Pixar-style 3D, vertical 9:16"*

2. **Keep each shot short and simple:** one action and one emotion per clip, with a fixed camera. Ask for **no speech** (I add the voice), or keep the generated audio to room tone only.
3. **For every clip, say:**
   - **framing:** "medium close-up, eye level, static camera"
   - **emotion on the LED face:** "eyes widen to two big circles", "pixel eyes turn into spirals", "LED shows a sweat drop"
   - **action:** "slowly closes the laptop", "slides off the chair", "puts on sunglasses"
4. Generate 2–3 versions of each shot and pick the best. Upload the picks to a Drive folder called **`Botley raw`**, named `skit1_shot1.mp4` and so on.

- If they win: one Botley skit a day, with me writing the scripts and prompts from the week's news.
