# Viral comedy research, 14 November 2026

What reached a million views within 7 days of upload, what those videos share, how
Samay Raina's comedy works, and what this channel should copy.

## Method and limits

- `python3 chatvid/tools/trending.py` asks YouTube search for the most-viewed videos
  **uploaded in the last 7 days** for comedy search terms. For this report: 44 terms,
  1,692 unique videos, 1,492 after removing films, live streams and kids' content.
- Views are as of 14 Nov 2026, so every number is "views within 7 days or less".
- Search covers what YouTube returns for those terms, not every upload. Shorts are
  under-counted (search mostly returns long videos). TikTok and Instagram are not covered.
- Captions could not be read from this environment, so the Samay Raina section rests on
  his channel's numbers, published reviews and reporting, not a line-by-line transcript.

## Samay Raina: the numbers

- **India's Got Latent season 2** (his channel, 50-60 min episodes): 47M to 85M views per
  episode. The premiere took 45M in 4 days (SpotboyE).
- **Still Alive** (81-minute special): 22M in 24 hours, 48M in a week, 81M now.
- **Shorts:** 2-45M each, with two-to-four-word titles ("Zomato Roast" 12M, "Save chicken" 8.1M,
  "Bisleri piyo bhai" 8.5M, "Core memory" 45M).
- His older solo videos (vlogs, chess, parodies) sit at 1-9M. The panel show is what
  multiplied him by ten.

## Samay Raina: how the comedy works

1. **The game is self-awareness, not talent.** Contestants rate themselves before performing;
   they win only if the judges' average matches. Every act therefore has a prediction, a
   performance and a reveal: three beats and a guaranteed payoff every few minutes.
   (Format borrowed from Kill Tony and the Got Talent shows.)
2. **The host is the lowest-status person on stage.** He makes himself the punchline first
   (height, looks, his degree, his own scandal) and laughs hardest when a contestant roasts him.
   That buys the licence to roast everyone else. One contestant won an episode by roasting him.
3. **Find one hook and keep pulling it.** A reviewer: he "finds a hook and keeps tugging at it
   from different angles". One odd detail about a contestant becomes five jokes and a callback.
4. **Edgy on the surface, safe underneath.** Critics call it "foaming rapids", not a "freefall":
   the dark line is said casually, then undercut at once by a giggle or by turning it on himself.
5. **Guests bring their own audiences, and their names are the title.** Every episode title is
   "INDIA'S GOT LATENT S2 EP8 ft. <four names>". The panel mixes a roaster, a kind one and a
   confused celebrity.
6. **The trouble becomes the material.** The comeback special is named after his own
   cancellation and tells the story from inside. Sponsors get roasted inside their own ad reads.
7. **Built to be clipped.** Each contestant is a self-contained 4-8 minute story; an hour is cut
   from a five-hour taping; the cut footage is sold to members.
8. **A brotherhood tone.** Hinglish, "bhai", in-jokes with a recurring friend group
   (Tanmay Bhat, Balraj). Viewers feel inside the group.

## What to copy

1. A **numbered series with a fixed game**, not one-off videos.
2. **10-25 minutes** per episode, built from 4-6 self-contained segments that also work as clips.
3. **Each episode hangs on that week's news.** Scan with `tools/trending.py` on Monday.
4. **A low-status host who roasts himself first**, then everyone else.
5. **Famous names in the title** ("ft. ChatGPT, Siri, Grok").
6. **Animation is enough**: 18% of the hits are cartoons, some from channels with 4,000 subscribers.
7. A **trailer** for every episode and 3-5 **Shorts** with two-to-four-word titles.

Copy the mechanics, which nobody owns. Do not copy names, logos, clips or specific jokes.
