This is what happens if your spacesuit tears during a spacewalk.
The air inside your suit rushes out. Within seconds, there's almost none left.
Your instinct is to hold your breath. Don't. The air in your lungs would expand, and tear them apart. So you breathe out.
Now, with no pressure around you, the water in your body starts to boil. Not because it's hot, but because nothing is holding it in.
Your skin swells up, and the saliva on your tongue begins to bubble.
After about fifteen seconds, your brain runs out of oxygen. Your vision fades, and you black out.
But you're not dead yet. If your crewmate gets you back inside within about ninety seconds, you'll probably survive.
We know this, because it actually happened. In 1966, a NASA engineer's suit leaked inside a vacuum chamber.
His last memory before passing out, was the saliva on his tongue starting to boil.
He woke up as the chamber filled with air again. And he was completely fine.
