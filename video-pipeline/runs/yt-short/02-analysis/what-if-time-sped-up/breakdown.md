# Breakdown: what-if-time-sped-up

Reference: "What If Time Sped Up and Couldn't Stop?" (Minute Zero), `01-sources/refs/what-if-time-sped-up/video.mp4`.
All times are seconds into the file. "Stand time" is the reference's own right-hand counter; it starts at 0 when the street appears, so stand time = file time minus about 6.3 s.

How the figures were got:

- Tool figures are from `measure.json`, `palette.json` and `transcript.json` in this folder.
- On-screen line times are from my own pixel detector over the caption band at 10 frames a second, checked by eye against contact sheets. The detector trims fades: a line is visible from about 0.3 s before its listed start to about 0.3 s after its listed end. Times in the tables below already include that margin.
- Word counts are mine, read off the sheets.
- The on-screen lines are not transcribed here. Short phrases are quoted where the wording itself is the point; everything else is described by count and timing.

## 1. What it is

- A 537.2 s (8 min 57 s) horizontal 1920x1080, 30 fps "what if" science scenario with no voice: one first-person 3D scene, two counters in the top corners, one short italic line at a time at the bottom, a low drone underneath.
- Subject: you stand still on a street at 5:42 PM while time outside runs faster and faster, from walkers moving too fast (11 s) to the last black hole evaporating (505 s).
- The one idea: the world ends around you while your own watch shows under nine minutes, and then you are back on the street at 5:42 with the clock starting to speed up again.

## 2. Script

There are no spoken words anywhere (see section 8), so the "spoken" column is dropped. Text on screen is of three kinds: the two counter blocks (always on), the story line (bottom, 139 lines), and two one-off texts (the cold-open line and the title).

### 2d. Where the layers are out of step

- No voice, so there is nothing for the lines to drift against. The checks are line against counter and line against picture.
- Line against counter: "fifty-four seconds" shows with the counter at 53-55 s (58-61 s), "six and a half minutes" at 6 min 26 s (392 s), "eight minutes" at 8 min 05 s (484 s): all agree. "Your watch says four minutes." shows with the counter at 4 min 09 s (255 s): 9 s out.
- Line against picture: the picture leads. Walkers speed up at 10.6 s, line at 11.1 s; night falls at 25.0 s, line full at 25.4 s; ice arrives at 128.9 s after the line about something white coming (127.5 s). I found no line that names something not on screen within a second or two, except lines that are about the viewer.
- The cold-open line says "five minutes" over a counter reading 5 min 06-12 s: rounded, not wrong.
- Counter against counter: the "NEXT" countdown names each event before its line. "THE FIRST SNOW" is announced from 31 s; the snow and its line arrive at 51-52 s.

### 2e. How the lines are written

- 139 story lines, 928 words: mean 6.7 words, shortest 1, longest 12. 12 lines are 3 words or fewer; 18 are 10 words or more.
- One line on screen at a time, always a single row, never wrapped.
- Present tense throughout. One or two short sentences; fragments are common (a bare noun, a noun plus a comma clause).
- About 35 of 139 lines (a quarter) address the viewer as "you" or "your", or tell them where to look.
- Numbers in the lines are spelled out as words; the digits live in the counters. The only digits in a story line are the clock time in the last line (534 s).
- A recurring move is to convert an outside event into the viewer's seconds: a night "in six seconds" (28 s), towers falling in six seconds (113 s), a canyon cut in five seconds (164 s), circling the galaxy every four seconds (267 s).
- A recurring check-in is the watch: lines at 14.3, 58.2, 76.6, 254.5, 391.4, 483.5 and 518.2 s bring it back to the viewer's own elapsed time, five of them with the arm in shot.
- Three lines mark a guess as a guess: 109.5 s (nobody knows how our story ends), 291 s ("It might miss. Say it does not."), 445 s (nobody knows if atoms last forever). The fuller split between real and guessed is in the video description, not on screen.

## 3. Hook (first 3 seconds)

- **First picture:** black at 0.00 s, fading up over 0.13-0.4 s to molten yellow-orange ground under a red sky with sparks streaking, both counters already running (`hook.jpg`, `still_1.5.jpg`).
- **First words:** no spoken words. The first on-screen line is faint at 0.5 s and full at 0.75 s: 8 words, upright bold serif, bottom centre, telling you that you have been standing here for five minutes. The counters put about 29 more words and figures on screen (the year in billions, 5 min 06 s, the rate, the comparison).
- **What stops someone:** the mismatch between two numbers on one screen. The left reads a year in the billions climbing by 0.03 billion every quarter second; the right reads five minutes. The line says the small number in plain words while the picture shows the consequence of the big one. Specific (a year, a duration), unusual (ground that is molten), and at stake is "you": the label and the line are both second person.
- **Question opened:** how did five minutes of standing still put you in the year 6.8 billion on melting ground? It is answered when the film reaches those same counter values at 312-318 s (58% of the run). The title at 6.8 s opens the larger one: where does it end?
- **Sound off:** nothing is lost. There is no voice and no sound cue the hook depends on.
- **Tool check:** `measure.json` puts the first cut at 0.233 s. That is the fade up from black, not a cut. The first real change of scene is the dip to black at 5.5-6.5 s.

**Pace of words.**

- Spoken words a minute: 0. On-screen words a minute: 105 (944 words in 537.2 s, counting the cold-open line and the title; my count).
- A new story line starts every 3.79 s on average (median 3.80, shortest 2.6, longest 9.2). That is 14.7 to 16.5 lines a minute in every part of the film.
- Each line is on screen about 2.6 s including fades of 0.2-0.3 s each way, whatever its length, with about 1.2 s of empty caption space before the next. A story line is on screen for 65% of the run.
- The cadence is slightly quicker in the first minute (2.8 to 4.2 s between starts across 11-48 s, mostly 3.0-3.5) and settles to 3.6-4.0 s from 95 s on. Only five gaps between starts exceed 4.5 s: 101, 271, 353, 484 and 498 s.

**Pace of picture.**

- Tool: 70 shots, mean 7.67 s, median 2.07 s, longest 108 s, 1.28 cuts per 10 s.
- By eye: no edit cuts at all. One continuous first-person scene, with one dip through black (5.5-6.5 s) and one fade up from black (528.9-531.0 s). Every other "cut" the tool logs is a change inside the scene: day flipping to night (25.0-41.9 s), snow flipping to green (55.8-75.4 s), ice arriving in one frame (128.9-150.2 s), the ground vanishing (321.4 s), the flash (505.5 s), a walker or the watch arm crossing the lens (11.7, 14.3, 518.2, 536.7 s). I checked 24 of them frame against frame (before and after).
- The tool's cut rate therefore measures how often the world flips, and it is front-loaded: 3.17 per 10 s in the first 60 s (19 cuts), 3.70 per 10 s over the first 92 s, against 1.28 for the whole run and 1.00 over the last 40 s.

**Longest stretch where nothing changes.**

- Tool: longest frozen stretch 0.77 s (461.1-461.87 s); frozen share 0.3%. The picture is never truly still: the counters tick every frame.
- By eye: the least-changing stretch is 437-496 s, a single ring on black with a new line every 3.8 s. Tool motion is under 0.3 for nearly every second from 419 to 496 s (whole-run mean 1.59).
- The slowest part is therefore at 81-92% of the run, directly before the payoff. Motion by chapter falls from 2.0-4.9 in the first two minutes to 0.08-0.13 across 444-505 s.

**How it ends.** Payoff picture at 505.5 s, payoff line at 506.9 s (94% of the run). Then 15 s of aftermath, a turn at 521.9 s, the opening shot back by 531 s, the last line off at 537.0 s and the file over 0.2 s later. From payoff to end: 31.7 s (5.9% of the run). End hold: 0.0 s.

## 10. What is weak

1. **The first frame is black and there are two runs of empty picture.** 0.0-0.13 s and 5.8-6.13 s are flat black (tool `flat_runs`). The first line is not fully readable until 0.75 s.
2. **The story starts late.** Cold open 5.5 s, a one-second dip to black, a title held 3.4 s: the first story line arrives at 11.1 s. In a nine-minute film that is 2% of the run. Copied into a 60 s film it would be 18%.
3. **The title is laid over the busiest part of the picture.** At 6.8-10.2 s the lamp post and a tree run straight through the title's words (`still_8.jpg`).
4. **The small counter text is very small.** The rate line, the NEXT label and the "OUT THERE" comparison have a cap height of about 12 px at 1080p (1.1% of frame height) at reduced opacity. They carry some of the film's best ideas, and only one of the top 100 comments quotes any of them.
5. **White lines on white ground.** Over snow, ice and the white peaks (52-55, 62, 72, 131-150, 194-200 s) the story line depends on a faint grey halo. My caption detector, which finds white text by contrast, nearly lost several of these.
6. **Every line gets the same time, whatever its length.** A 12-word line is on screen about 2.6 s, the same as a one-word line: over 4 words a second to read, with the counters competing for the eye.
7. **The cadence is a metronome.** A new line every 3.6-4.0 s from 95 s to 483 s, with five exceptions. The one long silence (7.0 s before the payoff) shows what varying it does; it is used once.
8. **Two dark, slow minutes just before the payoff.** From 383 s to 505 s the frame is near-black, and across 437-496 s it is a single ring with tool motion under 0.3. The slowest stretch falls at 81-92% of the run. There is no retention data to say what it cost.
9. **One caption disagrees with the counter beside it.** "Your watch says four minutes." at 255 s, counter at 4 min 09 s.
10. **No close on screen.** No credit, no end card, and nothing on screen pointing to what is real and what is guessed. (The missing end hold is not the fault: the loop restarting and the file cutting while the clock speeds up is the ending's design, and a commenter, rank 34, noticed it.)
