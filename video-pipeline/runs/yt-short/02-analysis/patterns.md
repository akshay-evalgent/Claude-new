# Patterns: how this kind of video works

**Evidence.** One reference, `what-if-time-sped-up` (537.2 s, horizontal, no voice). Every pattern below is "seen in the one reference": the labels "seen in every reference" and "seen in some" mean nothing here and are not used. All timestamps are seconds into that reference; the detail is in `what-if-time-sped-up/breakdown.md`.

**Tied to results: no result evidence.** `sources.json` has public numbers for this one video only (2,166,790 views, 23,825 likes, about 2,392 comments, about 24,000 subscribers, as of 2026-11-25). One video cannot show which of its features earned them. For context only, not as a pattern: `info.json` lists 15 videos in the same format on the same channel, with views from 8,300 to 2.4 million (median 50,000), so the format alone does not decide the result.

**The brief wins on format and length.** Our film is vertical 1080x1920 and at most 60 s. Where a pattern has to change to fit, a block headed "At 60 seconds" gives the arithmetic. Those blocks are worked out from the reference's figures and the brief; the reference does not show them.

**Not to be reused.** The brief forbids reusing the reference's footage, artwork, music or wording. These patterns describe how its parts work, so that new ones can be made. Do not restage its street, its events or its lines.

## Hook

1. **Open on the far end of the scenario, not the start.** The first picture is the most extreme one the film will reach by its midpoint: molten ground under a red sky (0.13-5.5 s). It is an excerpt of the film's own 312-318 s.
2. **Put both counters on that first picture, already disagreeing.** Left: a year in the billions, climbing visibly within each second. Right: "5 min 07 s". The gap between the two numbers is the hook.
3. **One flat sentence states the small number in plain words.** 8 words, second person, present perfect, no adjective. It is set upright and heavier than the story lines that follow, so it reads as a statement about the viewer, not as part of the story.
4. **Then go back to the ordinary start and show the title as a question.** The street at 0 seconds (6.5 s); the title, "What if ... and couldn't stop?", centred, on for 3.4 s (6.8-10.2 s). The title is the only text ever placed in the middle of the frame.
5. **The cold open's promise is kept in sequence.** The film reaches the same counter values and the same picture at 312-318 s, 58% of the way through. The viewer knows where it is going and waits to see how.
6. **It needs no sound.** No voice; nothing in the opening depends on hearing it.

**At 60 seconds.** The reference spends 5.5 s on the cold open and does not start its story until 11.1 s. Scaled by share of run those would be 0.6 s and 1.2 s, too short to read. Keep the parts and cut their length: cold open with its line readable from the first frame, 3 s at most; title on about 2.5 s; first story line by about 6 s.

**Tied to results:** no result evidence.

**Do not copy**
- A black first frame and a picture that fades up (0.0-0.4 s). The line is not fully readable until 0.75 s.
- A dip to flat black between the cold open and the start (5.8-6.13 s). `compare.py` fails any run of empty frames.
- 11.1 s before the first story line.
- The title laid across the lamp post and a tree (6.8-10.2 s). Put the title over a clear part of the picture.

## Structure

1. **One quantity changes in one direction and never stops; the viewer stays put.** The scenario is told as what "you" would see from one spot.
2. **Each stage is one step up in the unit of the counter, shown by one new visible change.** Seconds: walkers too fast (10.3 s). Minutes: the Sun sinking (21.9 s). Hours: night, a lamp coming on (25.0 s). Days: day and night flickering (36.8 s). Then seasons, years, decades, centuries, and so on through 23 chapters to the last black hole (505 s).
3. **The order inside a stage is fixed: countdown, picture, line, consequence.** The "NEXT" row names the event and counts down to it (the first snow is announced from 31 s). The picture changes (about 51 s). A short line names it within a second ("Snow.", 51.7 s). A following line gives a consequence or a measure of it.
4. **When a unit becomes too fast to see, say so and move up one.** "Blurred" is used twice as that hinge, for day and night at 44 s and for the seasons at 73 s. The picture shows the blur first: the Sun becomes a stripe at 40.7 s.
5. **Keep returning to the viewer's own clock.** Seven check-ins restate how little time has passed for you (14.3, 58.2, 76.6, 254.5, 391.4, 483.5, 518.2 s), five with your own wrist and watch in shot.
6. **Plant an ordinary object early and bring it back at the end.** The street light comes on at 25.0 s. The last light in the universe turns out to be that street light at 527.7 s.
7. **Say plainly when something is not known.** Three lines do it in passing, without leaving the story (109.5, 291, 445 s): the form is "nobody knows whether X" or "it might not; say it does".
8. **Payoff late, then a turn, then back to the first picture.** Payoff picture at 505.5 s and its line at 506.9 s (94% of the run). Three lines of aftermath (510.7-520.4 s). A one-word turn at 521.9 s. The opening street again by 531 s. The outside clock resets to 5:42 and starts to speed up again; the file ends there, in motion (end hold 0.0 s).
9. **Shares of the run:** cold open 0-1.1%; title 1.1-1.9%; rule and first symptoms 1.9-8.2%; human scale (days to centuries) 8.2-21.6%; planet scale 21.6-59.4%; cosmic scale 59.4-94.0%; payoff 94.0-95.7%; aftermath 95.7-97.1%; turn and return 97.1-100%.

**At 60 seconds.**
- Fixed-length parts do not shrink in proportion, because reading time does not: hook and title about 6 s together; payoff, one aftermath line, a turn and a return about 9-11 s.
- That leaves about 44 s for stages. The reference's shortest chapter is 8 s with two lines (44-52 s), and the steps in its first minute run 3 to 10 s each. So: 5 or 6 stages of 7-9 s with two lines each, or 4 stages of about 11 s with three lines each.
- Payoff picture at about 48-52 s (0.80-0.87 of the run). The reference's 0.94 leaves it 31.7 s after the payoff; 0.94 of 60 s would leave 3.6 s, not enough for a turn and a return.
- One counter check-in near the middle and one in the close, in place of seven.
- The brief requires the script to say which parts are guesses. Use pattern 7's form on screen at each guessed stage. The reference leaves most of this to its description, which is on the Do not copy list.

**Tied to results:** no result evidence.

**Do not copy**
- Leaving the split between real and guessed to the description. Only 3 of 139 lines mark a guess.
- A line that states a time different from the counter beside it (255 s: "four minutes" against 4 min 09 s).
- Objects that outlive the stated time: trees still standing beside you hundreds of millions of years on (208-227 s). Viewers caught it (396 likes on the correction).
- No pointer to the sources anywhere on screen. One can sit on the closing picture; it does not need a held end card.

## Holding attention

1. **Something new every stage, and the stages come fast at the start.** In the first 60 s the tool logs 19 abrupt picture changes (3.17 per 10 s). All of them are the world flipping state (day to night, grass to snow), not edits. Over the whole run the rate is 1.28 per 10 s.
2. **About 15 to 16 short lines a minute, with empty space between them.** 139 story lines in 537 s: one starts every 3.79 s on average (14.7 to 16.5 a minute in every part of the film), each is on screen about 2.6 s on average including fades, and a story line is showing for 65% of the run. For the other third the picture and the counters carry it. Match these as averages; do not keep them as a fixed beat (see Do not copy).
3. **Short lines.** Mean 6.7 words. One row, never wrapped. 12 of the 139 are three words or fewer and are used for arrivals and endings ("Snow." 51.7 s, "Except you." 283.5 s, "Gone." 460 s, "Wait." 521.9 s).
4. **About 105 words a minute on screen,** counting the title and the cold-open line (944 words in 537.2 s). No spoken words.
5. **The picture leads the words.** Walkers speed up at 10.6 s, the line comes at 11.1 s. Night falls at 25.0 s, the line is full at 25.4 s.
6. **Translate each event into the viewer's seconds.** A night "in six seconds" (28 s); towers falling in six seconds (113 s); a canyon cut in five seconds (164 s).
7. **Small questions open and close all the time; two big ones span the film.** The NEXT row opens one every chapter and closes it 10 to 40 s later (at least 27 of them). A thread opened at 62.5 s (to them you are a statue) closes at 91.1 s. The cold open (0.5 s) closes at 312 s; the title (6.8 s) closes at 506.9 s.
8. **One long silence, placed before the payoff.** After "Here it comes." (497.7 s) there is no line for 7.0 s, against a usual gap of 1.2 s. The flash is at 505.5 s.
9. **Nothing is ever frozen.** Both counters change every frame; the sky is always moving; the camera turns slowly. Tool: frozen share 0.3%, longest frozen stretch 0.77 s.
10. **The scene empties as the scale grows.** Eight to ten kinds of object in the opening frame, two through the desert (208-300 s), one ring on black by 444 s. The reference lets movement fall away with the objects; that part is on the Do not copy list.

**At 60 seconds.**
- Lines: 13 to 16 story lines including the cold-open line, plus the title. The reference's own first 60 s has 15 story lines, the cold-open line and the title: 17 text events, 103 words.
- Words: 90 to 120 on screen in all (105 a minute, 15% either way).
- Time on screen per line: give each line time by its length. The reference's average load is 6.7 words in 2.6 s, about 2.6 words a second; at that rate a 4-word line needs 1.5 s and an 8-word line 3.1 s. Its shortest line is on for about 1.4 s.
- Gaps: vary them. Keep one gap of two to three times the usual length directly before the payoff.
- Flips: a film told like the reference's first minute will measure about 3 tool "cuts" per 10 s, not 1.28. See the notes in `benchmarks.json`.

**Tied to results:** no result evidence.

**Do not copy**
- The same 2.6 s for every line whatever its length. Its 12-word lines ask for more than 4 words a second.
- A fixed beat of one line every 3.6-4.0 s for six minutes (95-483 s) with five exceptions.
- A stretch where the only thing changing is the text: 437-496 s, one ring on black, tool motion under 0.3 for 77 s, placed at 81-92% of the run.
- Lines of 10 words or more (18 of 139). They do not fit a vertical frame in one row at a readable size.

## Sharing

Evidence is from the top 100 comments; anything marked "guess" is my reading.

1. **End on a line that is final and can be quoted alone.** The payoff line at 506.9 s is the one commenters repeat (ranks 3 and 10; 2,200 likes on rank 3).
2. **Follow the bleakest moment with a release.** The return to the street at 521.9-537 s is the other moment people point to (rank 43, timestamp 8:45, 548 likes; ranks 34, 19, 25).
3. **One strong picture early.** The Sun as a stripe at 40.7 s is timestamped by a commenter (rank 57, 742 likes).
4. **Let it be about the viewer's own life without saying so.** The most-liked comment (rank 1, 4,700 likes) reads the film as what one's thirties feel like; the only comment the uploader hearted (rank 86) is about spending time on family. The film never draws that moral itself. Guess: sending it says "this is how fast it goes", a feeling, not a fact.
5. **A premise people already have a name for travels with them.** 32 of the top 100 comments, with 48% of the likes, are in-jokes from one anime whose villain speeds up time. Guess: it was sent between people who share that reference. This is a property of the topic, not of the craft.

**Tied to results:** no result evidence.

**Do not copy**
- Nothing in the film names its sources or says "the big numbers are real". That sits in the description, where a forwarded video does not carry it.

## Comments

Counts are from the top 100 comments (36,756 likes between them).

1. **Second person makes the viewer a character, and people finish the character's story.** 15 comments (17% of likes) are jokes about the person standing there: a passer-by asking why you have stood crying on the pavement for eight minutes, your boss, the cameraman who never dies.
2. **Flat, short, final lines get quoted.** 8 comments quote or answer a line: seven a story line (six different lines, at 87, 91, 171, 283, 407 and 506.9 s) and one a counter comparison (rank 83).
3. **A recognisable premise draws its fandom.** 32 comments, 48% of likes (see Sharing 5).
4. **A loop or a detail at the end gives people something to explain.** The clock speeding up again (rank 34); the clock reading 5:42 when eight minutes have passed (rank 19); the film lasting about as long as sunlight takes to reach Earth (ranks 26, 48).
5. **Timestamps follow strong single moments.** Eight comments carry one: 0:30-0:37, 0:41, 2:07, 2:51, 3:28, 4:43, 8:40, 8:45.
6. **The film asks the viewer nothing.** No question to camera, no call to comment, no claim made to be disputed. The replies come from the frame it sets up.

**Tied to results:** no result evidence.

**Do not copy**
- A slip people correct: the trees still alive at 3:28 (396 likes on the correction). It drew comments, but as a mistake. Jokes about the premise itself (the city planned around you, rank 11; the watch, rank 20) are not slips.
- A look that reads as machine-made: six of the top 100 comments say so. The brief already requires new models and a new scene.

## Look

1. **One continuous first-person 3D view from standing height; no edit cuts.** The viewer never moves by choice. The camera turns slowly the whole time, turns to follow something passing (10.6-13.5 s), tilts up when a line says look up (28-30, 167-173 s), turns round when a line says behind you (30.6-33.6 s), and looks down at your own feet twice (138-141, 325-327 s). The ground lifts and drops it (ice 128-150 s, canyon 162-185 s, mountains 189-203 s).
2. **The viewer has a visible body.** Something of the viewer's own comes into frame at each check-in (in the reference, a wrist with a watch, five times) and is there when the camera looks down (feet, twice). Ours must be newly made: see Do not copy.
3. **Low-polygon, flat-shaded solids below; soft, glowing things above.** No outlines, no surface texture. Figures are reduced to the fewest solids, one flat colour each, no faces. The sky is the opposite: painted cloud, a glowing arc, star trails, particle galaxies.
4. **The ground colour names the stage.** Dark green (street, to 116 s), white (snow, 52-75 s), pale blue #849dbc (ice), banded red-brown #4a3323 (canyon), white #e1d7d4 (peaks), ochre #c18a53 (desert), bare brown #745242, molten yellow-orange (305-321 s), black (344 s on). A stage change is usually the ground swapping colour in one frame.
5. **The sky is the clock.** A sunset gradient (#bb7e68 at the horizon in the opening) that flips to night and back, then a stripe of light where the Sun was, then star trails, then darkness with stars by day (about 262 s).
6. **One warm accent.** Orange (tool accent #ac6634, 6.3% of the picture): the sunset glow, the molten ground, the black hole's disc, and the single dot at 524.5 s that is the street light. Text is warm white (about #fefaf3).
7. **Brightness falls with the story and returns at the end.** Mid-tone for the first 64% of the run (chapter means 0.22-0.48), near-black from 64% to 98.6% (0.02-0.05), the opening picture again for the last 1.4%. Whole run: mean brightness 0.23, saturation 0.274, dark share 0.58, "muted".
8. **Two counter blocks, top corners, on every frame from first to last.** Same place and style throughout; only the contents change.
   - Left, the outside quantity: a small caps label, a large serif figure, a small caps rate row ("ONE SECOND FOR YOU = ..."), a thin rule, a "NEXT" row naming the coming event with a countdown.
   - Right, your time: a small caps label ("YOU HAVE BEEN STANDING HERE FOR"), a large serif figure running at real time, a small caps comparison ("OUT THERE: ...").
9. **The large left figure changes its unit and its form as it climbs,** 15 times in 537 s: a clock with seconds, without seconds, a weekday and date, a month and year, a year, millions, billions, powers of ten. Five of those changes fall in the first 66 s. Each change of form is a beat in itself.
10. **The comparison row turns the number into something human, and then runs out.** A whole day (34 s), a whole year (61 s), a human lifetime (about 90 s), longer than writing, than humans, than the dinosaurs, than animals, than the Earth, than the universe, and at about 418 s "NO COMPARISON IS LEFT".
11. **The NEXT row ends on its own punchline.** At the payoff (506 s) it reads "NOTHING" with the countdown replaced by "ever".
12. **Story line: one row of italic serif, bottom centre, warm white.** Cap height about 3.9% of frame height, baseline about 90.5% down the frame (estimates from stills). Fades in and out over 0.2-0.3 s. The cold-open line sits in the same place, upright and heavier. The line must stay readable over every ground colour; the reference's own answer, a faint halo, is on the Do not copy list.
13. **Two typefaces only.** A serif with a true italic for figures, title and story lines; a wide-tracked sans in capitals for labels. Neither identified by name.
14. **The centre of the frame belongs to the scene.** Text lives in the top corners and bottom centre; the only exception is the title.
15. **Transitions are in the world, not in the edit.** Instant swaps of one element, or slow morphs. Black frames near the end still carry the counters (514-528 s), so the picture is never empty there.
16. **A callout in the scene is allowed once.** A thin circle, a leader line and a small caps label mark two points in the sky at 120.6-123 s. Not used again.

**At 60 seconds, vertical.**
- The two counter blocks will not sit side by side at the reference's size: their widest rows are about 450 px and 430 px, which with the reference's margins comes to about 1,100 px against a 1,080 px frame. They need a new layout, and the small rows need to be larger in any case (see Do not copy). That is a decision for the moodboard; the reference does not show a vertical layout.
- A story line at a readable size on a phone will hold about 5 or 6 words in one row (my estimate, to be checked on the moodboard). The reference's 12-word lines span 77% of a 1920 px frame. Either keep lines short or set them in two rows.
- Changes of counter form: the reference makes 5 in its first 66 s, about one every 11-13 s. Expect 4 to 6 in 60 s, roughly one a stage.
- NEXT events: 7 in the reference's first 66 s; expect 5 or 6, one a stage.
- Comparison row: 3 states in the reference's first 62 s; expect 3 to 5.
- Brightness: `compare.py` holds our whole-run brightness and dark share to the reference's (0.23 and 0.58), which are low because a third of the reference is near-black. In proportion, a 60 s film would be mid-tone to about 38 s, near-black from about 38 s to 59 s, with the return in the last second or so. In practice the return needs 4 to 5 s to be read, so the dark part ends earlier.

**Tied to results:** no result evidence.

**Do not copy**
- The reference's own models and scene: capsule walkers, faceted trees, the street, the lamp and bench, the watch arm. The brief forbids it.
- Small rows at a cap height of 1.1% of frame height and reduced opacity (the rate, NEXT and comparison rows). They carry some of the best ideas and almost nobody quotes them.
- White story lines over white ground with only a faint halo for contrast (52-55, 131-150, 194-200 s).
- Empty black frames with no counters (0.0-0.13 s, 5.8-6.13 s).
- A third of the run with almost nothing moving. Keep the dark part of pattern 7; do not keep its stillness (tool motion 0.08-0.13 across 444-505 s).

## Sound

What the tools show. Tone of voice and musical character are not measurable from here and are not described.

1. **No voice.** `transcribe.py` found no speech, and a second pass without its voice filter found none either. Every word is on screen.
2. **One continuous low bed, never silent.** Audible for 100% of the run. In every 20 s window at least 85% of the energy is below 250 Hz and 52-77% is below 80 Hz. Steady components at 49 Hz and 98 Hz throughout; more components (147, 196, 220, 294 Hz) from 360 s.
3. **A slow regular pulse while time is running fast.** The low band swells about every 0.41-0.45 s from 24 s to about 524 s. No such pulse was found in 6-24 s, or after the return at 524 s.
4. **Level is flat, with one large move at the payoff.** Loudness range 3.1 LU. The level drops by about 12 dB across 508-511 s, as the payoff line leaves, holds there for ten seconds while the lines say nothing is left, and returns across 521-524 s, starting on the one-word turn and just ahead of the light.
5. **In that quiet, a click every second.** Nine high-band clicks exactly 1.00 s apart, 515.0-523.0 s, with the watch on screen at 518.2-520.4 s.
6. **The tools find no sound tied to individual picture changes.** Only seven other high-band events in 537 s (the lack of them is on the Do not copy list). The tool's 65% of cuts on a sound is inside what chance gives with 2.88 onsets a second (37% to 82%), so it is not evidence of sync. A producer aiming at that row needs sounds under the flips or a bed with about three onsets a second.
7. **Measured levels:** integrated -21.4 LUFS, true peak -7.0 dBTP, 2.88 onsets a second.

**At 60 seconds.** The one move scales directly: bed under everything, a drop of about 12 dB at the payoff for the aftermath lines, a once-a-second click in the quiet, and the bed back on the turn. In the reference the quiet lasts about 10 s out of 537; in 60 s it has room for 3 to 4 s.

**Tied to results:** no result evidence.

**Do not copy**
- -21.4 LUFS as a level to aim at for a Short without a decision. It is 7.4 LU under the -14 LUFS YouTube plays loud videos at, and YouTube does not turn quiet videos up. `compare.py` passes loudness within 1.5 LU of the reference, so a louder master will show as a difference on that row. Whether to accept that is for the producer and the brief owner; whether the reference's quietness was deliberate cannot be known from here.
- A bed with no distinct events for minutes at a time (three high-band events between 6 s and 510 s).
