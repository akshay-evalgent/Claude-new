- **Subject:** a lyric video. The words of a song appear on screen in time with the song as it plays; the lyrics are the picture.
- **Audience:** general viewers of music and short video. Not specified further by the user.
- **Platform:** not specified. Take the format (orientation, size, length) from the reference.
- **Reference (link, supplied by the user):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lp1KuI4E1xk
  Study it for how a lyric video works: how the words are set, timed, moved and coloured, and what sits behind them.
- **Supplied material:** none. The user chose original lyrics for this test: the scriptwriter writes short original lyrics (fiction, invented for this video), the built-in voice speaks them over the built-in sound bed, and the words are synced on screen. - **Length:** about 20 to 30 seconds (the user's choice for this test), whatever the reference's length.
- **Must not:** reuse the reference's song, recording, lyrics, artwork or footage in our video. The reference is for study only.

## What the reference turned out to be

The reference is not a lyric video. It is one still title card (cream paper, a stippled ink drawing of two crying eyes, one word in dark red) held for the whole 185 seconds under a full song. No lyric appears on screen.

So for this run:

- **Look:** take it from the reference's card: paper ground, ink drawing, one dark red accent, most of the frame left empty. New drawings and new words; nothing of the card itself is reused.
- **Song shape:** take it from the reference's song as analysed: a refrain stated first and repeated, short lines, words addressed to "you" about one plain feeling.
- **Lyric sync:** the reference gives no lead, so this comes from the brief. Each line of the lyrics appears on screen as it is spoken, and within the line the words light up or arrive one by one in time with the voice, so a viewer could follow along with the sound off. Use real word times (run `transcribe.py` on the voice track), not estimates.
- **How the pipeline's parts map onto a lyric video:** one beat per lyric line. The beat's "picture" is how that line is set on screen, plus whatever the drawing does during it. The ground and the drawing carry through the whole video, so a change of beat reads as the words changing, not as a new scene.
- **Language:** English, because the built-in voice is English.
- **Format:** horizontal 16:9 like the reference, at 1920x1080.
