Same research as the quicksand kit (`../quicksand/UPLOAD_KIT.md`): Zack D. Films titles are Title Case
with one emoji at the end, descriptions are blank, thumbnails are a frame of the video with its caption,
and every Short has burned-in captions. Their channel has no video about lightning hitting a car; the
closest, "What To Do If Power Lines Fall On Your Car 😨", has 56M views.

## Facts used in the script (all checked)

| Line | Source |
|---|---|
| Rubber tires give no protection | NOAA: "Rubber soles of shoes (or rubber tires) are not insulators from lightning. They offer zero lightning protection." |
| The metal shell protects you, windows closed | NWS: "the outer metal shell of hard-topped metal vehicles does provide protection to those inside a vehicle with the windows closed." |
| Hits the antenna or roof, through the shell, then the tires to the ground | NWS lightning and cars page |
| 30,000 amps | NWS "How Powerful Is Lightning?": about 300 million volts and 30,000 amps |
| Five times hotter than the sun's surface | NWS: heats the air to 50,000 °F, about 5 times hotter than the surface of the sun |
| Melted antenna, tires, rear window, electronics | NWS: "damage to the antenna, electrical system, rear windshield, and tires is common" |
| Convertibles give no protection, even with the top up | NOAA: "Convertible vehicles offer no safety from lightning, even if the top is 'up'." |
| "Miles of open sky" | NWS lightning myths: strikes often land more than three miles from the storm |
