# What all measured @wealth reels have in common

Three reels so far (`01`, `02`, `03` in this folder), with 6.3M to 29.1M views each. The same template shows up every time.

| Reel | Hook | Views | Likes / views | Comments | Length | Audio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 02 | Words Have Power | 29.1M | 8.3% | 3,045 | 60.0 s | Trending song, slowed |
| 01 | The Perfect Example Of What Working Hard Vs Working Smart Looks Like: | 17.6M | 5.5% | 1,377 | 41.6 s | The film's own sound |
| 03 | POV: You Learned How To Focus On The Solution, Not The Problem | 6.3M | 4.0% | 304 | 14.3 s | Trending song |

## 1. The topic is life lessons, not business news

All three are about mindset: hard work vs smart work, perspective in hard times, solving problems fast. None is a news story or a company breakdown, even though the bio says "business, tech, & news". At least at the top end, **@wealth is a mindset page with a money-sounding name.**

## 2. The frame never changes (measured from all three covers)

- 9:16, black.
- The clip is full width and **always starts at 31% down the frame** (y = 600 on a 1080 x 1920 canvas). Its height follows the source: 3:2 in two reels, closer to square in one.
- The hook sits **directly above the clip**, bottom edge about 40 px above it. 1 to 3 lines, centred, Title Case, white bold rounded sans, key words in pale yellow (#F5FA5A). Emojis are allowed.
- Everything below the clip is black, which leaves room for Instagram's caption overlay.
- No logo, handle, watermark or subtitles.

## 3. The clips are found footage

A Hollywood comedy scene, a staged short-film moment, a viral home video. None were made by @wealth and two of three are uncredited. The lesson comes from the hook and the caption, not from the footage. This is exactly what Instagram's originality rules push down for accounts without @wealth's size and history, so a new page needs footage it has rights to (see `content/07-LESSON-CLIP-REELS.md`).

## 4. Three hook types

| Type | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Metaphor | The Perfect Example Of What [A] Vs [B] Looks Like: | reel 01 |
| Story title | [Short Lesson Title] + 1 or 2 emojis | reel 02, "Words Have Power" |
| POV | POV: You Learned How To [Skill] | reel 03 |

## 5. Audio is emotional or trending

Two of three use a trending licensed song (one a slowed, sad-sounding version). The film clip keeps its own sound because the scene needs it. Music sets the mood, the clip shows the moment, and the caption delivers the lesson.

## 6. The caption is the same template every time

1. **One-sentence thesis plus two emojis.** For example, a treadmill line about running hard and going nowhere (🧠🏁).
2. **The universal pain**, often a list of 3 or 4 everyday examples (a breakup, a loss, bills, difficult people).
3. **The reframe:** what's actually going on.
4. **The advice:** what to do instead, sometimes with famous examples (Bezos, Buffett).
5. **A closing aphorism** of one or two lines.
6. **The fixed call to action:** "Follow @Wealth to learn valuable advice every time you open Instagram. ⭐" Identical on all three.
7. **Credit** if the clip is a film ("Media: [title]").
8. **Exactly 5 hashtags**, always starting with #Wealth, then #Mindset or #LifeLessons and two topic tags.
9. A blank Braille character (⠀) between paragraphs so Instagram keeps the spacing.

## 7. People like, they don't argue

Comments run at 0.005% to 0.02% of views. Likes run at 4% to 8%. These reels make people feel understood. They aren't built to start debates. The highest like rate came from the most emotional reel.
