# Originality and sourcing: what you can put in a reel

Big business pages, @wealth included, grew partly on downloaded clips. If a new account does that in 2026, Instagram cuts it off from non-followers, and those are the only people a new account can reach. This page is the line between "inspired by @wealth" and "invisible".

This is practical guidance, not legal advice. Copyright law differs by country. If you're ever unsure about a clip, leave it out.

## The two separate questions

1. **Is it legal?** Copyright. Can the owner take it down or strike your account?
2. **Will Instagram recommend it?** Originality. Even legal reposts get swapped out of recommendations for the original.

You need a yes to both. A credit tag in the caption ("🎥: @someone") answers neither question.

## What counts as original (Instagram's view)

Based on Instagram's originality guidance as summarised by agencies in October 2026:

**Counts as adding something:** new information placed on the content, your own graphics, substantial commentary or analysis, third-party material rebuilt with your own graphics or data, remixes that add context.

**Not enough on its own:** adding a logo, border, watermark or subtitles. A caption credit. Cropping, resizing or speeding up. A new caption.

**Treated as unoriginal:** watermarked reposts, screenshots with only a credit tag, slightly cropped copies.

The rule of thumb: **what you added must be visible in the reel itself, and it must be the main thing the reel offers.**

Instagram doesn't publish a percentage. Agencies suggest keeping almost all of your recent posts original over any rolling 30 days, so recovery is possible if you slip. Check Settings > Account status to see whether your account is eligible for recommendations.

## Footage: safe sources

| Source | Cost | Use for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Your own screen recordings** (articles, product pages, apps, charts) | Free | F1, F6 | Highlight and annotate, don't just scroll. Keep article screenshots brief and add your take. |
| **Your own phone footage** (stores, products, streets, receipts) | Free | Everything | Don't film people's faces without permission. Store interiors may have photo policies. |
| **Pexels, Pixabay** | Free | B-roll | Read each license. Fine for commercial use generally. Be careful with clips showing trademarks or recognisable people in a negative story. |
| **Storyblocks, Envato Elements, Artgrid** | Monthly fee | B-roll at volume | Worth it once you post daily. Much bigger business and city libraries. |
| **Company newsrooms and press kits** (most large companies publish b-roll and photos for media use) | Free | F1 news | Usually allowed for editorial/news use, not in ads. Read the terms on the page. |
| **Charts and graphics you make** (Canva, Datawrapper, `scripts/`) | Free | F4, F5 | Do the maths right. Cite the data source. |
| **AI-generated visuals** | Varies | Concepts you can't film | Label with Instagram's "AI info" when it looks real. Never fake a real person, real event or real product. |
| **Podcast or interview clips** | Free with permission | F3 | Only with permission, through an official clipping program, or very short with your commentary as most of the reel. Otherwise use a quote card plus your VO. |

## Never use

- Videos downloaded from TikTok, YouTube or another Instagram page, with or without credit
- Anything with another app's watermark (TikTok, CapCut templates, other pages' handles)
- Full news broadcasts or TV clips
- Movie and TV scenes as b-roll
- Music you downloaded and added yourself. Use Instagram's audio library so the rights are handled.

## Music

- **Normal posts:** trending sounds from Instagram's library (Creator accounts get the full library).
- **Sponsored or paid posts:** only commercially licensed audio. In Instagram, that's the library a Business account sees, or royalty-free music you've licensed.
- **TikTok and YouTube:** add music natively in each app, not baked into the file. The rights are platform by platform.

## Facts

A business page lives on trust. A single wrong number in a reel that breaks out gets screenshotted.

1. Every number gets two independent sources. Company filings and official releases beat articles. Articles beat blogs.
2. Put the main source at the end of the caption.
3. Write "about" or "roughly" when a figure is estimated, and say which year a figure is for.
4. If you get something wrong: pin a correcting comment, fix the caption, and if it's serious, post the correction as its own reel. Pages that correct themselves gain trust.
5. **Money posts:** state your assumptions on screen (rate of return, time period), never promise returns, and add "not financial advice" to the caption on anything about investing.

## Using real people and brands

- Reporting on companies and public figures is normal news. Don't make claims you can't source.
- Don't use a company's logo in a way that looks like the company endorses you.
- Don't put a real person's face on a sponsored post without permission.
- Never use AI to make a public figure say something they didn't say, even as a joke. Instagram removes it and it ends trust instantly.
