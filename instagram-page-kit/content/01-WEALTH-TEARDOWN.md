# @wealth teardown: what it is, why it gets millions of views, and what to copy

## What I could see and what I couldn't

- **Real data from three reels** (`reference/`), read from Instagram's public embed pages: 6.3M, 17.6M and 29.1M views, layouts measured from their cover frames, caption structure. **All three are life-lesson clips about mindset**, not news. ## How it got here

The page did not start as a news brand. Cached snapshots show three stages:

1. **"Business | Motivation | NFT"**, about 4.4M followers. Quote graphics and motivation, with whatever was trending (NFTs, at the time) stuffed into the Name field so the page showed up in search.
2. **"Business | Motivation | Quotes"**, about 5.1M followers. Same quote-page model, trend word dropped.
3. **"The leading source for all things business, tech, & news"**, about 15M. It turned itself into a media brand: news, tech, infographics, finance tips.

Two lessons for you:

- Quote pages grow followers cheaply, but they top out, and advertisers pay quote pages very little. The page roughly tripled after it became a news and explainer brand. Start where they ended up, not where they began.
- The Name field is a search tool. They changed it as trends changed. You should write yours for search too (see `02-PROFILE-SETUP.md`).

## Why its reels reach millions

1. **Volume.** Every post is another chance at a breakout. Most of their reels are ordinary. A few each week break out, and those carry the account.
2. **You get the story with the sound off, in one second.** The headline is burned into the top of the frame in big black-on-white text. A thumb that is scrolling past still reads it.
3. **Topics come with curiosity attached.** Dollar amounts, famous names (Musk, Bezos, Buffett, Huang), household brands, new tech that looks like science fiction. Nobody needs context for "Costco hasn't raised this price in 40 years."
4. **People send them to friends.** Business news is what people forward to a coworker or a group chat with "did you see this". Instagram's head, Adam Mosseri, has said sends count for more than anything else when Instagram shows a post to people who don't follow you, and that most reach now comes from recommendations.
5. **People argue in the comments.** A billionaire's spicy opinion, a "would you take this deal" question, a layoff story. Comments are a smaller ranking signal than sends, but arguments keep people watching and coming back to the post.
6. **The name does a lot of work.** "@wealth" reads like a publication. Yours should sound like one too, not like a person's side project.
7. **They post everywhere.** The same brand runs a 6M+ TikTok. Each video gets a second and third shot at an audience on other platforms.

## Confirmed: the Lesson Clip (measured on three reels, 6.3M to 29.1M views)

- **Frame:** black. The hook is a centred Title Case line in white with key words in pale yellow, sitting directly above a full-width clip that always starts 31% down the frame. Black below. No headline box, no handle, no subtitles.
- **Hook types:** a story title ("Words Have Power"), a metaphor ("The Perfect Example Of What A Vs B Looks Like:") or a POV ("POV: You Learned How To...").
- **Clip:** found footage, such as a film scene, a staged moment or a viral home video.
- **Audio:** usually a trending song, slowed for emotional clips.
- **Length:** 14 to 60 s.
- **Caption:** the same essay template every time, with a fixed follow line and exactly 5 hashtags.

Full measurements: `reference/PATTERNS.md`. Rebuilt for a new account without the copyright risk: `07-LESSON-CLIP-REELS.md`.

## Anatomy of the caption

```
Line 1   The headline again, with one extra fact. This shows before "more", so it has to work alone.
         (blank line)
Para 1   What happened, in two sentences. The number goes here.
Para 2   Why it matters, or the part most people miss.
Para 3   A question that has two sides: "Would you pay $X for this?"
         (blank line)
         Follow @yourhandle for business news that makes sense.
         Source: Reuters / company filing / The Verge
         3 to 5 hashtags, specific ones, not #business #money
```

## How they make money (and how you will)

- **Sponsored posts.** Finance apps, AI tools, trading platforms, courses, business books. Finance and business audiences command some of the highest rates per follower. faceless.my estimates 3 to 5 times what lifestyle pages earn.
- **Affiliate links.** Financial products and software pay per signup, not per view.
- **Off-Instagram list.** Newsletter or WhatsApp/Telegram channel, so a change in the algorithm can't wipe out the business.

## What to copy, and what not to copy, in 2026

**Copy:**
- The headline packaging. Black-on-white top bar, one highlighted word, readable in a second.
- The topic radar: money, famous names, big brands, new tech.
- The volume. One reel a day at the very least, two when you can.
- A Name field written for search, and a handle that sounds like a publication.
- Posting the same videos to TikTok and YouTube Shorts, with no watermarks.
- The business model: sponsors and affiliates in finance and software.

**Don't copy:**
- **Reposting other people's clips.** Big pages built their back catalogue on downloaded podcast and news clips. Since 2024, Instagram has been swapping reposts out of recommendations in favour of the original, and accounts that mostly post reposts can lose their place in recommendations altogether, so non-followers stop seeing them. A brand-new account lives entirely on non-followers. This one rule would sink you. Rights holders can also take reposts down with copyright strikes. Every reel you post must have something visibly yours in it: your script, your voice, your graphics, your data, your screen recording. See `06-ORIGINALITY-AND-SOURCING.md`.
- **The quote-page era.** Saturated, cheap to copy, pays badly.
- **Other apps' watermarks.** Mosseri said in October 2026 that content made in other apps is fine, but a visible logo from another app reduces distribution. Export clean files.
- **Trend words in your name that have nothing to do with you.** "NFT" aged badly.

## Your edge

@wealth is a firehose. It tells people what happened. You're going to explain it: what happened, why, and the one number people will repeat at dinner. That is easier to make original, easier to send, and it builds the kind of trust that sponsors pay for.

The working line for the page: **business news that makes sense in 30 seconds.**
