# The first 30 posts, ready to make

> **DRAFT.** Built from the genre and third-party data, not from watching @wealth's reels (Instagram blocked access). It gets rebuilt once real reels are in `reference/`.

Twenty-seven evergreen scripts plus three templates for daily news. Evergreen topics don't go stale, so you can make them in batches on weekends and keep your daily time for news.

**Before you post any of these:** check every fact in the "Verify" line against the source named there. These are well-documented stories, but figures change every year (Costco's membership fees, company values), and you are the publisher. A source that's a year newer beats this file.

How to read each entry:
- **Headline**: the top-bar text. `[yellow]` marks the highlighted word. Make the PNG with `scripts/headline.py`.
- **VO**: what you say. About 2.5 words a second, so 110 words is roughly 45 seconds.
- **B-roll**: search terms for Pexels, Pixabay or Storyblocks, or things to film yourself.
- **Send line**: the sentence the reel is built around. Use it in the caption when it fits.

## Daily news templates (use one a day)

### N1. Company news (F1 Headline)
- **Headline:** [Company] just [did the thing]. [Yellow: the surprising number or consequence]
- **VO (optional, 3 sentences):** What happened. The number. Who it affects, or why they did it.
- **Footage:** company newsroom b-roll, or your screen recording of the announcement with the key line highlighted.
- **Caption:** headline restated with one extra fact, 2 short paragraphs, a two-sided question, source.
- **Worked shape:** "Spotify is raising prices again. [Third time in two years.]" "Would you pay $X for this, or cancel?"

### N2. Tech and AI demo (F6 Demo)
- **Headline:** This [tool] can [result]. [Yellow: the part that sounds impossible]
- **Footage:** your own screen recording. Result first, then the 10 seconds that show how.
- **Caption:** tool name, price, what it's good at, one honest downside, "Would you use this at work?"

### N3. Billionaire or founder says (F3 Take)
- **Headline:** [Name] says [the claim in plain words]. [Yellow: the spiciest part]
- **VO:** one sentence on where and when they said it, then your take with one number that backs it or breaks it.
- **Caption:** link or name the interview. "Is [Name] right?"

### #01 The $1.50 hot dog
- **Format:** F2 Breakdown, 45 s
- **Headline:** Costco loses money on this hot dog. [On purpose.]
- **VO:** "Costco has sold this hot dog and soda for a dollar fifty since 1985. Beef went up. Buns went up. Wages went up. The price didn't. The co-founder reportedly told the next CEO, if you raise the hot dog, I will kill you. So why would a company keep a deal that loses money? Because Costco barely needs to make money on food. It makes its money on the card in your wallet. Membership fees bring in about five billion dollars a year, which is more than half of Costco's profit. And about nine in ten members renew. The hot dog is the reason you remember Costco. The sixty-five dollars a year is the business."
- **On screen:** $1.50 · 1985 · ~$5B membership fees · 9 in 10 renew · $65/yr
- **B-roll:** warehouse store aisles, hot dog close-up, membership card, shopping carts, checkout
- **Send line:** "Send this to your friend who goes to Costco just for the hot dog."
- **Verify:** Costco annual report (10-K) for membership fee revenue, net income and renewal rate. Price history and the Sinegal quote in widely reported interviews with Craig Jelinek.
