Episode 1 · Why Skinny Guys Stay Skinny, and How to Get Bigger
Narration only. Read calmly, like a friend explaining; pause at each line break.
Lines starting with [RIVAL] or [HOST] are character lines (different voice or delivery).


== COLD OPEN ==

[Scene 1 · 0:00]
In 1999, scientists at the Mayo Clinic ran an experiment most skinny guys would sign up for.
They fed sixteen people one thousand extra calories, every single day, for eight weeks.
Same extra food for everyone. Nobody was told to exercise.

[Scene 2 · 0:21]
At the end, some of them had stored about ten times more fat than others.
Same food. Same eight weeks.
The difference was something they did without noticing.

[Scene 3 · 0:37]
Fidgeting. Standing up. Walking around.
In one person, that everyday movement went up by almost seven hundred calories a day. Their body simply burned off the extra food.
If you've ever said "I eat so much and I never gain", this might be you.

[Scene 4 · 0:59]
So, three reasons you stay skinny, and a plan to fix each one, using food and training you can do at home.
Quick note: we're not doctors. If you're losing weight without trying, see one first.

== PART 1 · THE BODY: WHY YOU STAY SKINNY ==

[Scene 5 · 1:16]
[RIVAL]: "Bro, I eat like a horse. I just have a fast metabolism."

[Scene 6 · 1:23]
Here's the first problem. We remember the big meals.
The wedding buffet. The double burger. The night you finished a whole pizza.
We forget the skipped breakfast, the lunch that was just tea, and the day we weren't hungry.
Your body doesn't count the big meal. It counts the whole week.
And for a lot of skinny guys, the weekly average is just enough to stay the same.

[Scene 7 · 1:56]
There's a reason for that. When your stomach stretches and food starts arriving, hormones and nerves tell your brain you've had enough.
Some people get that "full" signal early and strongly.
So you eat a big lunch, feel stuffed for hours, and your dinner quietly shrinks.
You're not lying when you say you eat a lot. You're just not eating a lot every day.

[Scene 8 · 2:24]
Second reason. Your body spends energy in three ways.
The biggest is just keeping you alive: your heart, brain, organs.
A small slice goes to digesting food.
The rest is movement. And most of that isn't the gym. It's walking, standing, fidgeting.

[Scene 9 · 2:43]
That's the part that changed the most in the Mayo Clinic study.
When people ate more, some of them automatically moved more. They didn't decide to. Their nervous system just did it.
So if you add a little food and the scale doesn't move, it's not because food doesn't work on you.
Some of it was burned off by movement you didn't notice. You just need a bit more than you think.

[Scene 10 · 3:15]
Third reason, and this is the big one.
Muscle is expensive. It costs energy to build and energy to keep.
So your body only builds it when it has a reason.

[Scene 11 · 3:30]
Inside every muscle fibre, two things are always happening. Your body builds new muscle protein, and it breaks old protein down.
When you don't train, those stay roughly balanced. Nothing changes.
Hard training tips the balance toward building, as long as you've eaten enough food and protein.

[Scene 12 · 3:51]
Hard training means sets that take you close to the point where you can't do another rep.
That strain on the muscle fibres is the signal.
No signal, no reason to grow, no matter how much you eat.
Eat a lot without training, and most of the extra ends up as fat.

[Scene 13 · 4:14]
[RIVAL]: "Face it, bro. You're an ectomorph. Born skinny."

[Scene 14 · 4:20]
"Body types" come from a 1940s psychology theory, not from muscle science.
Genetics are real, though. In one study, 585 people did the exact same twelve-week arm program.
Some barely changed. A few grew their arm muscles by more than half.
Most people landed somewhere in between, and they grew.
You won't know where you land until you train properly, eat enough and give it time. Most skinny guys never get that far.

== PART 2 · THE FUEL ==

[Scene 15 · 4:56]
[RIVAL]: "Easy. Dirty bulk. Eat everything."

[Scene 16 · 5:01]
Not quite. Your body can only build muscle so fast.
Eat way more than that and the rest is stored as fat.
A review on gaining muscle suggests eating about ten to twenty percent above what you need, and aiming to gain about a quarter to half a percent of your body weight per week.
For a sixty-kilo guy, that's about one hundred fifty to three hundred grams a week. Slow. Steady.

[Scene 17 · 5:32]
How do you know if you're eating enough? Use the scale, but use it the right way.
Weigh yourself every morning, and look at the weekly average, not one day.
If the average hasn't moved in two weeks, add about three hundred calories a day.
That's roughly a glass of milk and a banana.

[Scene 18 · 5:56]
Next, protein. It's the raw material.
A big analysis of forty-nine studies found that gains in muscle levelled off at about one point six grams of protein per kilo of body weight per day.
For a sixty-kilo guy, that's about ninety-six grams a day.

[Scene 19 · 6:16]
Here's what that can look like in a normal day. Eggs and toast in the morning. Rice, dal and curd for lunch.
Chicken or paneer with rotis for dinner. A handful of roasted chana as a snack.
Spread over the day, ninety-six grams is not hard.

[Scene 20 · 6:43]
And if you get full fast, drink some of your calories.
Milk, a banana, two spoons of peanut butter and some oats. Blend it.
That's about six hundred forty calories and twenty-three grams of protein, and it doesn't feel like a meal.
Calories that don't fill you up are the skinny guy's best friend.

[Scene 21 · 7:10]
Three more tricks if eating more feels like a chore.
One. Add a spoon of oil or ghee to your rice or dal. That's about a hundred and twenty calories, and you won't feel it.
Two. Keep peanuts or nuts within reach. A small handful is around a hundred and seventy calories.
Three. Add one snack a day at the same time, like a sandwich after training. Habits beat willpower.

== PART 3 · THE TRAINING ==

[Scene 22 · 7:41]
Now the signal. Three rules.
One. Make your sets hard. Stop one to three reps before you couldn't do another.
Two. Do about ten or more hard sets per muscle every week.
Three. Train each muscle two or three times a week, not once.

[Scene 23 · 8:01]
And good news if you train at home. Lighter loads build muscle too, as long as you take the set close to failure.
Your muscle can't tell the difference between a dumbbell and your own body weight. It only feels the effort.

[Scene 24 · 8:20]
Here are six moves that cover your whole body with no equipment.
Push-ups for the chest. Keep your body in one straight line and your elbows at about forty-five degrees, not flared out.
Door rows for the back. Hold both handles of an open door, lean back, and pull your chest to the door.
Split squats for the legs. Let your front knee follow your toes, and lower yourself slowly.
Pike push-ups for the shoulders. Hips high, and your head goes down between your hands.
Single-leg glute bridges. Push through your heel and pause at the top.
And a towel curl for the biceps, or chin-ups if you have a bar. Keep your elbows pinned.
Every move has an easier and a harder version, so there's always a version that's hard for you.

[Scene 25 · 9:30]
How to progress: do three sets of eight to fifteen reps.
When you can do fifteen on every set, switch to the harder version, or add a backpack with books.
That's it. More reps, then a harder version. Every week, a little more.

[Scene 26 · 9:53]
Two small things that make a big difference.
Start each move with one easy warm-up set.
And rest one to three minutes between hard sets, so the next set can be hard too. Rushing your rest just makes every set weaker.

[Scene 27 · 10:12]
And sleep. In one study, a single night without sleep cut muscle building by eighteen percent.
Seven to nine hours. It's part of the program, not a bonus.

== PART 4 · THE PLAN ==

[Scene 28 · 10:25]
Here's the whole plan on one screen. Screenshot it.
Three full-body workouts a week, six moves, three hard sets each.
Eat about three hundred calories more than usual, hit your protein, and drink the shake if you get full fast.
Weigh every morning. Watch the weekly average. If it's flat for two weeks, eat a bit more.

[Scene 29 · 10:59]
What to expect: in the first few weeks, you'll get stronger fast. That's mostly your nervous system learning the moves.
Real size takes longer. Give it twelve weeks, and take a photo on day one.

== QUICK ANSWERS (FAQ) ==

[Scene 30 · 11:18]
[RIVAL]: "Won't I just get fat?"
A little fat comes along with any bulk. That's normal.
Keep the gain slow, a quarter to half a percent of your body weight a week, and most of it goes where you want it.
If your waist grows much faster than your arms and chest, eat a little less.

[Scene 31 · 11:43]
"Do I need supplements?"
No. Food and training do the work.
If you want one, creatine monohydrate is the most studied. It helps muscle gain during training, and it's considered safe for healthy adults.
Protein powder is just food in a tub. Useful if it helps you hit your protein, never required.
Under eighteen, or have a health condition? Ask a doctor first.

[Scene 32 · 12:11]
"Can I still do cardio or play sports?"
Yes. Walking and sports are good for your heart, and they won't stop you growing.
Just remember they burn calories. On days you play, eat a bit more.
