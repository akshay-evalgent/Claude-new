Episode 1 · Why You're Skinny, and How to Actually Get Bigger

Scientists take sixteen people and make them eat a thousand extra calories every day.

Nobody goes to the gym. Nobody does anything special.

[Scene 2 · 0:20]
You'd think everyone gains the same, right?
Nope. Some people gained about ten times more fat than others.
Same food. Same eight weeks.

[Scene 3 · 0:34]
So what was different about the ones who stayed skinny?
Their bodies started moving more. Without them even noticing.
Shaking their leg. Standing up more. Walking around.
One person was burning almost seven hundred extra calories a day, just from that.
Their body burned off a big chunk of the extra food.

[Scene 4 · 0:58]
If you're the guy who says "bro, I eat so much and I still don't gain", this video is for you.
Three reasons you're skinny. And a simple plan to fix it, at home.

== PART 1 · WHY YOU'RE SKINNY ==

[Scene 5 · 1:20]
[RIVAL]: "Bro, I eat a LOT. I just have a fast metabolism."

[Scene 6 · 1:27]
Eating "a lot" is tricky.
You remember the big meals. That wedding. That pizza night. That time you finished two plates of biryani.
You don't remember the day you woke up late and skipped breakfast. Or the lunch that was just chips. Or the day you weren't hungry.
Your body doesn't care about your best day. It counts the whole week.
And for most skinny guys, the whole week adds up to just enough to stay the same.

[Scene 7 · 2:03]
Why does this happen?
When you eat, your stomach stretches, and it basically texts your brain: "we're full, stop eating."
Some people get that message early and loud.
So you eat a huge lunch, feel stuffed all afternoon, and dinner quietly gets smaller.
You're not lying when you say you eat a lot. You just don't eat a lot every day.

[Scene 8 · 2:29]
Second reason. Think of your daily energy like your phone battery.
Most of it goes to just keeping you alive. Your heart, your brain, keeping you warm.
A small part goes to digesting food.
And the rest goes to moving. Not just the gym. Walking to class, standing, fidgeting, pacing while you're on the phone.

[Scene 9 · 2:53]
That last part is sneaky.
Remember the study? When people ate more, some of them started moving more automatically. They didn't plan it. Their body just did it.
So when you eat a bit more for a few days and nothing happens, it's not that food "doesn't work" on you.
Your body is burning some of it off. You just need a little more than you think.

[Scene 10 · 3:21]
Third reason. And this is the big one.
Muscle is expensive. Your body has to spend energy to build it, and energy to keep it.
So it won't build any unless you give it a reason.

[Scene 11 · 3:38]
Think of your muscle like a building that's always being worked on.
One crew keeps building new parts. Another crew keeps tearing old parts down.
If you don't train, both crews work at the same speed. Nothing changes.
Training hard tells the builders to work faster. And food and protein are their bricks.

[Scene 12 · 4:01]
And "training hard" means sets where, by the end, you can barely do another rep.
That struggle is the message to your body: "we need more muscle."
No message, no new muscle.
Eat a ton without training, and most of it just turns into fat.

[Scene 13 · 4:21]
[RIVAL]: "Face it, bro. You're an ectomorph. You were born skinny."

[Scene 14 · 4:28]
That "ectomorph" thing comes from an old 1940s theory about body types and personality. Not from muscle science.
Genes do matter, though. In one study, five hundred eighty-five people did the exact same twelve-week arm workout.
Some barely grew. A few grew their arm muscles by more than half.
But most people were somewhere in the middle. And they grew.
You won't know which one you are until you train properly, eat enough, and give it time. Most skinny guys quit way before they find out.

== PART 2 · THE FOOD ==

[Scene 15 · 5:07]
[RIVAL]: "Easy. Dirty bulk. Eat everything!"

[Scene 16 · 5:12]
Not so fast. Your body can only build muscle at a certain speed. Anything extra just becomes fat.
So you want a small extra amount of food, every day.
A review of the research suggests gaining about a quarter to half a percent of your body weight per week.
If you weigh sixty kilos, that's around one fifty to three hundred grams a week.
Slow. Boring. Works.

[Scene 17 · 5:41]
How do you know you're eating enough?
Weigh yourself every morning. After the toilet, before breakfast.
Your weight will jump around every day. That's normal. Just look at the average for the week.
If the average hasn't gone up in two weeks, add about three hundred calories a day.
That's a glass of milk and a banana. That's it.

[Scene 18 · 6:06]
Next, protein. Protein is the bricks.
Scientists looked at forty-nine studies. Muscle gains stopped improving at about one point six grams of protein per kilo of body weight a day.
So if you weigh sixty kilos, aim for about ninety-six grams a day.

[Scene 19 · 6:25]
Sounds like a lot? It's not.
Three eggs and toast for breakfast. Rice, dal and curd for lunch.
Chicken or paneer with rotis for dinner. A handful of roasted chana as a snack.
Add the shake I'm about to show you, and you're there.

[Scene 20 · 6:51]
If you get full fast, drink your calories.
Milk, a banana, two spoons of peanut butter, some oats. Blend it.
That's about six hundred forty calories and twenty-three grams of protein.
And it doesn't feel like eating a full meal. For skinny guys, that's a cheat code.

[Scene 21 · 7:14]
Three more easy tricks.
One. Add a spoon of oil or ghee to your rice or dal. About a hundred twenty calories, and you won't even notice.
Two. Keep peanuts around. A small handful is about one seventy.
Three. Add one snack at the same time every day, like a sandwich after your workout. Habits beat motivation.

== PART 3 · THE WORKOUT ==

[Scene 22 · 7:39]
Now the workout. Just three rules.
One. Make your sets hard. Stop when you've only got one to three reps left in you.
Two. Do about ten hard sets per muscle, every week.
Three. Train each muscle two or three times a week. Not once.

[Scene 23 · 7:59]
Good news if you don't have a gym.
Light weights build muscle too, as long as the set is hard enough.
Your muscle doesn't know if it's lifting a dumbbell or your own body. It only feels how hard it's working.

[Scene 24 · 8:17]
Here are six moves for your whole body. No equipment.
Push-ups, for your chest. Keep your body straight like a plank, elbows a bit in, not flared out.
Door rows, for your back. Hold both handles of an open door, lean back, and pull your chest to the door.
Split squats, for your legs. Front knee follows your toes. Go down slow.
Pike push-ups, for your shoulders. Hips up high, head goes down between your hands.
Single-leg glute bridges. Push through your heel and pause at the top.
And towel curls for your biceps, or chin-ups if you've got a bar. Keep your elbows by your sides.
Too hard? Every move has an easier version. Too easy? There's a harder one.

[Scene 25 · 9:21]
How do you level up?
Do three sets of eight to fifteen reps.
When you can do fifteen on all three sets, the move is too easy for you now.
Switch to the harder version, or put some books in a backpack and wear it.
More reps, then a harder move. Like levelling up in a game.

[Scene 26 · 9:48]
Two small things.
Before each move, do one easy warm-up set.
And rest one to three minutes between sets. If you rush, the next set gets weak, and weak sets don't send the message.

[Scene 27 · 10:04]
And sleep.
In one study, just one night without sleep cut muscle building by eighteen percent.
Staying up till 3am scrolling isn't free. Aim for seven to nine hours.

== PART 4 · THE PLAN ==

[Scene 28 · 10:17]
Here's the whole plan. Screenshot this.
Three workouts a week: Monday, Wednesday, Friday. Six moves, three hard sets each.
Walk on the days in between.
Eat about three hundred extra calories a day, hit your protein, and drink the shake if you get full fast.
Weigh every morning, check the weekly average. If it's flat for two weeks, eat a bit more.

[Scene 29 · 10:52]
What to expect.
The first few weeks, you'll get stronger fast. That's mostly your brain learning the moves.
Getting bigger takes longer. Give it twelve weeks.
Take a photo on day one. You'll want it later.

== QUICK QUESTIONS ==

[Scene 30 · 11:11]
[RIVAL]: "Won't I just get fat?"
A little fat comes with any bulk. That's normal.
Gain slowly, and most of it goes where you want.
If your belly grows way faster than your arms and chest, eat a little less.

[Scene 31 · 11:29]
"Do I need supplements?"
No. Food and training do the real work.
If you want one, creatine is the most researched. It helps you build muscle when you train, and it's considered safe for healthy adults.
Protein powder is just food in a box. Useful, not required.
Got a health condition? Ask a doctor first.

[Scene 32 · 11:53]
"Can I still play football or do cardio?"
Yes. Sports and walking are great for you, and they won't stop you growing.
They just burn energy. So on days you play, eat a bit more.
