## kamath-we-use-ai.png
Zerodha co-founder Nithin Kamath has sparked a debate among founders with his remarks on AI in startup pitch decks, posted on X on 14 August.

He said the number of investment decks he receives that lead with "we use AI" is ridiculous, arguing that AI has become a baseline capability and no longer shows what makes a company different.

He also pointed to the questions founders should answer instead, including how they manage security risk, how they evaluate their models continuously, and how well they understand the workflow they are automating.

## bengaluru-hiring-slop.png
Bengaluru-based founder Raj Vikramaditya has drawn attention with a post on X about a recent hiring round at his company.

He said that of 35 candidates in the first round, 34 submitted work that was copied and pasted from AI tools with no effort of their own, and that the single candidate who wrote his own answer was the one hired.

He also argued that using AI is fine, but copy-pasting its output has become the fastest way to be rejected, and that the gap between people who use AI well and people who let it do the work is now visible in the first five minutes of a screening.

## benioff-per-seat.png
Product leader Aakash Gupta has sparked a discussion on software pricing after highlighting Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff's statement that per-seat SaaS is dead.

He pointed out that Salesforce earns close to $40 billion a year, almost all of it from per-user licences priced from $25 a user per month on the Starter plan to $550 a user per month for Agentforce 1 Sales, and argued that the market is still valuing the company as if humans, not AI agents, log in.

He also noted that Salesforce's AI pricing has changed several times in the past 18 months, including a retreat from per-conversation pricing after customer pushback.

## nikhil-ai-bubble.png
Zerodha co-founder Nikhil Kamath has raised questions about the economics of the AI boom in a recent episode of his podcast, recorded with people building AI companies in San Francisco.

He asked whether AI is the biggest revolution of our lifetime or the most expensive bubble ever created, pointing to estimates of around $700 billion spent against roughly $100 billion in revenue.

He also criticised the state of venture funding around AI, saying the VC bubble is getting to the point of ridiculous, while leaving open the possibility that the spending is justified if the returns arrive.

## linkedin-slop-button.png
LinkedIn has added a "Seems like AI slop" option to its post reporting menu, following months of user complaints about machine-written content in the feed.

The company said posts flagged this way will see reduced reach, similar to the effect of users marking a post as "not interested", and its chief product officer called AI slop a top priority. LinkedIn said AI-assisted writing remains allowed as long as posts represent the author's own voice.

Fortune has separately reported that accounts relying on generic AI-generated copy are seeing around 40% fewer views than before, while analyses put the share of fully AI-written long posts on the platform at roughly four in ten.
