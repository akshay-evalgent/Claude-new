# Voice guide: how Lean Stack sounds like people, not a model

Why this matters now: LinkedIn added a "Seems like AI slop" report option on 30 July 2026 and cuts reach on flagged posts. Fortune reports accounts writing with generic LLM output seeing about 40% fewer views. Posts with no concrete detail ran 13 to 23% below their author's own average through 2026. Originality.ai found human posts beat likely-AI posts by about 45% on engagement, with the biggest gap in categories where readers check your credibility. Software costs is exactly that category. Founders will test every number we post, and they will smell a template.

## Who is talking
Lean Stack has no face, so it needs a narrator. The narrator is "we": two people who run a small operation, pay for software with their own card, and try every swap before writing about it. We say what day we did it, what broke, how long it took, and what we would not do again. We have opinions and we say when we are unsure.

The page never says "we" did something we did not do. Drafts in this kit have [brackets] for the real detail. Fill them with what actually happened or cut the line.

## The five ingredients (every post has all five)
1. **One number that is not round.** $2,310, 40 minutes, 7 seats, 3 support tickets. Round numbers read as made up.
2. **One named tool and one named plan.** "HubSpot Starter, 5 seats, monthly billing", not "a popular CRM".
3. **One thing that went wrong.** The OAuth screen, the Docker volume, the import that dropped phone numbers. Nothing earns trust like a small failure.
4. **One opinion stated plainly.** "Per-seat pricing is a tax on hiring." No "it could be argued".
5. **One thing we are not sure about, or a real trade-off.** "We have not run this past 20 seats. If you have, tell us what broke."

## Rhythm
- Uneven. A six-word line, then a forty-word one. Tidy, balanced paragraphs are the loudest tell.
- One idea per line in the hook region, then let sentences run normal length in the body.
- Write the first draft as if explaining it to a friend over a call. Several of the better guides recommend recording a voice memo and cleaning the transcript. Do that for the Teardown posts.
- Read it aloud. If you would not say a sentence to a person, cut it.

## Banned (these are the tells founders screenshot)
- Scene-setters: "In today's fast-paced world", "After 15 years in this industry, I've learned", "Let that sink in".
- Pivot phrases: "Here's the thing", "Here's the kicker", "The truth is", "Let's dive in", "Spoiler".
- Negation framing: "It's not about X, it's about Y." "This isn't just a CRM, it's a mindset."
- Triplets and anaphora: "Same job. Same inbox. Same result." Three parallel fragments in a row is the single most recognisable pattern. Two is fine. Three, rewrite.
- Hype vocabulary: game-changer, unlock, supercharge, leverage, seamless, robust, delve, elevate, empower, journey, landscape, navigate, harness, revolutionise.
- Em dashes. Use a full stop or a comma.
- Rhetorical-question openers followed by the answer: "Why do most agencies overpay? Because..."
- Perfectly even bullet lists where every item is the same length.
- Fake humility and fake inspiration: "Grateful", "humbled", "this one hit different".
- Closing with a lesson: "The lesson? ...", "Takeaway:". End on the question or the detail instead.
- Emojis as bullets. One emoji in a post, maximum, and only if a person would type it.

## Allowed and encouraged
- Short asides in parentheses when a person would say them. (Yes, the Docker one again.)
- Mild irritation. "The pricing page needed three clicks to show the per-seat number. That is a choice."
- Saying "we don't know".
- Correcting ourselves in the comments publicly when a reader is right, and editing the post with a note.
- Numbers in the hook, a trade-off in the body, a two-sided question at the end.
- Crediting readers by first name and role, with permission.

## The test before publishing
Could a competitor page have posted this word for word? If yes, it is not ready. Add the thing that only happened to us.

## Carousel copy rules
- Cover: say what we did and what annoyed us, not a headline. "We rebuilt a $2,310 agency stack. It came to $47. Two of the swaps were a pain."
- Each swap slide: "you keep" and "you give up" in plain speech, one line each may be a real complaint.
- At least one screenshot slide per deck: the pricing page, the invoice, the setup screen. Screenshots are the strongest "a person did this" signal in the feed. Use the `shot` slide type.
- The numbers slide can be wrong by a dollar and say so: "give or take, depends on your plan".
- Close with the question, not a slogan.

## Comment-reply voice
Reply like a person who read the comment. Quote the specific thing they said, answer it, ask one thing back. Never "Great point!" Never "Thanks for sharing!" If they are right and we were wrong, say "you're right, fixing it" and fix it.
