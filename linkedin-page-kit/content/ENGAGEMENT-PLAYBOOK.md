# Engagement playbook: how Lean Stack posts so people like, comment, and tell us what to cover

Built from 2026 studies (AuthoredUp 372k posts, MagicPost 687k and 1.2M posts, Ligo 134k posts, Buffer 4.8M posts, Socialinsider, van der Blom 1.3M posts). All are vendor datasets with conflicting exact numbers; the directions agree. Validate against the page's own analytics after 30 posts.

## 1. What the feed rewards now
- **Comments outweigh likes** by anywhere from 2x to 15x depending on the study. Long comments (15+ words) and reply threads count more than one-word comments.
- **Dwell time** is a ranking signal. Long carousels and long, well-broken text keep people on the post.
- **First 60 to 90 minutes** decide distribution. Roughly 10 real interactions in that window signal a wider push.
- **Topic relevance beats follower count.** Stay on "software cost, swaps, free and cheap tools for business" in every post and every page comment.
- **Comment-keyword bait is penalised.** "Comment SWAP and I'll send it" posts dropped about 47% in reach from 27 July 2026. Genuine closing questions did not drop. Debatable stances got about 14% more comments.
- **Company page polls** still reach about 1.24x a normal post's median, lower than personal-profile polls but above average for a page. One a week, with three lines of context.

## 2. Post anatomy (text + document or image)
1. **Hook, under 210 characters, number first.** This is all anyone sees before "see more" on desktop; about 140 on mobile. Example: "Intercom: $390/mo. Chatwoot: $0. Same inbox, same WhatsApp, same AI answers."
2. **Second line creates the gap.** "Here is what you give up, honestly."
3. **Body, 1,300 to 2,000 characters, one sentence per line, 12 to 16 short paragraphs.** Dense posts with fewer than three line breaks underperform badly.
4. **One concrete trade-off.** "You give up product tours and a hosted SLA." Trade-offs are what make people trust the saving and argue in the comments.
5. **Close with a genuine two-sided question**, never a keyword ask. Good: "Would you run support on a $6 VPS, or is hosted uptime worth $390 a month to you?" Bad: "Comment YES for the template."
6. **No links in the body.** First comment, posted within one minute: repo links, pricing page links, the newsletter link, and a second question.
7. **Three hashtags max**, rotated from the page copy list.

## 3. The comment engine (what the page does after posting)
- **Minute 0:** post. Minute 1: first comment with links and a follow-up question. Pin it.
- **Minutes 1 to 90:** reply to every comment within 15 minutes, 15+ words each, always ending in a question back ("Which plan are you on? The maths changes above 10 seats."). Replies count as comments and keep the thread alive.
- **Disagreement is fuel.** When someone defends the expensive tool, thank them, ask for the one feature they would miss most, and promise to test it. Never argue; invite evidence.
- **Pin the best reader comment** after two hours, not the page's own.
- **Tag two or three people** per post who can speak to the swap: the maintainer, a user who posted about it, the paid tool's community lead. Tags that make sense bring their audiences; random tags get ignored.
- **Comment as the page 15 to 20 times a day** under posts about software costs, SaaS pricing, bootstrapping and AI tools, within 15 minutes of publication, each with a specific cheaper alternative. This is the page's only outbound reach.

## 4. Input loops (so readers tell us what to make)
Weekly rhythm, every week, so the audience learns when to show up:
- **Monday, swap card.** Single image, one swap, question: "Would you make this swap?"
- **Tuesday, The Teardown.** Serif editorial carousel, one real business bill rebuilt. Ends with: "Send us a bill. Anonymised. We tear it down."
- **Wednesday, poll.** "Which bill should we tear down next?" Three named options plus "Your bill, name it below." Three lines of context explaining what happens to the winner.
- **Thursday, debate card.** A defensible contrarian position about software cost. "Per-seat pricing is a tax on growth." Two sides stated, readers pick.
- **Friday, reader swap.** A swap a reader suggested in comments, credited by first name and role with permission. People comment to be featured.
- **Saturday, newsletter.** The Swap List, five swaps from the week plus the winning poll.
- **Monthly, scoreboard.** "Readers reported saving $X this month." Social proof, invites more submissions.

Other input mechanisms:
- **Teardown request form** (Tally, free tier) linked in Featured and in every Teardown first comment: tool list, seats, monthly cost, what they cannot live without.
- **Submission credit rule.** Every reader-sourced swap is credited. Being named is the reward, not a free PDF.
- **"Defend your tool" series.** Ask fans of an expensive tool to make the case. Their fans show up to agree. Strong comment volume with zero bait.

## 5. Timing (IST, for an India plus US audience)
- **Slot 1: 8:30 to 9:00 am IST**, Tuesday to Thursday strongest. Catches India and Europe mornings.
- **Slot 2: 8:30 to 9:30 pm IST**, which is the US East Coast late morning, the window most studies rank first for company pages. (Buffer's data favours US mid-afternoon, which is after midnight IST; skip it.)
- Weekdays beat weekends by roughly 48%. Post the newsletter Saturday morning anyway; it is a notification, not a feed post.
- After 30 posts, read the page analytics and move slots to where your own followers are online.

## 6. Formats, ranked for this page
1. Document carousel, 10 to 15 slides (comparison engine). Highest reach and saves.
2. Single swap card with a 1,500-character text body. Fast to make, strong comments.
3. Poll with context. Reach plus direct input.
4. Text-only debate post with the debate card image. Highest comment ratio.
5. Short native video (45 to 90 seconds screen recording of a setup) once a week when possible. Favoured in ranking, but production cost is higher.

## 7. Do not
- No comment-keyword gating. No "DM me", since the page cannot DM anyway.
- No engagement pods, no bought followers, no reposting the same carousel twice in a week.
- No unverified prices. Every number has a pricing-page screenshot in the working folder with the date.
- No claims about tools we have not run. The audience will test it and say so.
- No drifting into general AI news. The topic fingerprint is the asset.

## 8. Weekly review (15 minutes, Sunday)
Log for every post: impressions, saves, comments, reshares, follows attributed. Keep three numbers on a wall: saves per 1,000 impressions (target 20), comments per post (target 15 by day 30), new followers per week. Double the format that wins saves, cut the format that wins only likes.
