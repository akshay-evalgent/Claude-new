**Settings**
- Audience: choose honestly. "Made for kids" switches comments off, which removes the pinned comment and replies.
- Altered or synthetic content: No. It's clearly animation and the narrator isn't a real person's voice.
- No `#shorts` needed: YouTube files vertical videos under 3 minutes as Shorts automatically, and Zack uses no hashtags.
- Keep Zack's name out of the title and tags. Borrowing another channel's name for traffic counts as misleading metadata under YouTube's spam policy.
- First hour: reply to and heart early comments, so the thread keeps growing.

## What Zack D. Films actually does (checked 23 Nov 2026)

Sources: their public Shorts tab (latest 48 titles with view counts), their RSS feed (latest 15 uploads
with descriptions) and 24 of their thumbnails from YouTube's image server.

**Titles**
- Title Case, 4 to 8 words (about 25 to 45 characters).
- Exactly one emoji at the end.
- No hashtags, no "#shorts", and almost no punctuation.
- Formats: "How X Works 🤔", "How To X 🤔", "Can/Could X 🤔", or a story headline
  ("She Was Trapped Underground For 3 Days 😵"). Made-up stories carry "(Fiction)".
- The emoji matches the tone:

| Emoji | Tone | Example |

| 🤔 | how / could / can | Can A Fired Bullet Beat A Dropped Bullet 🤔 (13M) |
| 😨 | danger | Woman Hangs Upside Down Seven Floors Up 😨 (15M) |
| 😮 | surprising | Cutting A Watermelon With Dental Floss 😮 (17M) |
| 🤯 | mind-blowing | Dumping Yellowstone's Magma On NYC 🤯 (5.8M) |

**Descriptions:** blank on all 15 uploads in the feed (20 to 23 Nov 2026).

**Thumbnails:** always a frame of the video itself, showing the burned-in caption that's on screen at that
moment ("She jumped", "The lid shut", "If you dropped"). There is never added title text, arrows, circles or
zoomed faces. 2 of the 12 Shorts I checked had a chosen thumbnail, and both were just a video frame with its caption.

**Captions:** in every video, 2 to 3 words at a time, white heavy sans-serif, thin dark outline and soft shadow,
centred about 78% of the way down the frame. Lowercase mid-sentence ("was waiting").

**Opening frame:** the frames on their Shorts tab look like each video's opening frame (the captions
are all sentence openings). Each shows a big subject in the
middle of an action, with the first caption already on screen. Our opener is a wide shot with a small figure.

**Volume:** 15 uploads in 4 days, about 4 a day.

**Not verified:** their tags and pinned comments. YouTube's individual video pages returned a bot check,
which I didn't try to get around. The comment above is written to invite replies (an easy yes/no question
