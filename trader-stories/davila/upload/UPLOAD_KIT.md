## Description

```
Santiago, January 1994. The head of futures trading at Codelco, the world's biggest copper company, cuts short his beach holiday to tell his boss he made a mistake. Months earlier, he says, he entered a sale as a purchase. Buy instead of sell. It cost thirty million dollars, and instead of reporting it, he tried to win it back.

By the time Codelco closed his positions, the loss was about $206 million, 44% of the company's profit for the year. Chile called it Coppergate, and gave the language a new verb: davilar, "to botch things up miserably". But the judge who investigated never believed it was just a slip of the finger.

This is the true story of Juan Pablo Dávila: one man, one spreadsheet, and a committee that stopped meeting.
```

## Before you publish

1. **Watch it once at full length** and listen to the Spanish names (Juan Pablo Dávila, Codelco, Guerrini, Reñaca, Benquis, davilar). Fixes go in `pronunciations.json`.
2. **Open facts** (`RESEARCH.md`): the loss is given as $206m (Codelco, February 1994); later figures range from $170m to $207m. The total prison term is reported differently, so the film gives the Supreme Court's sentences.
3. **Living person**: Dávila is alive. The film states his account, Codelco's claims (attributed) and the court's verdicts separately; keep it that way in titles and comments.
4. **Synthetic voice and monetisation**: as with the earlier films, a human narrator can replace `build/audio/voice.wav` without re-editing.
