# Format test: 5 formats × (1 long + 1 short)

Status: ideas for approval. Nothing built yet. Facts are checked again during research, as with every film.

## Targets

YouTube does not report "stayed to watch" for long videos; it reports it for Shorts as **viewed vs swiped away**. So each target is set on the metric YouTube actually shows.

| Goal | Long video (8–12 min) | Short (30–50 s, vertical) |
|---|---|---|
| Hook holds | **75%+ still watching at 0:30** | **75%+ viewed (vs swiped away)** |
| Retention / average view duration | **50%+ average percentage viewed**; no drop steeper than the channel average after 1:00 | **100%+ average percentage viewed** (means people loop it) |
| Repeat / loop | Returning viewers ≥ 20% after 28 days | Loops: script ends mid-sentence into its own first line |
| Share | Shares per 1,000 views ≥ 5 | Shares per 1,000 views ≥ 10 |

A 75% average view duration on a 10-minute video is very rare, even for top channels; 50% is excellent. 75% is the target for the first 30 seconds of a long video, and for the swipe rate on a Short.

## Rules every script follows

**Hook (first 5–8 seconds):**
- Open by breaking something the viewer believes is normal.
- Then make a promise that only pays off at the end.
- No logo, no "in this video".

**Long videos:**
- A new question every 60–90 seconds (re-hook).
- A picture or graphic change every 4–8 seconds.
- The answer to the opening question is held until the last 90 seconds.
- Ends with one sentence worth repeating to a friend.

**Shorts:**
- One idea only, with captions burned in.
- No intro and no outro.
- The final line runs straight into the first line, so the loop is seamless.
- One surprising number people want to send to someone.

## 1. Clever-hustle true stories

**Long: "The Lottery That Lost Money to a Shopkeeper"**
- **Hook:** "The lottery is a tax on people who can't do maths. In 2003, a retired shop owner in Michigan did the maths, and found a lottery that was losing money to him."
- **Questions the normal:** everyone "knows" the lottery always wins.
- **Story:**
  - Jerry and Marge Selbee spotted the "roll-down" rule in Michigan's Winfall game: when the jackpot passed $5M with no winner, the prize money flowed to smaller prizes. On those draws a ticket was worth more than it cost.
  - They set up a company, bought tickets by the hundred thousand, moved to Massachusetts' Cash WinFall when Michigan closed its game, and ran into MIT students doing the same thing.
  - Reported gross winnings were about $26M over nine years.
- **Ending:** the lottery's own maths, and why it was legal.
- **Share line:** "He didn't beat the lottery. The lottery was beating itself."

**Short: "When a lottery ticket is worth more than it costs"**
- **Hook:** "There was a lottery where buying tickets made you money."
- One roll-down diagram, one number.
- **Loop:** the last line "…so he bought another—" cuts back to the first frame.

## 2. Cartoon "Explained Like You're 5"

**Long: "The GameStop Squeeze Explained Like You're 5"**
- **Hook:** "Some of the smartest people on Wall Street bet that a dying video-game shop would go to zero. A man in a red headband bet $53,000 that they were wrong."
- **Questions the normal:** the little guy always loses to hedge funds.
- **Story:**
  - Short selling explained with cartoon characters.
  - Short interest above 100% of the shares available.
  - Reddit piles in. GameStop goes from about $17 at the start of January 2021 to $483 on 28 January.
  - Melvin Capital needs $2.75 billion from other funds.
  - Brokers block buying.
- **Ending:** who actually made money.

**Short: "How you can lose more than 100%"**
- **Hook:** "You think the most you can lose is what you put in. Not if you short."
- Buy at $10: you can lose $10. Short at $10 and it goes to $480: you owe 48 times your bet.
- **Loop:** ends on "…which is why the most you can lose—" and returns to the start.

## 3. "If You Don't Understand X, You Don't Understand Money"

**Long: "If You Don't Understand How Banks Create Money, You Don't Understand Money"**
- **Hook:** "When a bank gives someone a loan, it doesn't lend out your savings. It types new money into existence. That isn't a conspiracy theory. It's what the Bank of England says."
- **Questions the normal:** banks lend out deposits.
- **Source:** Bank of England, "Money creation in the modern economy" (2014).
- **Structure:** deposit, loan, new money; what limits it; why this drives booms and busts; what it means for your savings.
- **Visuals:** mostly charts and diagrams (cheapest film of the ten).

**Short: "A 1% fee doesn't take 1% of your money"**
- **Hook:** "A 1% fee sounds tiny. Over 40 years it takes almost a third of your money."
- **The maths:**
  - At 7% a year for 40 years, a 1% fee leaves you about 69% of the pot, so it takes about 31%.
  - A 2% fee takes about half.
- **Loop:** the counter resets to the opening number.

## 4. "Explained With One Lemonade Stand" (our running object)

**Long: "The Stock Market Explained With One Lemonade Stand"**
- **Hook:** "When you buy a share of Apple, Apple doesn't get a cent of your money. So who does?"
- **Questions the normal:** buying stock funds the company.
- **One lemonade stand grows step by step:**
  1. Selling shares to friends
  2. The company "goes public"
  3. Strangers trading those shares
  4. Dividends
  5. A price bubble
  6. A short seller
  7. A crash
- **Ending:** the answer to the opening question. The company only gets the money once, when it first sells the shares; after that you buy from other investors.
- **Reuse:** the same characters and stand return in every later episode.

**Short: "Why prices are never supposed to go back down"**
- **Hook:** "Central banks want your lemonade to cost more every year. On purpose."
- The 2% inflation target, and why falling prices scare them more.
- **Loop:** the price tag ticks up and the video restarts.

## 5. "Your Life at Every Level of…"

**Long: "Your Life at Every Level of a Trader: $1,000 to $1 Billion"**
- **Hook:** "At level one, you trade fifty times a day. At level ten, you might trade twice a year. And the person trading fifty times a day is almost always losing."
- **Questions the normal:** more trading means more money.
- **Ten levels:**
  1. Phone app
  2. First prop-firm account
  3. Full-time desk
  4. Bank trader
  5. Fund manager
  6. …up to a billion-dollar fund
- **Each level:** your day, your money, and the risk that ends you.
- **Real data:** the Brazilian study of day traders (Chague, De-Losso and Giovannetti, 2019): 97% of those who kept going for 300+ days lost money.
- **Fit:** ties straight back to our trader-story films.

**Short: "Every level of trader in 45 seconds"**
- **Hook:** "Every level of trader, from $1,000 to $1 billion."
- Quick cuts up the ladder. The final level says "…and it all started with one trade on a phone—", which loops back to level one.
