# Script study: "Day Trading Legend: BNF - The God of Japanese Day Trading"

Reference: Billionaire Bears, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jnc77gMihPo
(published Feb 2023, 26:57, about 4.6M views when studied in Nov 2026).

Studied from the full English caption track and the video's storyboard
thumbnails (one frame every ~10 s). The video file itself could not be
downloaded, so notes on motion and sound are inferred, not measured.

Three things follow from this. The narrator talks fast but stops often, so the
film feels quick without feeling crowded. Sentences are short enough to be cut
to. And nearly a third of the film is the picture and music carrying the story
alone.

The shape is: peak, origin, rise, twist, big win, fall, last test, win. Money
only goes down twice, and both times late in the film.

## The techniques worth taking

1. **Open with a refusal.** The first thing we learn about the hero is what he
   turns down. That sets character before any biography, and it raises the
   question the rest of the film answers: who says no to that?

2. **A scoreboard runs through the whole film.** $16K, $1M, $10M, ¥2B, minus
   $6.5M, $64.8M at risk, plus $12M. Every act ends on a number, and the
   storyboard shows a small on-screen readout of year, age and account size.

3. **Paired hinge lines.** At 2:55: "a blessing happened to BNF that turned his
   whole worldview bright". At 10:35: "a tragedy happened to BNF that turned his
   whole worldview dark". Same sentence, one word changed. The viewer feels the
   structure without being told.

4. **The twist is an inversion.** The tragedy is that the market went up.
   "You heard that right." It works because the first act taught us his method
   only works on fear.

5. **The method is told in his words, with numbers.** Stocks 20 % below the
   25-day moving average, 35 % as a safer entry, different ranges per sector.
   The trader in the audience gets something to take away, and the quotes make
   the film feel sourced.

6. **One mechanism per act, in under 40 s.** Japan's lost decade. Limit up and
   limit down. Why an export economy got hit in 2008. Each is explained only
   when the story needs it.

7. **Everyone else does the obvious thing.** His forum friends buy the crash;
   he waits. His protege cashes out; he holds overnight. The institutions hand
   their shares back; he refuses. "While most traders would have gone on tilt,
   BNF remained calm." The hero is drawn by contrast every time.

8. **Set pieces switch to clock time.** "It's December 2005, 9 a.m." Sentences
   drop to three to six words: "BNF moved quick." "Everybody wanted a piece."
   "But BNF didn't."

9. **Cut to the other side.** "Meanwhile, at Mizuho Securities, a man was
   fearing for his life." Thirty seconds in the opponent's chair makes the
   trade a duel.

10. **A question before each silence.** "Would he lose half of his account
    size? Or make an ungodly fortune?" Then music. The unnarrated stretches sit
    right after a question, which is why they hold attention.

11. **Props stand in for personality.** Cup ramen, anime and the small room
    come back at 1:35, 3:20, 22:40 and the last frame. When anime "no longer
    worked" to calm him, we know things are bad.

12. **The narrator talks to the viewer.** "Like a lot of you." "He's not like
    you, he didn't have YouTube." "Are you kidding me?" A dry aside every two
    minutes or so keeps it from sounding like a textbook.

13. **The name is a drumbeat.** Initials, used 68 times, with titles that
    grow: "the J-Com Man", "the great bear", "a god amongst traders".

14. **The theme arrives late and once.** 24:05: his greatness "didn't come from
    his strategies. It came from his ability to tune out the noise." It is the
    answer to the opening refusal.

## What not to copy

- **Loose facts.** The opening meeting with Masayoshi Son is acted out as if
  it happened, with invented dialogue and no label. Several figures are stated
  with more confidence than the public record supports (the Lehman position,
  "the Nikkei had fallen below $7,000"). A channel built on true stories needs
  the opposite habit.
- **Borrowed footage.** The picture is mostly re-voiced scenes from a Korean
  TV drama. That is a copyright and monetisation risk, and it is the gap this
  format fills with original illustration.
- **The 2008 macro detour** (20:35-21:15) is the one place the story stops
  for a lecture.
- **Small language slips** ("identify meaning", "would have went") that a
  second read would catch.
