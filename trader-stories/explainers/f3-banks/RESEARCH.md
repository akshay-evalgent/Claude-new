# How banks create money: sources and checked facts

| Claim in the film | Source | Exact wording / note |
|---|---|---|
| Most money is created by commercial banks making loans | Bank of England, *Money creation in the modern economy*, Quarterly Bulletin 2014 Q1 (McLeay, Radia, Thomas), 14 March 2014 | "the majority of money in the modern economy is created by commercial banks making loans" |
| The textbook story is not how it works | same | "Rather than banks receiving deposits when households save and then lending them out, bank lending creates deposits." Also: banks "do not act simply as intermediaries, lending out deposits that savers place with them" |
| Money multiplier is inaccurate | same | the money multiplier theory "is not an accurate description of how money is created in reality" |
| A loan creates a matching deposit | same | "Whenever a bank makes a loan, it simultaneously creates a matching deposit in the borrower's bank account, thereby creating new money." |
| Repaying loans destroys money | same | "Just as taking out a new loan creates money, the repayment of bank loans destroys money." |
| Deposits 97% of broad money | same | "Of the two types of broad money, bank deposits make up the vast majority — 97% of the amount currently in circulation." (UK, 2014) Film says "when the Bank of England wrote its paper" |
| Three main limits | same | "three main sets of constraints": (i) banks themselves (must remain profitable in a competitive system; prudential regulation), (ii) households and companies (repaying debt destroys money), (iii) "The ultimate constraint on money creation is monetary policy." |
| UK has no reserve requirements | same | "As there are no reserve requirements in the United Kingdom..." |
| Lower lending after 2008 slowed money growth | same, footnote | "The fall in bank lending in the United Kingdom since 2008 is an important reason why the growth of money..." (has been lower than before the crisis) |
| US reserve requirement 0% since March 2020 | Federal Reserve, Reserve Requirements page | "the Board reduced reserve requirement ratios to zero percent effective March 26, 2020." |
| Northern Rock: first UK bank run in 150 years (Sept 2007) | Wikipedia, Northern Rock | "a bank run – the UK's first in 150 years". Run 14 Sept 2007; nationalised Feb 2008 (film does not say it failed from the run) |
| SVB lost over $40bn on March 9, 2023; expected $100bn+ next day; closed March 10 | Federal Reserve, Review of the Supervision and Regulation of Silicon Valley Bank (April 2023) | "On March 9, SVB lost over $40 billion in deposits, and SVBFG management expected to lose over $100 billion more on March 10." California DFPI took possession on March 10 |
| FDIC $250,000 | FDIC, Understanding Deposit Insurance | "$250,000 per depositor, per FDIC-insured bank, for each account ownership category." |
| 17th-century London goldsmith bankers issued receipts/notes beyond the gold held | General banking history (e.g. Bank of England Museum material on goldsmith bankers) | Presented as the origin story, in general terms; no figures given |

Not used: the often-quoted "$42 billion" SVB figure (the Fed review says "over $40 billion").
