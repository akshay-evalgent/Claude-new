# Templates, courses and ebooks: zero-cost distribution playbook

Researched 11 November 2026. For a solo creator with no budget and no audience, with attention to creators based in India. Companion to `PLAN.md` and the other playbooks in this folder.

## How far to trust this

- No reliable dataset exists on what an unknown template seller earns. Etsy and Gumroad figures are third-party snapshots or models.
- First-person case studies from 2024–2026 are thin for all three products, and thinner still from India.
- No source documents a zero-audience, zero-spend route to course income end to end.
- No 2025–26 first-person account was found of a zero-audience non-fiction ebook with full numbers.
- Many official pages could not be opened (Etsy, Udemy, Skillshare, Canva, Gumroad help), so those items rest on search snippets.
- Author and creator surveys skew to people who take the work seriously.
- The 30-day plans are assembled from the cases. No source tested them as sequences.
- Items marked "unconfirmed" need a direct check before anyone relies on them.

## 1. The short answer

| Product | Fastest documented first sale | Realistic first month |
|---|---|---|
| Templates | A Framer template through Framer's own marketplace: 10 days from publish in one case | $0–200 in the first 60 days |
| Courses | A small paid live workshop sold by direct outreach, with a free live lesson as the lead source | 3–15 paid seats |
| Ebooks | A narrow, experience-based short guide sold direct, plus Amazon free days for reach | Single-digit to low-double-digit sales |

One pattern runs across all three: the marketplace's own search is the only free source of buyers that works without an audience, and it pays little. Direct sales pay more per sale but need you to find each buyer.

### Channels, ranked by evidence

1. Search inside a marketplace. The only channel with repeated no-audience first sales.
2. A free version on the tool's own gallery, linking to the paid one.
3. X posts showing the build, for Framer and developer products.
4. Niche communities and Notion directory sites.
5. Email list. Needs a list first.
6. YouTube. Largest outcomes, but audience-dependent.
7. Pinterest. Recommended everywhere; no hard numbers found.
8. TikTok and Reels. No verified 2025–26 case found.

- No template-specific data exists on free-to-paid conversion.
- Copied listings: Etsy removes on notice, but the copier can counter-claim.

### What changed

- Generic Notion planners are saturated. Profession-specific ones (therapists, real estate, trades, nursing students) show thin supply.
- AI features have cut income for icon and vector sellers.
- Etsy tightened its AI rules in October 2026.

### 30 days

| Days | Action |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Pick one tool and one profession niche where top listings have under about 60 reviews. Sort out payout. |
| 4–10 | Build one free and one paid version. Submit the free one to the tool's gallery. |
| 11–17 | List the paid version with a preview image and demo video. On Etsy, add 5–10 variants. |
| 18–30 | Post the build daily on X or in the tool's community. Start a second template. |

### Channels, ranked by evidence

1. Direct outreach and one-to-one demos.
2. Free live lessons. Each share brings 10–20 signups for a newcomer; 5–10% of attendees buy.
3. Udemy's own search. Slow and small, but works from zero. Cross-selling began around the third course in one case.
4. Udemy coupon links for the 97% share.
5. SEO and free templates. Strong in one case after about two years.
6. YouTube tutorials. No verified course-sales report from a channel under 5,000 subscribers.
7. Guest teaching in someone else's course.
8. LinkedIn, Instagram, X, Reddit. No measured course-sales data found.

### Format and pricing

- Scheduled cohorts complete at 53% against 42% for self-paced. Cohorts of 11–25 reach 65%.
- Students active in week one complete at 78% against 17%.
- Median one-time course price is $150; the middle half is $49–357.
- India: self-paced ₹1,500–4,999; cohorts ₹15,000–50,000; live workshops ₹99–998.
- About 40% of webinar registrants attend.
- Maven advises about 250 emails before a first paid product.
- Every case with early revenue started with a live paid session or client training, not a recording.

### What changed

- Udemy's subscription pool share fell to 15%, and total payouts dropped about 12% in 2025. Coursera now owns Udemy.
- Skillshare added the 50-follower gate.
- Teachable, Thinkific and Podia have no free plans.
- Udemy requires AI disclosure and reportedly rejects fully AI-generated courses.

### 30 days

| Days | Action |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Pick one narrow outcome. Write a one-page offer. Set up Topmate or Systeme.io and an email list. |
| 4–10 | Announce a free 45-minute live lesson. Share it about 10 times and message 50–100 people. Aim for 100+ signups. |
| 11–14 | Run it. Pitch a paid 2-hour workshop (₹199–999) with a 48-hour code. |
| 15–21 | Offer calls to non-buyers. Deliver the workshop and record it. Collect testimonials. |
| 22–30 | Publish the recording on Udemy. Post 3–5 clips on YouTube. Pre-sell a 2–4 week cohort of 11–25 to attendees. |

### Channels, ranked by evidence

1. Amazon's own search, categories and free list. 68% of surveyed readers discover books on Amazon. Free days bring roughly 50–450 downloads for an unknown book.
2. An email list with a free sample. The income gap between authors with and without a list is about twentyfold.
3. Topic communities and writing in public, for non-fiction. Reddit's r/books bans promotion; r/selfpublish allows a weekly thread.
4. Free listing sites. Small reach.
5. Early reviewers. Free copies are allowed if no review is required.
6. Social media. No free-channel sales data found for BookTok, YouTube, LinkedIn or X.
7. Libraries. Now behind Draft2Digital's fee.

AI tools account for under 1% of book discovery.

### Pricing

| US price | Author receives |
|---|---|
| $0.99 | About $0.35 |
| $2.99 | About $2.05 |
| $9.99 | About $6.90 |
| $12.99 | About $8.88 |

- Most surveyed readers spend under $5 on an ebook.
- Topmate suggests ₹49–299 for an entry product and a ₹3,000+ product for revenue.
- In the one direct-sale case at $19–26, 35% of buyers paid more than the minimum.

### What changed

- Amazon raised the 70% ceiling to $12.99 in July 2026.
- Kindle Unlimited was renamed Kindle+, and AI audiobooks now share the page fund.
- How-to demand is falling: self-help print units fell 26% in early 2026. Tim Ferriss reports his backlist down 46% in 2025 and blames AI.
- About three-quarters of readers in one survey say they would not buy an AI-written book.
- Still selling for unknown authors: series genre fiction in Kindle+; narrow technical non-fiction from experience; in India, career, exam and AI-prompt guides.

### Best route

| Type | Route |
|---|---|
| Non-fiction guide | Short, experience-based guide sold direct (Leanpub or Gumroad abroad; Topmate or Payhip in India), validated by writing it in public; then KDP at $2.99–4.99 for search |
| Fiction | KDP Select with a series in mind, free days and niche reader groups; or serialise on Royal Road or Pratilipi first |

Avoid generic how-to that a chatbot can answer.

### 30 days

| Days | Action |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | Pick one problem you have solved. Post the outline as a question in one or two communities. Set up a list with a sample chapter. |
| 6–15 | Write a 15–40 page version, posting excerpts. Open an early-draft sale on Leanpub or Topmate. Complete Amazon's tax interview. |
| 16–20 | Publish on KDP, enrol in Select, choose keywords and categories from competing titles. |
| 21–25 | Run 2–3 free days. Submit to free listing sites. Give early readers free copies with no review asked. |
| 26–30 | Return to paid. Email the list. Review search terms and reviews. |
