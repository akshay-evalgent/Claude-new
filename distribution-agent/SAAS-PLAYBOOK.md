# SaaS and micro-SaaS: zero-cost distribution playbook

Researched 11 November 2026. For a solo maker with a working product, no budget and no audience, with attention to makers based in India. Companion to `PLAN.md` and `AI-AGENTS-PLAYBOOK.md`.

## How far to trust this

- Almost all first-person data comes from makers who succeeded and wrote about it. Real odds are worse than the case studies suggest.
- Most "2026 benchmarks" online are vendor blogs. Figures from them are marked as estimates.
- Indie Hackers pages and several official pricing pages could not be opened, so those items rest on search snippets.
- The main channel dataset (70 solo founders, 59 naming a channel) is built from public success posts and leans towards tools sold to founders, which inflates Reddit.
- Revenue base rates come from TrustMRR, where makers opt in by connecting a payment provider. That understates failure.
- India payout is unconfirmed for Freemius, Wix, Atlassian and Freshworks. Check before building for those stores.
- Stage timings and 90-day expectations are synthesis, not measured benchmarks.

## 1. The short answer

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Where does the first customer come from? | Other people's communities (34%), then an existing audience (20%), then one-to-one outreach (15%) |
| What does not produce it? | Paid ads (0 of 59), Product Hunt without preparation, bulk cold email, paid directory packages |
| What is the fastest passive route from India? | A marketplace that bills for you: Shopify first |
| How long does it take? | About 5 months to a first dollar and 10 months to $1,000 a month on TrustMRR data |
| What kills products after the first sale? | Early churn, more often than lack of traffic |
| What price? | $29 a month or more for business buyers, with an annual option from day one |

- Among revenue-earning indie products, the median is $145 a month.

- Over a third of products above $5,000 a month shrank in the last 30 days. Plateaus are normal.

Being found inside a store:

- **Shopify:** keywords in the name and subtitle, recent reviews, low uninstall rate. Shopify says it boosts small, trending, high-quality apps. The shelf is crowded: 31,075 live apps, 3,102 launched in October 2026.
- **WordPress.org:** exact-match title, recent updates, active installs, support threads resolved.
- **Google Workspace:** ranking for the name of the product you integrate with drove one maker's installs.

### Directories, launch sites and review sites

| Place | Free? | What it sends |
|---|---|---|
| Peerlist Launchpad | Yes, weekly | One launch: 130 upvotes, 90 visits |
| Uneed | Yes, queue of 50+ days | One founder saw no referred visits on launch morning |
| Fazier, Microlaunch, DevHunt, BetaList | Yes, queues of weeks to 6 months | No first-person numbers |

No first-person report of revenue from any launch site or directory turned up. Their value is presence in search and AI answers.

Deals and exits:

- **AppSumo:** one maker grossed $144,000, kept 30%, had 21.7% refunded, and received $34,900 about 60 days later. Terms bar lifetime deals elsewhere for 12 months afterwards.
- **Selling the business:** Microns has no listing fee and takes 6–10% at sale. Acquire.com now charges $25 a month plus 8%. Asking prices for small products run about 1.7 times annual profit; closed prices are likely lower.

## 5. Organic channels

| Channel | Evidence | 90-day expectation |
|---|---|---|
| Human replies where buyers ask for tools | 34% of first customers | 1–10 paying customers if the niche is active; real risk of removal |
| Hand-picked outreach, under 50 at a time | 14–58% replies | 100–300 messages, 15–60 conversations, a handful of customers |
| Personal network | 12 of 23 fast-growing B2B companies | 2–5 early customers if any network exists |
| One free tool plus 5–10 comparison, integration and use-case pages | Correlational only | Tens to low hundreds of visits a month; 0–5 signups; pays off in months 4–12 |
| Third-party mentions for AI answers | Correlations and single-brand tests | Named for narrow prompts in some cases; a few high-intent signups |
| Product loop from day one | Tally's badge brought 10% of new users | Near zero until there are users |

SEO in 2026:

- Only 1.74% of new pages reach the top 10 within a year, in Ahrefs' reported 2025 figure.
- Vendors say 3–6 months to a first trickle of signups. They sell SEO, so read that as optimistic.
- Google ran four spam updates in 2026 against one in 2025.
- Self-promotional "best X" lists dropped in January and did not recover. In AI Overviews, the publishing brand was left out of its own list 83% of the time by September.
- Clicks on the top result fall about 58% where an AI Overview appears. This mostly affects informational queries, so comparison and pricing pages are less exposed.
- One founder generated 200+ comparison pages for terms nobody searched.

AI answer engines:

- Visitors from AI assistants signed up at 1.66% against 0.15% from search (Microsoft Clarity, 1,200 sites).
- Being named correlates most with YouTube mentions (0.74) and branded web mentions (about 0.7), and least with site size. The study covers established brands only.
- In Ahrefs' made-up-brand test, detailed third-party posts beat the official site in most assistants. ChatGPT used the official FAQ in 84% of answers. Claude said the brand did not exist.
- Review-site profiles look like a threshold: nearly all tools ChatGPT named had Capterra and G2 reviews, but review count barely tracked rank.
- Free measurement: Bing's AI Performance report, Search Console's AI view, a "how did you hear about us" field, and running 10–20 buyer prompts by hand each month.

Commonly repeated, no numbers found:

- Conversion from comparison, integration or template pages.
- Conversion from calculators and graders by a solo maker.
- LinkedIn founder content, newsletters, changelogs.
- Job-posting and review-site signals as outreach triggers.
- The one documented free-tool failure: Preceden's calculators out-drew the whole site and converted so poorly they were moved to a separate domain.

Channel fit by product type (inference from the evidence above):

| Product | Best-supported channels |
|---|---|
| Business tool, higher price | Personal network, hand-picked outreach, LinkedIn messages |
| Business tool, low price, self-serve | Community recommendation threads, free tool plus comparison pages, AI-answer mentions |
| Developer tool | Reddit, Hacker News, niche Discord, documentation, marketplaces |
| Tool for one trade | Niche forums and Facebook groups, direct outreach, listing in that trade's main platform |
| Shareable output | "Powered by" link, templates, AI answers |
| Consumer | Go where users are, invites, search-led video |

Outreach rules:

- **United States:** no prior consent needed, but a postal address and a working opt-out are required.
- **Germany and Austria:** opt-in required even for business recipients.
- **India:** the data-protection act has no general legitimate-interest ground; its main obligations start 13 May 2027. No source addressed cold email directly.
- **LinkedIn:** publishes no weekly limit; about 100 a week is vendor consensus. Restrictions follow many ignored requests or suspected automation.

## 6. Validation: which signals predict payment

| Rank | Signal | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Money before or at delivery | Never produced a false positive in the cases read |
| 2 | Existing spend or marketplace search for the thing | Buyers arrived where demand already sat |
| 3 | People stating the problem in public who reply to a message | 26 messages, 6 customers |
| 4 | Customer interviews | Good for killing ideas, weak for confirming them |
| 5 | Signups, waitlists, upvotes, traffic | Did not predict payment: 592 signups gave 6 payers; 900 gave none |
| 6 | An AI rating the idea well | One founder built four apps on it; "the signals were fake" |

## 7. Niche and positioning

- **Business buyers and higher prices retain better.** Median net revenue retention is 82% for business SaaS and 48–49% for consumer and AI-native products.
- **Categories most often above $5,000 a month:** mobile apps 8%, e-commerce 6%, marketing 4.9%, AI 4.2%. Lowest: productivity 0.6%, utilities 0.5%.
- **AI is crowded but not worse:** 24% of listings, with the same success rate as non-AI.
- **Most at risk of being rebuilt in-house:** workflow automation, admin tools, dashboards, CRM and form builders, project management, support. 35% of surveyed builders had already replaced a SaaS tool.
- **Single-feature tools next to AI coding assistants get absorbed.** One maker shut down after reaching $10,000 a year.
- **Code is not a moat.** One maker saw weekly clones and sold the codebase instead.
- **Third-party API dependence is a live risk.** One team lost months when Calendly changed its terms.

## 8. Pricing and retention

Customers needed for $1,000 a month:

| Price | Customers |
|---|---|
| $9 | 112 |
| $29 | 35 |
| $79 | 13 |
| $199 | 6 |

- Under $10 an account, churn runs 6–7% a month. At $500 and above it is 1–2%.
- At $9 and 6.5% churn, about 7 new customers a month are needed to stand still.
- Under $25 an account, annual plans keep 62% of customers a year against 41% for monthly.
- A card-required trial converts 30% of signups; an open free tier about 8%, and around 1% in small-maker reports.
- Raising price worked in both cases found. One plugin sold 43 licences at $50, then 10 at $499 for more than twice the revenue.
- Lifetime buyers are reported as the heaviest support load, and acquirers discount them.

Early churn fix: interview people who signed up and never paid, people who paid and left, and people who stayed two months. Change onboarding and the core offer before adding traffic. One maker lost 3 of 4 first customers and recovered this way.

## 10. Stage plan

### Zero to first customer (2–6 weeks)

1. Pick one buyer with one problem, preferably a business already paying for a worse tool.
2. Find 30–50 people who stated that problem in public in the last 30 days. Message each one about their own post.
3. Ask for payment: a paid pilot or first month at the real price, with setup done by hand.
4. Price at $29 a month or more, with an annual option.
5. If the product fits a marketplace, list it there in parallel.
6. Apply to a merchant of record on day 1 if billing yourself.

### First customer to ten (by day 60–90)

1. Keep the same single channel at about 20 personal contacts a week.
2. Interview every non-converter and every churner. If over half of early customers leave within two months, stop acquiring and fix.
3. Collect two or three written testimonials and the first marketplace reviews.
4. Add a "made with" link to anything customers share.

### Ten customers to $1,000 a month (months 6–12)

1. Raise the price once for new customers.
2. Move existing customers to annual with an incentive.
3. Write comparison pages for the incumbent you undercut, and answer the same questions in communities.
4. Add a 30% affiliate scheme once retention is stable. Free options: TinyAffiliate or Reditus (one programme each), or coupon codes tracked by hand.
5. Use a lifetime deal only as a capped, one-off event.

## 11. What this changes in our agent

| Skill | What it does |
|---|---|
| Store matcher | Maps the product to a store that bills for the maker and pays their country |
| Listing optimiser | Applies each store's known ranking factors to the name, subtitle and description |
| Problem-post finder | Finds 30–50 recent public statements of the problem and drafts a per-person opening line for the maker to edit |
| Payment-first validator | Refuses to count signups or upvotes as validation; asks for a paid pilot |
| Churn interviewer | Prepares the three interview sets and summarises the answers |
