# Strategy: positioning, business model and first clients

Updated 19 November 2026. Decisions taken: the premium line for founders who build with AI is the main focus, sold as a one-time First Customers Programme followed by a Distribution Partner retainer with a flat option and a growth option. The programme is built around buyer conversations, not channel setup. This file is the top-level summary. `AI-BUILDER-MODEL.md` holds the detail of each offer.

## How far to trust this

- Nothing here is tested. Prices, reply rates and client counts are starting points to check against the first clients.
- No research was done on what this segment will pay. The premium case rests on judgement and on prices of comparable human services.
- The programme was reweighted after a check of 21 prospects on 19 November 2026. It found 7 not set up, 4 set up but quiet, 7 publishing heavily to almost no audience with no customers, and 3 with a first customer or a marketing lead. One had over 100 videos and 2 subscribers. That is a small sample, though it matches the earlier research on where first customers come from.
- Prospecting on 18 November 2026 confirmed AI building for only 4 of 20 shortlisted founders. In practice we qualify on trade background, a live product and no customers. The "built with AI" line is used only where the build is confirmed.
- Reply and conversion estimates come from datasets of founders who succeeded and wrote about it.
- No account was found of an India-based agency winning foreign clients through LinkedIn or YouTube. US prospects replied least (6%) in one outreach study.
- How an India-based service business best collects payment from foreign clients was not researched. See section 9.
- The revenue-share terms have not been reviewed by a lawyer.
- Competitor prices come partly from competitors' own blogs.

## 2. Who we serve

| | Premium client | Sprint client |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Consultant, agency owner, clinician, broker, coach, contractor, operations lead or small-business owner | Indie maker with a live product |
| Built with | Lovable, Bolt, Replit, v0, Cursor, Claude Code, where known | Anything |
| Product | Solves a problem in their own trade | Micro-SaaS, AI tool, extension, template, course, ebook |
| What a customer is worth | $300 a year or more | Under $300 a year |
| Asset they hold | A network in that trade | Usually none |
| Offer | Programme, then Partner | Sprint |
| Location | US, UK, EU, Canada, Australia first | Same |

Qualifying questions before any paid step:

1. Do you earn a living in the industry this product serves?
2. Does a customer pay at least $300 a year?
3. Can a stranger sign up, pay and get a useful result today?
4. Can you give 30–45 minutes a day for 30 days?
5. What is already in place: listings, social accounts, content, press?

The answer to question 5 sets the pitch:

| What they have | What it tells us | Opening angle |
|---|---|---|
| Not set up: few or no listings, accounts or content | Buyers cannot find or try the product | "Nobody in your trade has seen this yet. Here are the first people to show it to." |
| Set up but quiet: listed and posting, no sign of customers | Presence is not the problem; conversations and conversion are | "You are listed in the right places and still have no reviews. Here is what I think is missing." |
| Busy but unseen: frequent posts, videos or articles with almost no audience and no customers | More output will not help; they need buyers, not content | "You have published a lot and almost nobody has seen it. Here is where your buyers actually are." |
| Has a customer or a marketing lead | Lower priority | Lead with one specific fix we noticed; the partner step if they ask |

### Busy but unseen: a target group in its own right

These founders publish heavily and reach almost nobody. In the first check, 6 of 20 prospects fell here.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| How to recognise them | Many videos, articles or listings against single-digit subscribers, installs or reviews |
| Why they are good clients | They already give time or money to distribution every day, so the programme asks for no new effort, only a change of direction |
| Why they feel the problem | They can see the numbers: months of output and nothing back |
| What we change for them | Pause new publishing for 30 days, spend that time on conversations, and reuse their best three pieces as proof |
| The risk | They may believe the answer is more content, or use tools that make content cheap to produce |

Not served:

- Idea-stage founders with nothing live.
- Trading, investment-tip or income-claim products.
- Founders who want us to log in to their personal accounts.

## 3. Where competitors lag

| Group | How they charge | Where it falls short |
|---|---|---|
| Building platforms (Lovable) | Marketing features planned for 2027 | Nothing today |
| Self-serve agents (Morket, NowBuild, AgentCeres, Okara) | Free to $249 a month | Produce listings and posts; assume the founder will find the buyers |
| Open-source skills (marketingskills, gtm-cofounder) | Free, then consulting | Need a coding agent; nobody does the work |
| Intent finders (Prospy, Buska) | $24–249 a month | Hand over a list; the founder does the rest |
| Auto-posters (Rankcow, Leadmore) | $99–199 a month or per action | Post from rented accounts, which Reddit bans for |
| Directory submitters | $59–299 one-off | Listings send few signups |
| Agencies and sprints | $2,500–9,500, prices often hidden | Not built for first-time founders |
| Polsia | $49 a month plus 20% of all revenue | Share applies to everything, with lock-in complaints |

Across all of them:

- Setting up listings and posts is the easy part, and founders and tools already do it.
- Almost none publishes how many users got a paying customer.
- None starts from the founder's own trade network.
- None coaches the founder through the actual conversations with buyers.
- Nobody found sells a fixed-price first-customers programme for founder-built products with published results.

## 4. The proposition

**Headline:** Your product is live. We line up its first customers with you.

**Supporting line:** In 30 days we tell you what is blocking the sale, fix your offer, and put you in at least 80 conversations with people who have the problem, starting with people you already know. You pay half to start and half on delivery.

Where the build is confirmed, the headline may open with "You built it with AI."

| Proof | What it is |
|---|---|
| The free report | Real buyer conversations for their own product, before they pay anything |
| Week-1 verdict | Go, change or stop. On "stop" we refund $700 |
| The conversations | 50 named people from their own network and 30 public posts, each with a message written for them |
| Daily coaching | We help with every reply until day 30 |
| Half on delivery | The second $500 is due only when the programme is delivered |
| Public scorecard | Each client's conversations, signups and sales, published with permission |
| No ban risk | Nothing is posted by a bot or from a rented account |

Why we can offer it at this price: our cost base is in India. Comparable human services start at $2,500.

## 5. How we serve them

### First Customers Programme, 30 days

| Week | We deliver | Founder does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Verdict: go, change or stop. Positioning. Readiness check. Review of what is already in place and what it has produced | Answer the intake |
| 2 | Offer page rewritten. Price and trial set. Tracking. 50 named people from their own network and trade, each with a message | Approve the page; start sending |
| 3 | 30 public conversations with opening lines. Daily reply coaching. Gaps filled where missing | Send, reply |
| 4 | Daily reply coaching. Paid pilot offer. Notes on why non-buyers said no. Scorecard. 90-day plan | Take calls; ask for payment |

Filling gaps in week 3:

| If the client lacks | We add |
|---|---|
| A main channel | One channel set up, with up to 12 posts written and scheduled for approval |
| Listings | Up to two store or directory listings |
| Neither | 20 more conversations in place of the setup work |
| An audience, despite heavy publishing | Their publishing time redirected to conversations for 30 days; their best three pieces reused as proof |

A "stop" verdict in week 1 ends the work: we keep $300 and refund $700.

### What we publish and what they send

| Where | Who acts | How |
|---|---|---|
| Company page, YouTube channel, website, blog | We write and schedule; they approve | Page roles or a scheduler they authorise |
| Store and directory listings | We build; they submit from their account | — |
| Community replies on Reddit, Hacker News, forums | They send | We find the thread and draft the reply |
| Personal messages and their personal LinkedIn profile | They send or approve | We draft; we never log in |

### Distribution Partner, monthly

- 20 new conversations a week, with opening messages.
- Continued network outreach and reply coaching.
- Interviews with customers who leave and buyers who said no.
- Weekly posts for their channels, written and scheduled for approval.
- Listing upkeep and review requests.
- Comparison pages and tutorial scripts.
- A weekly report against signups and sales.
- Paid channels only after the gate in `PAID-AND-SOCIAL-CHANNELS-PLAYBOOK.md` is passed.

We offer the partner step only when the programme has produced a channel that is getting replies.

### Rules for delivery

- The method starts with the founder's own trade network, then public conversations.
- One main channel and one support channel. No more.
- We check readiness and list fixes. We do not fix code or review security, and we say so in writing.
- Delivery is written and asynchronous, with one call slot a week in the Indian evening, which is the US morning.

The tool builds the report, runs the readiness check and logs messages and sales. It is free for clients.

## 7. How we get clients

### Where to find them

| Place | Signal |
|---|---|
| Product Hunt | Low-vote launches where the maker says they work in the trade |
| TrustMRR | Listings with little or no revenue, with the founder named |
| Launch boards such as Fazier and BetaList | New trade-specific products |
| Hacker News | Low-scoring launch posts by people from a trade |
| LinkedIn | Posts saying "I built an app with AI" by people with a non-engineering job title |
| Lovable, Bolt, Replit and v0 showcases | Live apps with a named builder; hard to read automatically |
| Reddit communities for AI building | Posts asking how to get users; must be searched by hand |

The first four produced the first prospect list. The last three need checking by hand.

Signals of a busy-but-unseen founder, all visible without logging in:

| Where to look | Signal |
|---|---|
| YouTube or TikTok linked from the site | Dozens of videos, fewer than 20 subscribers or followers |
| The site's sitemap | Hundreds of pages or articles published in weeks |
| Software directories and app stores | Listed in several, with zero reviews or single-digit installs |
| Launch boards | Posted on many, with a handful of votes on each |
| Press pages | A press release sent on several wires and no customer named |
| Founding offers | "20 of 20 spots left" or a first-customers price still open months later |

### The outreach

1. Pick 10 founders a day by hand and run the five qualifying questions against what is public.
2. Sort each into "not set up", "set up but quiet", "busy but unseen" or "has a customer or a marketing lead".
3. Build each one's report, with real posts from buyers in their trade.
4. Send the opening message for their group where they already are: a public reply where allowed, otherwise a direct message or an email.
5. Offer a call to those who reply, and the programme on the call.

Rules:

- A person sends every message.
- Lists stay under 50 at a time. Small hand-picked lists got 14–58% replies; scraped lists got 1–1.6%.
- Email to the US needs a postal address and an opt-out.
- For Germany and Austria use direct messages or public replies, not cold email. Check the rules for other countries before emailing.
- One follow-up, then stop.

### Channels, in order

| Channel | Use | Expectation |
|---|---|---|
| Reports sent unasked | Main channel from day 1 | 50 reports may bring 7–29 replies; expect the low end for US prospects |
| LinkedIn | 4–5 founder posts a week showing real reports; about 100 hand-picked invites a week | 4–15 calls in 90 days; inbound unlikely before month 3 |
| Referrals | Ask every client for one introduction in their trade | Top source of new business for most agencies |
| Public scorecards | Publish each client's result | Proof for every other channel |
| YouTube | Screen recordings of reviewing a real founder-built product | Slow; under 1,000 subscribers in year one is the base case |
| Borrowed lists | Offer free reports to the audience of an AI-building educator or community | One documented case of this pattern |
| Ads | None yet | A client from LinkedIn ads costs about $3,000–6,000, which only the partner fee recovers |

### Overcoming distance

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "I do not know you" | The free report is real work on their own product |
| "I am already listed and posting" | That is why we start with conversations, not setup |
| "I am already doing a lot of marketing" | We use the same daily time, pointed at named buyers; nothing extra is asked |
| "$1,000 is a lot" | Half to start, half on delivery; $700 back if we advise stopping |
| "Will you spam from my account?" | We publish only to their own channels, with approval; they send everything personal |
| "What if it does not work?" | Refund for missed deliverables; public scorecards |
| "A share of my revenue?" | Optional. Growth only, capped, and it ends three months after we stop |
| "Time zones" | Written delivery, one weekly call slot |

## 9. Open items to check before selling

| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Payment route for a service business in India billing foreign clients | Direct Stripe is invite-only. Merchant-of-record platforms may not allow human services. PayPal costs about 7–8% all-in; Razorpay charges up to 3% plus GST on international cards |
| Whether this segment pays $1,000 | Untested. Send 50 reports before building more |
| Whether "set up but quiet" and "busy but unseen" founders buy | They have shown they will spend on distribution, but may think they have it covered or that more content is the answer |
| Revenue-share agreement | Needs a lawyer's check, and clients must agree to dashboard access |
| Email rules for the UK and Canada | Not researched; several early prospects are there |
| Each platform's rules for third-party page roles and schedulers | Sets what we can publish for a client |
| Tax paperwork for exported services | Have a chartered accountant confirm GST and the export undertaking |
| Wording of the "no security review" note | Protects us when a founder-built product has flaws |
| Capacity per person | Sets how many programmes and partners we can run a month |

## 10. What we do not do

- Sell channel setup as the product.
- Produce more content for a client whose content nobody sees.
- Autonomous posting or rented accounts.
- Logging in to a client's personal accounts.
- More than two channels per client.
- A generic AI CMO subscription.
- Code fixes or security reviews.
- A share of all revenue, or a share with no end date.
- Any promise of revenue.
