## 2. The format, dissected

### 2.1 Title formula
Every single title is `What IF You [power / situation]?`

- "What IF" with capital IF is the brand signature. It is in all 30 titles.
- Second person always. The viewer is the protagonist.
- One concrete, visualisable premise per video. No abstractions.
- Optional intensifier in caps: ONLY GUY, FAMOUS, ACTUALLY.
- Numbers for scale: "10x", "100x", "1cm Every Hour", "10 Seconds".

| **Wealth / cheat-code fantasy** | Infinite money (609K), Creative mode in real life (524K), Control your luck (399K), Respawn (319K), Level up (192K) | Top tier |
| **Mind-reading / foresight** | See 10 seconds into the future (310K), See who will become famous (199K), See everyone's biggest fear (97K) | Mid-high |

The pattern is clear: premises that tap **status, money, romance and video-game logic** win by 5–20x.
Generic superpowers and body-physics premises underperform on this channel.
Note the gendered split: "Every Girl on Earth Loved You" did 459K; "Every GUY on Earth Loved You" did 20K.
The audience skews young male, and the channel leans into that deliberately.

### 2.3 Video length
10:36–10:52 is the target. That clears the 8-minute threshold for mid-roll ads while staying short enough to
hold retention. Treat 10:30–11:00 as your spec, not "about 10 minutes".

### 2.4 Story structure (from YouTube chapters)
The videos are not listicles. Each one is a **three-act short story** in second person:

1. **The gift** (0:00–1:20) – You wake up with the ability. Immediate small, funny demonstrations.
2. **Rules and small wins** (1:20–4:00) – How it works, limits, everyday exploits (school, money, sport, dating).
3. **Escalation and fame** (4:00–7:30) – Pushing it further; public notices; science or society reacts.
4. **The cost** (7:30–9:30) – Emotional toll, a tragedy or moral dilemma, a rescue scene.
5. **The choice / twist ending** (9:30–10:45) – "The final offer", "Choosing to let go", "The mysterious future".

Example chapter lists pulled from the channel:

- *Infinite Money*: The infinite money dream → Understanding economics → The limits of wealth → The social consequences → Fixing the national debt → Choosing to let go → The final test
- *10 Seconds Into the Future*: The gift of foresight → How the vision works → Daily life and minor wins → Sports, casinos, and fame → The personal cost of vision → The bus tragedy → A new perspective
- *100x Vision*: The experience → How it works → Rules and limitations → Benefits and challenges → The microscopic world → Observing the cosmos → Scientific and public fame → New rules → The emotional toll → A heroic rescue → The mysterious future

Reusable beat template: **Wake up with it → test it → exploit it → get noticed → pay for it → decide.**
The "cost" act is what makes a 10-minute runtime sustainable; pure wish-fulfilment runs out of gas at 4 minutes.

## 3. Visual style

### 3.1 Protagonist
A faceless **white, egg-headed "YOU" character** with simple dot eyes and expressive brows, wearing a
**bright blue hoodie** (occasionally a blue suit). This is the whole brand identity:

- Identical in every thumbnail, so the feed is instantly recognisable.
- Faceless and generic so any viewer projects onto it. Reinforces the second-person script.
- Cheap to redraw in any pose and setting.

Supporting characters are drawn in a more detailed, chibi-anime style (big eyes, blush marks, pastel outfits).
The contrast between the plain white protagonist and the colourful cast is intentional.

### 3.2 Art direction
- Flat 2D illustration, thick clean outlines, high-saturation palette (blues, pinks, greens, oranges).
- Bright, evenly lit scenes. No realistic shading, no photographic textures.
- Environments are full illustrations (classroom, casino, Minecraft-style voxel world, burning city), not blank backgrounds.
- Consistent look that reads as AI-image-generated with a fixed style prompt, then animated in an editor.

### 3.3 Thumbnails
Every thumbnail follows one of three layouts:

1. **Character + big word**: protagonist in a smug/determined pose, 1–3 words in bold rounded font
   with heavy outline ("infinite money", "LUCK", "/gamemode creative").
2. **Protagonist + crowd reaction**: "YOU" arrow pointing at the white character, surrounded by reacting
   cast members (girls' school, crush labels "friend / CRUSH / friend").
3. **Split or confrontation**: two halves showing the viewer vs the threat (school shooting thumbnail).

Rules they follow: one focal character, large face at left or centre, text never more than ~4 words, text
in a different colour from the background, no clutter, no channel logo.

## 6. What makes it work (and what doesn't)

**Works**
- One format, zero variation. The viewer knows exactly what they get.
- Second-person storytelling turns an explainer into a fantasy the viewer lives.
- Volume: with ~2 uploads/day the algorithm has 30 lottery tickets in three weeks. Nine hit.
- Premise choice is the whole game. Social status, money, romance, game mechanics.
- Stories end with a cost or a choice, which keeps mid-video retention up and drives comments.
- A reusable mascot removes the need for per-video character design.

**Weak spots you can beat**
- No personality or community. Comments are generic "what would you do?" prompts.
- Superpower and body-physics premises underperform, yet they keep making them.
- The school-shooting video shows they will drift into tasteless territory for clicks. Don't.
- No shorts, no community posts, no series or recurring characters beyond the mascot.
- Titles are starting to repeat themselves ("See who..." four times). The well runs dry unless premises broaden.

## 7. Playbook for whatifgraphics

### 7.1 Brand decisions (make these once)
1. **Mascot**: design your own faceless, projectable protagonist. Keep it simple (round head, one signature
   colour) but do not copy the white egg + blue hoodie. Suggestions: a bright orange or teal hoodie,
   or a distinct head shape. Lock a style sheet: front, three-quarter, side, 8 expressions, 6 poses.
2. **Title signature**: own a variant. Options: `What If You…` with a trailing emoji, or a pattern like
   `WHAT IF: You Could…`. Be consistent from video one.
3. **Palette**: pick 5 colours and use them in every thumbnail. Make the feed look like a set.
4. **Thumbnail font**: one heavy rounded sans (e.g. Fredoka One, Luckiest Guy, Bangers) with a 6–10px outline.

### 7.2 Production pipeline (to hit 3–7 videos a week)
1. **Premise bank**: brainstorm 100 premises, score each 1–5 on *wish-fulfilment*, *visual spectacle*,
   *social stakes*, *gaming overlap*. Only produce 4+ average. Use the table in section 2.2 as the prior.
2. **Script**: 1,500 words, five acts (section 2.4). Write as prose in second person, present tense.
   Every sentence must describe something drawable. End on a choice or a twist.
3. **Shot list**: split script into 90–130 shots of 4–8 seconds. One line of script per shot.
4. **Art**: generate or draw one illustration per shot with a fixed style prompt and the mascot reference.
   Keep backgrounds full and colourful. Batch by location to keep continuity.
5. **Voice**: one consistent narrator. If AI, pick one voice and never change it.
6. **Edit**: Ken Burns moves, 2–3 frame limb animation, pop-in reactions, SFX on gags, music bed at -20 dB.
   Target 10:30–11:00.
7. **Package**: thumbnail from the single most emotional shot, title from the premise bank,
   description from the section 5.1 template, 6–8 chapters, hashtags.

### 7.4 Growth tactics they are not using
- Cut 3 Shorts per video from the best gag, the twist and the cost scene. Link to the long video.
- Pin a comment with a poll ("Keep it or give it back?") to drive engagement in the first hour.
- Make "Part 2" videos for anything over 200K views within a week.
- Build 2–3 recurring side characters (the best friend, the rival, the scientist) for continuity and fan art.
- Community posts with the next three premises as a vote.
