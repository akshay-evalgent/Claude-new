# Channel-theme research: animated formats on YouTube, Nov 2026

Goal: find channel-level themes (not one-off premises) where a new, AI-built animated channel can win, with the
video format and the animation style documented well enough to decide.

Method: YouTube search results for the past week and month, sorted by views, across 30 format keywords;
channel pages for 40 channels (subs, upload count, cadence, median and peak views of the latest 30 uploads);
video descriptions and chapter lists for the format-defining videos; thumbnails for every format. Blogs were
used only for background on tools. All numbers were read on 3 Nov 2026.

Two things stand out across the table:

1. **The daily-upload second-person formats (survival diary, what-if, horror rules) produce 1M+ videos on
   channels that are 3 to 5 weeks old with 10K to 100K subs.** Nothing else in the table does that. Weekly,
   high-craft formats (Historically, StoryBox, Bernard) have higher medians, but they took 40 to 90 uploads
   and a year or more.
2. **Execution gap is real.** Final Sight's clone PictoMinute runs the identical title formula and gets 9.7K
   median against 215K. Kit vs The End runs the survival-diary formula at 15K median against Nobody Gets In's
   198K. The format gets you tested; writing and pacing get you distributed.

## 2. The six channel themes, ranked

Each one is a whole channel: name direction, episode format, animation style, build plan, risks, and a score.
Scoring (1–5): **Evidence** (how many channels are winning with it now), **48-hour potential** (do new
channels spike fast), **Buildability with our Claude pipeline**, **Monetisation** (RPM lane, runtime, policy
risk), **Durability** (will it still work in 6 months).

### Theme 1 — "Day 1 to Day 30": second-person survival diaries (stickman)

**Channel direction:** "You're trapped. Here's the plan." Every episode is one bad situation, one location,
counted in days. Starts with zombies because that is the proven engine, then widens to any disaster (flood,
blackout, pandemic lockdown, nuclear winter, snowed-in, stranded at sea, prison riot, hostile AI).

**Evidence.** Nobody Gets In: 25 uploads, 57K subs, median 198K, peaks of 3.2M and 1.8M, with Parts 2–5 of the
same location doing 518K, 273K, 139K, 84K. Beanie Survives: 24 uploads, 107K subs, median 149K, peaks 2.8M,
1.1M, 866K, 632K. Galactic Highway "Bunker Survival 1 Year": 2.2M, with Part 2 at 1.1M and Part 3 at 740K.
Clones (StickFallout 887K, DoodleSurvivals 476K, WhyThat 162K) show the formula travels. A Turkish channel's
"Trapped in a Gas Station for 365 Days" did 1.09M. Both leaders are about a month old.

**Video format (from the chapter lists and descriptions):**
- **Title formula:** `Zombie Outbreak: [N] Days [Trapped / Locked Inside] [a Location]`. Sequels add
  `(Part 2)` and the new day count. Beanie's later titles switch to dramatic lines ("She Hid The Bite. Then
  She Turned") once the audience knows the series.
- **Cold open (0:00–0:20):** a time, a place, a job. "It is 2:14 a.m. You stack shelves on the night shift at a
  24-hour supermarket, and the automatic doors have just opened for something that is not a customer."
- **Structure:** 6 to 8 chapters, each one a problem specific to the location. Supermarket: "The Cold Chain"
  (four tons of meat about to rot), "The Regulars" (people outside who know where the food is), "Forty
  Seconds" (the fire-exit magnet). Each chapter is a day range, 60 to 100 seconds, with a decision at the end.
- **Day cards:** a "DAY 7" title card between segments, and a resources counter (water, food, people) that
  changes every chapter. The tension comes from the numbers going down.
- **Ending:** a door, a choice, and a hook for the next part. Description ends "What would you have done
  differently? Tell me in the comments."
- **Length:** 8 to 12 minutes. Every 5 to 7 episodes get stitched into a 45 to 75 minute "Day 1 to Day 100
  (Full Movie)" upload, which pulls sleep-time watch hours (Kit vs The End's 1h13m did 668K).
- **Cadence:** daily. Both leaders say so in their channel description.

**Animation style and type:**
- Stick figures with one signature accent: Beanie's mustard-yellow beanie, Nobody Gets In's green apron. Heads
  are plain circles, bodies are thick lines, hands are mitts. No faces beyond dot eyes and a mouth line.
- Environments are flat 2D but detailed: supermarket aisles, rooftops, stairwells, drawn in perspective with
  a dark palette (deep navy and teal nights, amber emergency lights, red for danger). Zombies are grey-green
  silhouettes with lit eyes.
- Motion is limited: walk cycles, camera pans across a scene, doors sliding, counters ticking. Dramatic
  moments are 2 to 3 frame cuts (a hand on the glass, then the whole glass). Sound design does the work.
- Thumbnail: split image, "DAY 1" clean on the left, "DAY 30" ruined on the right, same location, same
  character, big red and white numerals.
- Voice: calm male narrator, second person, present tense, no music during tension beats, a low drone instead.

**Build plan with our pipeline:** easier than episode 1. Swap the mascot for a stick figure (fewer parts),
add a day-card component, a resource HUD, 8 to 10 dark interior backgrounds, a zombie silhouette crowd prop,
and a "damage over time" variant of each background (shelves empty, windows cracked). Script to shot list
is identical. One episode is roughly 90 shots, a day's work for the pipeline.

**Risks:** zombie fatigue in six months (mitigated by widening to other disasters); three channels already
upload daily, so ours needs a sharper location per episode. Violence stays cartoonish or ad suitability drops.

**Score:** Evidence 5 · 48-hour 5 · Buildability 5 · Monetisation 4 · Durability 3 → **22/25**

### Theme 2 — "What IF" second-person power scenarios (our current lane, upgraded)

**Channel direction:** what whatifgraphics already is: a faceless "you" gets one impossible ability, and the
story follows it from the first morning to the final choice. Upgrade: borrow the visible-number HUD from
Minute Zero ("YOUR SPEED 1,235 km/h and still speeding up") and the stat-over-every-head device from ConChaser
so every thumbnail shows the mechanic.

**Evidence.** Eight channels under 60K subs with 250K to 4.7M videos in the last month (full table in
`viral-ideas-research.md`). ConChaser median 191K on 11 uploads. Awaken 4.7M on 8 uploads. Ifverse 4.7M and
1.4M. Jeremy Bearimy 749K after three weeks. IFOLOGY, the steadiest, 48 uploads at median 81K.

**Video format:** five acts (gift → rules and small wins → escalation and fame → the cost → the choice),
10 to 11 minutes, second person, present tense, 80 to 90 shots, one text card per rule, thought bubbles for
other people's reactions. Title `What IF You [Could See / Had / Kept] [concrete premise]?`, one word in caps.
Thumbnail: mascot plus the mechanic (bubble, number, skull, console) plus 1 to 3 words. Cadence 3 to 5 a week.
Series hooks: Part 2 within 7 days of any 300K video; "FULL MOVIE" stitches of a series.

**Animation style and type:** flat 2D vector, bright saturated palette, faceless cream-headed mascot in an
orange hoodie, chibi side cast with big eyes, full illustrated backgrounds, pop-in bubbles and cards, Ken
Burns camera. Competitors split into two looks: AI-illustrated black-blob characters with glossy rendering
(ConChaser, Ifinite) and silhouette stick-bodies in hoodies (Ifverse, Minute Zero). Ours sits between them
and is more legible at thumbnail size than either.

**Build plan:** done. Episode 1 is rendered; the pipeline makes a new episode from a shot list in one day.

**Risks:** the most crowded of the six lanes and getting more crowded weekly. Premise exhaustion within the
"see everyone's X" sub-format is already visible (ConChaser's "biggest mistake" 21K, "partners" 54K).

**Score:** Evidence 5 · 48-hour 5 · Buildability 5 · Monetisation 4 · Durability 3 → **22/25**

### Theme 3 — "If You Ever See This… DO THIS": analog-horror survival rules

**Channel direction:** a collection of disturbing "broadcasts": each video is a rule set for one uncanny
situation ("If your substitute teacher starts laughing at you… leave the classroom. There are 7 rules. The
fourth one is especially important.") Settings are school, home, hotel, flight, restaurant, theatre, store:
places the 13 to 20 audience is in every day.

**Evidence.** Final Sight: 22 uploads in 22 days, 20K subs, median 215K, peaks 1.2M ("If You See A Deer In
Your Window… LEAVE THE HOUSE"), 884K, 392K, 374K. YureiTales "If You Hear Po… Po… Po…, Never Look Up" 1.1M.
The clone PictoMinute (35 uploads, median 9.7K) proves title alone is not enough.

**Video format:**
- **Title formula:** `If [ordinary thing does something wrong]... [CAPITALISED COMMAND]`. Two halves, the
  second is always an imperative: HIDE NOW, DO NOT LOOK, RUN FAST, NEVER OPEN IT, DON'T REACT.
- **Structure:** a rule list. Opening states the premise and the number of rules in the first 15 seconds, then
  rules 1 to 7, each a 60 to 90 second mini-story with a mounting detail (five small lines carved into the
  desk; a class photo taped under the desktop with one boy missing). One rule is flagged as "especially
  important" and withheld until minute 5. Ending: what happens to the person who breaks the rule.
- **Length:** 7 to 11 minutes. Cadence: daily.
- **Thumbnail:** solid red or black left half with two words in white ("HIDE NOW"), right half a close-up of a
  face doing something wrong (laughing too wide, eyes too open), with a red arrow.

**Animation style and type:** this is not character animation. It is illustrated stills (AI-generated or
drawn) with slow push-ins, light flicker, VHS tracking lines and film grain, occasional 2-frame "glitch"
replacements of a face, and heavy sound design (rain, fluorescent hum, a laugh looped). The characters look
like a 2000s cartoon gone wrong: clean outlines, flat colour, one feature distorted.

**Build plan:** very light on animation, heavier on art direction. Our renderer can produce the flat cartoon
scenes and the distortion variants (scale a mouth, swap an eye) procedurally, add a grain and scanline filter,
and drive slow camera moves. We would not need AI image generation. About 30 to 40 illustrated frames per
episode instead of 90 shots.

**Risks:** YouTube's "made for kids" and ad-suitability classifiers; keep gore absent and dread implied. Trend
burn is possible, but the engine ("rules for a familiar place") has run since the 2010s creepypasta era.

**Score:** Evidence 4 · 48-hour 5 · Buildability 4 · Monetisation 3 · Durability 3 → **19/25**

### Theme 4 — "Your Life As…": comedic second-person history (stick figures or pixel art)

**Channel direction:** you are dropped into one historical role and live it start to finish: a Roman legionary,
a Spartan boy at 7, a Victorian chimney sweep, a Mongol horse archer, a plague doctor, a 1920s rum-runner.
Bernard's angle ("Why It Sucks to Be a Rented Wife in Song Dynasty China") shows the premise should be a
strange social arrangement, not a battle.

**Evidence.** Bernard: 89 uploads, 394K subs, median 367K, peaks 1.8M, 1.6M, 660K, 520K, weekly. Wallace
("The ENTIRE History of…"): 42 uploads, median 413K, peak 2.9M. "Your Life at Every Rank in the Third Reich
Army" 800K and "…Every KGB Rank" 689K from a Russian-language channel in the same month. game.mp4's "Your Life
as the Adeptus Custodes" 1.67M. Historically (10M on "ENTIRE History of ROME") shows the ceiling, but it is a
"[No AI]" full-animation studio.

**Video format:**
- **Title formula:** `Why It Sucks to Be [a Role] (in [Era / Empire])` or `Your Life As [a Role] ([Era])`.
- **Structure:** chronological life. Birth or recruitment → training or initiation → the daily grind (the
  comedy) → the one event everyone remembers → how you die or retire → a two-line "was it worth it".
  10 to 12 chapters, each with a map insert or an artefact card when a real source is cited.
- **Length:** 15 to 25 minutes (educational RPM rewards runtime). Cadence: weekly, or twice weekly if written
  in parallel.
- **Thumbnail:** one dramatic illustrated scene with a four-word caption in yellow outlined caps (MARRIED HER
  BROTHER), or the pixel-art "YOUR LIFE AS AN ORK WARBOSS" block-letter style.

**Animation style and type:** two proven options. (a) Bernard: stick figures with period hats and props on
flat painted backgrounds, deadpan narrator, jokes in the visuals (a stick figure holding a tiny flag). (b)
Pixel art: 16-bit sprites, parallax scrolling landscapes, map scenes, the Warhammer channels' look. Pixel art
is the better fit for code generation: sprites are grids, parallax is three layers moving at different
speeds, and the style reads as "intentional" rather than "cheap".

**Build plan:** medium. Needs a sprite builder (head, body, hat, weapon variants), 15 to 20 period
backgrounds, an animated map component with routes and borders, and a research step per episode (dates,
names, a cited source or two in the description). Two days per episode including research.

**Risks:** slower to spike; the audience rewards accuracy, so a factual error costs trust. Sensitive history
(slavery, genocide) needs care in tone to stay ad-safe. Best as a second channel or a weekly slot, not the
daily engine.

**Score:** Evidence 4 · 48-hour 3 · Buildability 4 · Monetisation 5 · Durability 5 → **21/25**

### Theme 5 — "…and Couldn't Stop": numbers-driven escalation with a live readout

**Channel direction:** one variable grows without limit and the screen shows the number the whole time.
Speed, height, IQ, strength, luck, age, time rate, gravity. The hook is the readout: "YOUR SPEED 1,235 km/h.
And still speeding up. You can't slow down."

**Evidence.** Minute Zero: "You Sped Up and Couldn't Stop" 2.2M, "Time Sped Up" 2.0M, "Car Couldn't Stop
Speeding Up" 432K, "Time Ran Backwards" 394K, on a 16-upload, 21K-sub channel. All Answers "Kept Getting
Nicer" 617K, "Richer" 441K. "Every Step Made You Twice as Big" 2.4M. Ifverse "Day 1: 6 MPH. Day 28: Faster
Than Light" 82K shows the day-count variant.

**Video format:** 12 to 15 chapters, each one order of magnitude ("Your street → The highway → Faster than
sound → The Moon → The Sun → Andromeda"). Real numbers in the description and on screen (sound 1,235 km/h,
orbit 28,000, escape 40,000, Moon 384,400 km). 8 to 10 minutes. The ending flips the variable into a cost
(at near light speed, time gives way instead). Title `What If [You / Time / Your X] [Sped Up / Got Stronger /
Kept Growing] and Couldn't Stop?`.

**Animation style and type:** the most minimal of the six. Black silhouette character, large environment
gradients (dawn road, stratosphere, space), a monospace HUD in the corner with the live number, speed lines,
scale comparisons (you next to a bullet, a jet, the Moon). Motion is mostly camera and parallax. Thumbnail:
dark gradient, the readout in large serif numerals, a one-line caption.

**Build plan:** the cheapest of the six to render. A silhouette rig, a HUD component, 12 "scale" backgrounds,
and a number tween. Half a day per episode. It also folds into Theme 2 as a sub-series rather than needing
its own channel.

**Risks:** narrow; the premise set is small (perhaps 30 strong variables) and Minute Zero's own follow-ups
dropped to 6K to 19K once the novelty passed. Best as a recurring series inside Theme 2, not a standalone.

**Score:** Evidence 4 · 48-hour 4 · Buildability 5 · Monetisation 3 · Durability 2 → **18/25**

### Theme 6 — Storytime melodrama ("I was born with an ice-cold body")

**Channel direction:** first-person confessional stories with an impossible twist, told by a teen girl
narrator: "This Is What It's Like to Live with 4 Strange Brothers", "I Never Studied but I Always Got the Best
Grades", "Bullies Melt When They See Me".

**Evidence.** StoryBox Animated: 41 uploads, 213K subs, median 1.1M, peak 1.8M, weekly on Saturdays. MSA at
27.7M subs is the category king (medians 8.7M). TJ Toons median 2.2M on comedic personal stories. Almost real
("cute lil animations") 1.9M peak on 26 uploads.

**Video format:** first person, past tense, a named narrator ("Hi everyone, my name is Leonor Fernández"),
a family or school premise with one supernatural or extreme detail, 12 to 40 minutes, a cliffhanger every
three minutes, resolved with a moral. Title is a first-person sentence with a contradiction ("I'm a Famous
Singer, but I Live in an Orphanage"). Thumbnail: 3 to 5 anime-styled characters reacting to one object.

**Animation style and type:** anime-adjacent full-colour characters (large eyes, styled hair, fashion),
soft shaded backgrounds, lip-synced-ish talking heads, frequent close-ups. This is the one style on the list
our vector pipeline cannot match at competitive quality; the audience judges the character art directly.

**Build plan:** would need either AI image generation for characters (consistency problems) or a much larger
illustration library. Not a fit for a code-rendered pipeline today.

**Risks:** craft bar is high, the audience is different from ours (female 13 to 24), and MSA's scale makes
suggested-feed placement hard.

**Score:** Evidence 5 · 48-hour 2 · Buildability 1 · Monetisation 4 · Durability 4 → **16/25**

## 3. Scorecard and recommendation

| Theme | Evidence | 48-hour | Build | Money | Durable | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Survival diaries (stickman) | 5 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 3 | **22** |
| 2. What IF scenarios (current lane, upgraded) | 5 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 3 | **22** |
| 4. Your Life As… history (pixel / stick) | 4 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 5 | **21** |
| 3. If You Ever See… horror rules | 4 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 3 | **19** |
| 5. Couldn't Stop readouts | 4 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 2 | **18** |
| 6. Storytime melodrama | 5 | 2 | 1 | 4 | 4 | **16** |

**Recommendation.** Run whatifgraphics as a two-engine channel, not one:

1. **Primary engine: Theme 1, survival diaries,** because it is the only format where two brand-new channels
   both passed 2.5M in their first month, it is the easiest style to render (stick figures, dark rooms), and
   daily uploads are realistic with the pipeline. Start with "Zombie Outbreak: 30 Days Locked Inside a
   School" (Nobody Gets In's school series did 1.8M, 489K, 273K, 147K across four parts) and "30 Days
   Trapped in a Shopping Mall", then widen to blackouts and floods by week three.
2. **Secondary engine: Theme 2, What IF,** three a week, using the HUD and stat-over-head upgrades from Theme
   5. Episode 1 is already built, and the five premises in `viral-ideas-research.md` are ready to script.
3. **Keep the same mascot across both.** The survival stories are "you" too, so the orange-hoodie figure can
   be the survivor, just drawn in the stick style and in the dark palette. One recognisable character across
   two formats is how a channel becomes a brand rather than a feed.
4. **Park Theme 4 for month two** as the weekly long-form slot (20-minute pixel-art "Your Life As…"), because
   it has the best RPM and the longest shelf life, but it will not spike in 48 hours.
5. **Skip Themes 3 and 6.** Theme 3 is a strong format but its horror-rules tone does not sit beside the
   playful What IF brand on one channel; it would need its own channel and its own name. Theme 6 is out of
   reach for a vector pipeline.

## 4. A note on Claude Motion and the "made with Claude" angle

Anthropic launched Claude Motion in beta on 8 October 2026 alongside Claude Dashboards. It does not generate
video; Claude writes each animation as code and renders it to MP4, exactly the approach our pipeline uses. It
is limited to Team and Enterprise plans so far, built for short clips, and the launch video did 1.4M views.
Tutorials about making animated YouTube videos with Claude are themselves a hot topic this month (Mr omi 366K,
Mark Ai Guy "Stickman Animation with AI" 526K, Futurepedia 434K, Higgsfield 354K, Planet Ai 219K, The AI
Blueprint "Claude + Google Vids = Full Stickman Animations" 195K). Two takeaways:

- Stickman-with-AI is specifically what the tutorial market is teaching, which means Theme 1 will get more
  crowded. Speed and writing quality are the moat, not the style.
- A behind-the-scenes Short ("this whole episode was written and animated by Claude") taps a second audience
  for free. Our pipeline already does what Claude Motion does, at episode length, without the plan restriction.
