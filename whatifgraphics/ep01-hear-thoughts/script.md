# What IF You Could Hear What Everyone REALLY Thinks of You?

Channel: whatifgraphics  ·  Format: animated what-if, second person, ~10:40  ·  Voice: single narrator, conversational


## Act 1 — The gift

**01.** What if you could hear what everyone really thinks of you?  
<sub>visual: bedroom · card: "WHAT IF YOU COULD HEAR / WHAT EVERYONE REALLY / THINKS OF YOU?"</sub>

**02.** Not what they say. Not what they post. The real stuff. The thoughts they'd never say out loud.  
<sub>visual: bedroom</sub>

**03.** One morning, you wake up, and there's a voice in your head that isn't yours.  
<sub>visual: bedroom</sub>

**04.** It's your mum. She's in the kitchen. And she's thinking, 'If that boy sleeps through one more alarm, I'm selling his bed.'  
<sub>visual: kitchen</sub>

**05.** You stumble downstairs. 'Morning,' she says, smiling. In her head: 'He looks tired. Did he eat yesterday? I should make eggs.'  
<sub>visual: kitchen</sub>

**06.** Okay. That's... kind of sweet, actually. Maybe this is a one-off. Maybe you're just dreaming.  
<sub>visual: kitchen</sub>

**07.** Then you get on the bus.  
<sub>visual: busstop</sub>

**08.** Forty people. Forty heads. And every single one that notices you lights up like a notification.  
<sub>visual: bus</sub>

**09.** 'Nice hoodie.' 'He sat in my seat.' 'Is that the kid who fell asleep in maths?' 'Please don't sit next to me. Please don't sit next to me.'  
<sub>visual: bus</sub>

**10.** You sit next to that last one. Just to see what happens.  
<sub>visual: bus</sub>

**11.** By the time you reach school, you've learned three things. One: people think about you a lot more than you'd expect.  
<sub>visual: hallway · card: "1. People think about you / MORE than you expect"</sub>

**12.** Two: most of it is about your hair.  
<sub>visual: hallway · card: "2. It's mostly about / your hair"</sub>

**13.** And three: you can't turn it off.  
<sub>visual: hallway · card: "3. You can't turn it OFF"</sub>


## Act 2 — Rules and small wins

**14.** So let's set some rules. You don't hear everything. You only hear thoughts that are about you.  
<sub>visual: void · card: "THE RULES"</sub>

**15.** If someone's thinking about lunch, silence. The moment they think about you, their voice is in your head. Clear as a text message.  
<sub>visual: cafeteria</sub>

**16.** It works within about ten metres. Walls don't matter. Headphones don't help.  
<sub>visual: void · card: "10 METRES"</sub>

**17.** And you hear it in their actual voice. Which means you now know what your maths teacher sounds like when he's being honest.  
<sub>visual: classroom</sub>

**18.** 'If he gets this one wrong, I'm retiring.' You get it right. He's genuinely surprised. You know, because you heard it.  
<sub>visual: classroom</sub>

**19.** Now, the fun part. Your crush. Mia. You've had three conversations with her this year, and you've overthought all of them.  
<sub>visual: hallway</sub>

**20.** You walk past her locker. You wait. And then you hear it.  
<sub>visual: hallway</sub>

**21.** 'Oh no, he's doing the walk again. Why does he walk like that? ...It's kind of cute, though.'  
<sub>visual: hallway</sub>

**22.** Kind of cute. KIND OF CUTE. You make it to the end of the hallway before you start celebrating.  
<sub>visual: hallway · card: "KIND OF CUTE"</sub>

**23.** This is where the power starts paying off. You know exactly when to make a joke, because you can hear when someone wants you to.  
<sub>visual: cafeteria</sub>

**24.** You know when to shut up, because you can hear 'please stop talking' before anyone's face shows it.  
<sub>visual: cafeteria</sub>

**25.** You know which teachers like you, which ones are pretending, and which one still remembers the fire alarm incident.  
<sub>visual: classroom</sub>

**26.** Group project? You hear who's planning to do nothing, and you assign them the slides.  
<sub>visual: classroom</sub>

**27.** Job interview for the weekend café? The manager thinks, 'I'll hire whoever mentions they can do Sundays.' So you mention you can do Sundays.  
<sub>visual: cafe</sub>

**28.** You're hired. You haven't lied once. You've just... listened.  
<sub>visual: cafe · card: "HIRED"</sub>

**29.** Within a month, you're the most liked person in your year. Not the loudest. Not the richest. Just the one who somehow always says the right thing.  
<sub>visual: hallway</sub>

**30.** Your best friend, Leo, notices. 'Dude, what happened to you?' In his head: 'Seriously, what happened to him?'  
<sub>visual: hallway</sub>


## Act 3 — Escalation and fame

**31.** So you push it. You stop waiting for thoughts to come to you, and you start fishing for them.  
<sub>visual: hallway</sub>

**32.** You stand near the popular table and say your own name, loudly, for no reason. Six heads turn. Six opinions arrive.  
<sub>visual: cafeteria</sub>

**33.** You walk through the shopping centre on a Saturday just to see how strangers rate you. It's mixed.  
<sub>visual: mall</sub>

**34.** You go to a party and learn, in real time, what everyone thinks about your dancing. It's not mixed.  
<sub>visual: party</sub>

**35.** You start a channel. You're not funny, exactly, but you know what people want to hear before they know it themselves.  
<sub>visual: desk</sub>

**36.** Every video lands. Every comment section is a love letter. Because you've already heard the criticism and fixed it before uploading.  
<sub>visual: desk</sub>

**37.** At school, people start to notice that you're never wrong about people.  
<sub>visual: hallway</sub>

**38.** You settle arguments by telling both sides exactly what the other one thinks. It works. It also freaks everyone out.  
<sub>visual: cafeteria</sub>

**39.** Mia asks you how you always know. You say you're 'good at reading people.' She thinks, 'That's not reading. That's cheating.'  
<sub>visual: hallway</sub>

**40.** A teacher pulls you aside. 'Are you recording people?' You say no. She thinks, 'Then how does he know I was going to fail him?'  
<sub>visual: classroom</sub>

**41.** Then a video goes viral. You, at a street market, guessing strangers' first impressions of you out loud, word for word.  
<sub>visual: market</sub>

**42.** Twenty million views. The comments split in two. Half of them think you're psychic. The other half think you're a magician with a really good team.  
<sub>visual: void</sub>

**43.** A university lab emails you. They want to test you. You say yes, because you can hear the professor thinking, 'If this is real, it changes everything.'  
<sub>visual: lab</sub>

**44.** It's real. Ten volunteers behind a wall, thinking about you on cue. You repeat every single thought.  
<sub>visual: lab</sub>

**45.** By the end of the week, you're on the news. By the end of the month, you're on every news.  
<sub>visual: news</sub>

**46.** And that's when the thoughts change.  
<sub>visual: void</sub>


## Act 4 — The cost

**47.** Because now, everyone knows. And when people know you can hear them, they don't think about you less. They think about you constantly.  
<sub>visual: street</sub>

**48.** Walking down the street used to be forty voices. Now it's four hundred, and every one of them is trying to be careful.  
<sub>visual: street · card: "400 VOICES"</sub>

**49.** 'Don't think anything weird. Don't think anything weird.' 'I wonder if he can hear this.' 'He can definitely hear this.'  
<sub>visual: street</sub>

**50.** You stop going out.  
<sub>visual: bedroom_night</sub>

**51.** At home, it's quieter. Mostly. Your mum thinks about you every eleven minutes. You've counted.  
<sub>visual: kitchen · card: "every 11 minutes"</sub>

**52.** Most of it is worry. 'Is he eating.' 'Is he sleeping.' 'Should I knock.' She never knocks. She just stands outside the door, thinking about you.  
<sub>visual: door</sub>

**53.** You start noticing the silences too. The people who never think about you at all.  
<sub>visual: classroom</sub>

**54.** The girl you sit behind in history. Your cousin. The guy you've said 'hey' to every morning for two years. Nothing. You're not in there.  
<sub>visual: void</sub>

**55.** Turns out there's something worse than hearing what people think of you. It's hearing that they don't.  
<sub>visual: void · card: "silence."</sub>

**56.** Then there's Leo.  
<sub>visual: street</sub>

**57.** Leo's been your best friend since you were six. And one afternoon, while you're laughing about something, you hear him think, 'I miss when he was normal.'  
<sub>visual: street</sub>

**58.** He didn't say it. He'd never say it. But you heard it, and now it's the only thing you can hear.  
<sub>visual: street</sub>

**59.** You try to act normal. But he can tell you're acting, and you can hear him figuring that out.  
<sub>visual: street</sub>

**60.** And Mia? Mia stopped thinking about you the day the video went viral. You know, because you were listening.  
<sub>visual: hallway</sub>

**61.** This is the part nobody puts in the superpower brochure.  
<sub>visual: bedroom_night · card: "NOT IN THE / BROCHURE"</sub>

**62.** You thought you'd gotten a cheat code for people. You actually got a microphone pointed at the one thing people are supposed to keep to themselves.  
<sub>visual: void</sub>

**63.** Then one night, your grandma gets rushed to the hospital.  
<sub>visual: hospital_ext</sub>

**64.** She can't speak. The doctors say she might not. Your mum is crying in the hallway. Your dad is pretending he isn't.  
<sub>visual: hospital_hall</sub>

**65.** You sit by the bed. You hold her hand. And you hear her.  
<sub>visual: hospital_room</sub>

**66.** 'There he is. My favourite. Look at him, all worried. Tell him I'm not scared. Tell him I'm proud of him. Tell him.'  
<sub>visual: hospital_room</sub>

**67.** So you tell your mum. Word for word. Everything grandma can't say.  
<sub>visual: hospital_room</sub>

**68.** And for the first time since this started, the power doesn't feel like a curse. It feels like the only thing in the room that's doing any good.  
<sub>visual: hospital_room</sub>

**69.** She gets better, by the way. Slowly. And every day she tells you, out loud, the same thing she was thinking. Just to make sure you heard it both ways.  
<sub>visual: kitchen</sub>


## Act 5 — The choice

**70.** A week later, a stranger sits next to you on the bus.  
<sub>visual: bus</sub>

**71.** He doesn't say anything. He doesn't have to. Because he's thinking directly at you.  
<sub>visual: bus</sub>

**72.** 'You've had it for a year now. I had it for forty. Do you want it to stop?'  
<sub>visual: bus</sub>

**73.** He explains. If you say yes, it's gone the moment you step off the bus. No thoughts. No voices. Just people's faces again, and whatever they decide to tell you.  
<sub>visual: void · card: "KEEP IT?"</sub>

**74.** You think about the bus full of opinions. The party. The channel. Mia's locker.  
<sub>visual: void</sub>

**75.** You think about Leo. About four hundred people trying not to think.  
<sub>visual: void</sub>

**76.** And you think about a hospital room, and a voice that only you could hear.  
<sub>visual: hospital_room</sub>

**77.** 'Could I keep it,' you ask, 'but only for the people who can't speak?' He laughs. 'That's not how it works, kid. It's all of them or none of them.'  
<sub>visual: bus</sub>

**78.** The bus slows down. Your stop. You stand up.  
<sub>visual: bus</sub>

**79.** And here's the thing. We're not going to tell you what you chose.  
<sub>visual: busdoor</sub>

**80.** Because the real question isn't whether you could hear what everyone really thinks of you.  
<sub>visual: busstop</sub>
