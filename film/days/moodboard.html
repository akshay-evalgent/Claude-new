At birth everyone gets a glass jar of about 30,000 glowing marbles, one for each day of an average life. Every night one marble rises out of the window and becomes a star. Days he spends become bright stars. Days he puts off become dim ones. A wordless 2:30 paper-cut lightbox film in the LANTERN style, built so the number in the thumbnail is also the clock inside the story.

What we take

  • A number big enough to stop a thumb: the jar's label, in the thumbnail and in every frame.
  • A face. SkyeWei shows faces win likes, so the puppets now have a backlit face sheet with eight expressions.
  • A habit everyone has: putting things off, drawn as one enemy, the LATER box.
  • A light ending that asks for one action.

Where we beat them

  • The number lives inside a story, so it rises and falls with a life you care about.
  • The payoff is a picture, a sky made of the days he spent, instead of a sum.
  • 2:30, shorter than all four, so more people finish it.
  • No narration: it works in every language, with captions in seven.
  • It ends on a choice for today, never on death.

Light and motif rules

Everything on screen means one thing

The jar is the clock

The only counter in the film, written on its label. Fewer marbles means less glow, so his rooms really do get darker as he ages, unless the sky outside is bright.

Stars are days

Spent days rise warm and bright with a chime. Wasted days hang as cold pinpricks. The sky is the scoreboard and the final payoff.

The scarf is love

His mother knits it at 7. It is the one saturated colour on him at every age, so you always know where he is in a wide shot.

LATER is the enemy

A cardboard box. Everything he postpones goes in one. They pile up until they block the window and the sky.

Faces carry it

A backlit face sheet with cut eyes, brows and mouth. No one speaks; the face tells you how a day went.

No one dies on screen

The neighbour's jar reads 3 and he is dancing. Sadness lowers sharing; lift and one action raise it.

Character sheet

Him, at every age

Paper puppets: joints rotate, nothing bends. The cowlick and the scarf stay the same from 7 to 82; the coat, glasses and beard tell you the year.

Title: What If You Could See How Many Days You Have Left?

Alt: 30,000 DAYS | An Animated Short Film

First line: If you're 25, you have about 20,900 days left. Here's what one man did with his.

Sound palette

  • The tink. One glass marble a night is the sound logo. In the time-lapses the tinks speed up into a rhythm.
  • The neighbour's waltz. A 3/4 tune on an old gramophone leaks through the ceiling at the turn, then becomes the full score at dawn.
  • Childhood: music box and felt piano. LATER: a cold pad, phone buzz, cardboard thuds.
  • The end: strings under the last line. No narration anywhere.

Decide before production

  1. End line: "Today is one of them." or "Spend one today."
  2. Lead title: the "What if" question (fits the channel name) or "30,000 DAYS".
  3. Length: 2:30 as boarded, or 3:00 with a longer middle.
  4. Anything to change on the cast or the look.
