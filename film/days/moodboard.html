Everyone has a glass jar of about 30,000 glowing marbles, one for each day of an average life, and a phone that reminds them how many are left. He keeps tapping "Remind me later". Each night one marble rises out of the window and becomes a star: bright if he lived the day, dim if he put it off.

Version four is a Japanese 3D anime look: toon-shaded 3D with ink outlines, painted anime faces, manga sound effects on screen, and a manga storyboard page. The beats of I'll Start Tomorrow… stay; the characters, designs and story are ours.

Every image on this page is a real-time 3D render from the film's own scene files, not a paint-over. Any shot can be re-rendered from a new angle in about two seconds.

The look · キャラクター設定

Toon-shaded 3D that reads like a hand-drawn anime

  • Cel shadingTwo or three flat tones per material, never a smooth gradient. Shadows lean violet; lit sides lean warm.
  • Ink outlinesEvery mesh has a dark line around it, so a 3D frame holds up as a manga panel.
  • Painted facesEyes, brows and mouths are drawn, not modelled: big irises, two highlights, a painted fringe shadow. The jar can appear in his pupil.
  • Clumped hairTapered locks with a jagged sheen band. The curl on top (the アホ毛) is his silhouette at any distance.
  • Only time glowsBloom lands on the jar, the stars and the screens, and nothing else.
Manga storyboard · 絵コンテ

The same scenes, printed as a manga page

Each panel is a 3D render turned into ink and dot screentone, with closer cameras for the small panels. Narration is in Japanese, with English under each box. It reads left to right for our audience.

What we take

  • A number big enough to stop a thumb: the jar's label, in the thumbnail and in every frame.
  • A face. SkyeWei shows faces win likes, so he has eight painted anime expressions.
  • A habit everyone has: putting things off, drawn as one enemy, the LATER box.
  • A light ending that asks for one action.

Where we beat them

  • The number lives inside a story, so it rises and falls with a life you care about.
  • The payoff is a picture, a sky made of the days he spent, instead of a sum.
  • 2:45: as short as the shortest of the four, and 1:40 shorter than SkyeWei's, so more people finish it.
  • No narration: it works in every language, with captions in seven.
  • It ends on a choice for today, never on death.

Light and motif rules

Everything on screen means one thing

The jar is the clock

The count appears three ways: on the jar's label, on the lock screen, and on the countdown cards. They always agree. Fewer marbles means less glow, so his rooms really do get darker as he ages, unless the sky outside is bright.

Stars are days

Spent days rise warm and bright with a chime. Wasted days hang as cold pinpricks. The sky is the scoreboard and the final payoff.

The scarf is love

His mother knits it at 7. It is the one saturated colour on him at every age, so you always know where he is in a wide shot.

LATER is the enemy

A cardboard box. Everything he postpones goes in one. They pile up until they block the window and the sky.

Only time glows

The jar, the stars and the screens are the only things that glow. Everything else is flat cel colour with an ink line.

Faces carry it

Painted anime eyes, brows and mouths on 3D heads. No one speaks; the face tells you how a day went.

Japanese as texture

Sound effects are Japanese, as in manga. Every card and line is in English first, with the Japanese under it, so nothing needs translating.

No one dies on screen

The neighbour's jar reads 3 and he is dancing. Sadness lowers sharing; lift and one action raise it.

Thumbnail, title and sound

The number in the thumbnail is the clock in the film

THUMBNAIL A

A face, a jar, a number

Combines Kurzgesagt's big number with SkyeWei's face. 20,504 is the count at 26, the age most of the audience is near.

Title: What If You Could See How Many Days You Have Left?

Alt: 30,000 DAYS | An Animated Short Film

First line: If you're 25, you have about 20,900 days left. Here's what one man did with his.

Sound palette

  • The tink. One glass marble a night is the sound logo. In the time-lapses the tinks speed up into a rhythm.
  • The neighbour's waltz. A 3/4 tune on an old gramophone leaks through the ceiling at the turn, then becomes the full score at dawn.
  • Childhood: music box and felt piano. LATER: a cold pad, phone buzz, cardboard thuds.
  • The end: strings under the last line. No narration anywhere.

Decide before production

  1. End line: "Today is one of them." or "Spend one today."
  2. Lead title: the "What if" question (fits the channel name) or "30,000 DAYS".
  3. Length: 2:45 as boarded (theirs is 4:25; ours drops their least-watched grind).
  4. Japanese on screen: sound effects only, or the bilingual cards as well.
  5. Anything to change on the cast or the look.
