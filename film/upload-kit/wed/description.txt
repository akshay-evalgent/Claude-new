Picture a modern wedding: the dress, the rings, the cake, and about thirty four thousand dollars. Marriage turns up in every known human culture. But fifty thousand years ago there were no rings, no paper and no cake. So how did ancient humans get married?

From gibbon duets and love-struck voles to Hadza couples with no ceremony at all, Kalahari husbands who hunted for their in-laws for years, a special "mother-in-law language", Neanderthal families read from DNA, walking marriage in the Himalayas, a 4,000-year-old marriage contract and the Lovers of Valdaro, this is the story of how marriage began, and what it was really for.

Which custom surprised you most: bride service, the mother-in-law language, or walking marriage? Tell me in the comments.
