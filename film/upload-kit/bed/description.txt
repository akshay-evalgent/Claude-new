Fifty thousand years ago, a hunter lies down on the bare ground. No mattress. No pillow. No walls. And somewhere in the dark, the lions are waking up. So how did ancient humans actually sleep without beds?

From a 200,000-year-old grass bed laid on ash in a South African cave, to bedding topped with leaves that kill mosquitoes, to chimpanzees whose nests hold far fewer body bacteria than our beds, this is the story of sleep before the bedroom. And why, in a Hadza camp, everyone was asleep at once for just 18 minutes in more than 220 hours of night.
