Fifty thousand years ago, a five-year-old girl wakes up in a camp. No school. No teacher. No books. Yet by the time she grows up, she has to know which plants are food, where to find water, how to read tracks and how to keep a baby alive. So how did ancient kids learn it all?

From toddlers' finger marks on a cave ceiling in France, to a trail of footprints where someone carried a toddler for more than a kilometre and a half, to Aka babies handed real knives and taught how to use them, this is the story of growing up before school existed. And why the oldest classroom on Earth didn't have any walls.
