Fifty thousand years ago, a small group stands around a grave. No temple. No holy book. No priest. Yet they lay someone they loved gently into the earth, sprinkle red ochre over the body, and place something beside them for the journey. So what did ancient humans believe in?

From chimpanzees who refused to sleep where a friend had died, to a child laid to rest 78,000 years ago with its head probably on a pillow, to Neanderthal stalagmite rings deep in a cave, the Lion Man, and the giant carved pillars of Göbekli Tepe, this is the story of the oldest rituals we know. We can't know exactly what ancient people believed. But we can see what they did.
