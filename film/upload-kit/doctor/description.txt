Fifty thousand years ago, a man wakes up in a cave with his jaw on fire. No pharmacy. No dentist. No painkiller. So how did ancient humans survive injuries, infections and toothache without doctors?

From a Neanderthal with painkilling poplar in his tartar, to 14,000-year-old dental work done with flint, to a child whose leg was amputated 31,000 years ago and who lived for years afterwards, this is the story of medicine before medicine. And why the oldest treatment of all was someone who stayed.
