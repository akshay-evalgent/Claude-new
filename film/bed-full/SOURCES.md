# Sources for "How Did Ancient Humans Sleep Without Beds?"

Checked on 2026-11-12 against the original papers, museum pages and university releases. The script follows the "safe to say" wording.

| Claim in the film | Safe to say | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Short sleep today | About 1 in 3 US adults sleep less than 7 hours. | CDC, *MMWR* 65(6), 2016 |
| Lions at night | Most lion attacks on people in Tanzania happened after dark; lions hunt most successfully on dark nights. Used as a modern analogy. | Packer et al., *PLoS ONE* 2011 |
| Half-brain sleep | Some birds and dolphins sleep with one brain hemisphere at a time. | Rattenborg et al., *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 2000 |
| First-night effect | On the first night in a new place, part of the left hemisphere sleeps less deeply and reacts more to odd sounds. 35 volunteers. | Tamaki et al., *Current Biology* 2016 |
| Randy Gardner | A 17-year-old student stayed awake about 264 hours (11 days) in 1963–64; poor concentration, memory lapses, hallucinations (a street sign seen as a person); recovered. | Ross, *Archives of Neurology* 1965 |
| Sleep and brain waste | In sleeping mice, the space between brain cells grew by about 60% and waste cleared faster. Mice, not humans. | Xie et al., *Science* 2013 |
| SK 54 | Juvenile *Paranthropus robustus* skull from Swartkrans, roughly 1.5–2 million years old; two holes about 33 mm apart match a leopard's lower canines (SK 349). Leopard probably took the body into a tree. | Brain 1970; Australian Museum |
| Hunters or hunted | C.K. Brain's book *The Hunters or the Hunted?* (1981). | Brain 1981 |
| Chimp nests | A new nest every evening, built in a few minutes. | Hernández-Aguilar et al., *American Journal of Primatology* 2013 |
| Ironwood beds | At Semliki, Uganda, *Cynometra alexandri* is 9.6% of trees but held 73.6% of 1,844 nests; firm, stable, springy. | Samson & Hunt, *PLoS ONE* 2014 |
| Orangutan roofs | Orangutans sometimes add a roof, "pillow" or "blanket" to the nest. | van Casteren et al., *PNAS* 2012 |
| Nest bacteria | 41 nests in Issa Valley, Tanzania: only 3.5% of bacterial reads came from chimp bodies; four ectoparasites found. The 35% figure for human beds is from the team's earlier US home studies. | Thoemmes et al., *Royal Society Open Science* 2018 |
| Ground sleep | Hypothesis: by about 2 million years ago early *Homo* (probably *H. erectus*) left tree nests for ground sleep, perhaps helped by fire. | Coolidge & Wynn, *Before Farming* 2006 |
| Wonderwerk fire | Burned bone and ashed plants inside the cave, about 1 million years ago. | Berna et al., *PNAS* 2012 |
| Swartkrans fire | Burned bones about 1.0–1.5 million years old. Brain's "balance of power" idea was not clearly supported by a 2008 re-test. | Brain & Sillen, *Nature* 1988; Pickering et al., *Journal of Anthropological Archaeology* 2008 |
| Human sleep vs primates | Humans sleep about 7 h, less than expected (about 9.55 h) and less than chimps (about 11.5 h); REM is nearly 25% of human sleep. "Shorter but deeper" is a hypothesis. | Samson & Nunn, *Evolutionary Anthropology* 2015; Nunn & Samson, *AJPA* 2018 |
| Border Cave | Grass bedding at least 200,000 years old, laid on ash; camphor bush remains on top; red ochre and stone-tool debris in it. Insect deterrence is the authors' suggestion. | Wadley et al., *Science* 2020 |
| Sibudu | From about 77,000 years ago: sedge and rush bedding topped with *Cryptocarya woodii* leaves, which contain insect-killing chemicals; at least 15 layers to about 38,000 years ago; burning from about 73,000 years ago. | Wadley et al., *Science* 2011 |
| Ohalo II | About 23,000 years old, Sea of Galilee; grass bundles along the walls of a brush hut around a central hearth; exposed when the lake dropped in 1989. | Nadel et al., *PNAS* 2004 |
| Hunter-gatherer sleep | Hadza, San, Tsimane (94 people): 5.7–7.1 h sleep; onset about 3.3 h after sunset; usually woke before sunrise; sleep follows falling night temperature; about an hour more sleep in winter (San and Tsimane); insomnia rare, no word for it in San or Tsimane; light under 5 lux at night; no regular segmented sleep. | Yetish et al., *Current Biology* 2015 |
| Camping and the body clock | Summer week in the Colorado Rockies (8 people): melatonin rhythm about 2 h earlier, night owls shifted most. Later study: a summer weekend shifted it about 1.4 h; a winter week about 2.6 h. | Wright et al., *Current Biology* 2013; Stothard et al., *Current Biology* 2017 |
| Skara Brae | Neolithic village on Orkney, about 3100–2500 BC; stone box beds either side of a central hearth, stone dressers; exposed by a storm in 1850. Bed fillings are unknown. | Historic Environment Scotland |
| Egyptian headrests | Made of wood, ivory, pottery or stone; Tutankhamun was buried with eight, including an ivory one held up by the god Shu. | Glencairn Museum, 2018 |
| Hammock | From Taíno via Spanish *hamaca*; first written about in Columbus's 1492 journal. | Language Log; Columbus journal (Las Casas) |
| Mattress | From Arabic *maṭraḥ*, "place where something is thrown; cushion, mat", via Italian and French. | Dictionary.com |
| Segmented sleep | Ekirch documents "first sleep" and "second sleep" in pre-industrial Europe. Not seen in the three equatorial societies; a Madagascar farming village showed an after-midnight activity peak on 49% of nights. | Ekirch, *American Historical Review* 2001; Samson et al., *American Journal of Human Biology* 2017 |
| Night watch | 33 Hadza adults over 20 days: in more than 220 hours, all were asleep at once for only 18 minutes; more than a third awake or dozing on average; older people slept and woke earlier. The "poorly sleeping grandparent hypothesis". | Samson et al., *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* 2017 |
| Mother-infant sleep | Close mother-infant sleep is common across cultures and nearly universal among primates. Not health advice. | Gettler & McKenna, *AJPA* 2010 |
