# Sources for "How Did Ancient Humans Survive Without Doctors?"

Checked on 2026-11-12 against the original papers and university releases. The script follows the "safe to say" wording.

| Claim in the film | Safe to say | Source |
|---|---|---|
| El Sidrón bitter plants | Northern Spain, about 49,000 years ago. Chemical traces (not DNA) consistent with yarrow and chamomile in one adult's tartar, with wood smoke and cooked starch. Self-medication suggested; flavouring or accident also possible. | Hardy et al., *Naturwissenschaften* 2012; Krief et al., *Antiquity* 2015 |
| El Sidrón poplar and Penicillium | One individual with a jaw abscess and a gut parasite had poplar and Penicillium DNA in his tartar. "May have been self-medicating"; the mould may come from food. | Weyrich et al., *Nature* 2017 |
| Chausiku the chimpanzee | Mahale Mountains, Tanzania, 1987. A sick chimp chewed bitter Vernonia pith and recovered within about a day; the local Tongwe use the plant. | Huffman & Seifu, *Primates* 1989 |
| Rakus the orangutan | Suaq Balimbing, Sumatra, June 2022 (published 2024). Chewed Fibraurea tinctoria, applied juice for about 7 minutes, then covered the wound; closed in 5 days, healed within a month. One observation. | Laumer et al., *Scientific Reports* 2024 |
| Neanderthal injuries | Rodeo-rider comparison (1995); a larger 2018 study found head-injury rates similar to Upper Palaeolithic modern humans. | Berger & Trinkaus 1995; Beier et al., *Nature* 2018 |
| Hunter-gatherer lifespans | Low averages reflect child deaths; adults who survive childhood often reach their 60s–70s; most common adult age at death about 70. | Gurven & Kaplan, *Population and Development Review* 2007 |
| Assisted birth | Help at birth is close to universal across cultures; babies usually emerge facing away from the mother. | Rosenberg & Trevathan |
| Ötzi | About 5,300 years old; found 1991; whipworm; carried birch polypore (a possible remedy); 61 tattoos mostly over worn joints and the lower back; Lyme disease bacteria in his genome; arrowhead in the left shoulder. | iceman.it; Capasso, *Lancet* 1998; Samadelli et al. 2015; Keller et al., *Nature Communications* 2012 |
| Krapina | Croatia, about 130,000 years; toothpick grooves and scratches beside a twisted tooth, "presumably" easing irritation. | Frayer et al. 2017 |
| Villabruna | Northern Italy, about 14,000 years; a molar cavity scratched and levered with pointed flint tools; oldest known evidence of treating a cavity. | Oxilia et al., *Scientific Reports* 2015 |
| Riparo Fredian | Tuscany, about 13,000 years; both upper front incisors widened and holding bitumen, plant fibres and probable hairs; a probable filling. | Oxilia et al., *AJPA* 2017 |
| Mehrgarh | Pakistan, 9,000–7,500 years; 11 drilled molars from 9 adults; experiments drilled a tooth in under a minute. | Coppa et al., *Nature* 2006 |
| Liang Tebo | Borneo, at least 31,000 years; lower left leg amputated in childhood; survived 6–9 years; healed with no infection; foot not in the grave. Previous oldest amputation about 7,000 years. | Maloney et al., *Nature* 2022 |
| Trepanation | Oldest well-healed case Vasilyevka II, Ukraine, 7300–6220 BC; most patients survived in parts of later Peru. | Lillie, *Nature* 1998; Kushner, Verano & Titelbaum, *World Neurosurgery* 2018 |
| Dmanisi | Georgia, about 1.77 million years; lost all but one tooth years before death; others "may have" helped; debated. | Lordkipanidze et al., *Nature* 2005 |
| Benjamina | Sima de los Huesos, about 430,000 years; craniosynostosis; survived more than 5 years (age 5 to under 12.5); same care as other children. | Gracia et al., *PNAS* 2009 |
| Shanidar 1 | Iraq, found 1957 by Ralph Solecki, roughly 50,000 years; crushed eye socket (possibly blind in one eye), withered right arm with forearm and hand lost, limp, deaf in one ear; died aged 40–50. | Trinkaus & Villotte, *PLoS ONE* 2017 |
| Man Bac | Northern Vietnam, about 3,500–4,000 years; paralysis from childhood; completely dependent for at least a decade. | Oxenham et al. 2009; Tilley & Oxenham 2011 |
| Grandmothers | Hadza grandmothers' foraging improves grandchildren's nutrition; the grandmother hypothesis. | Hawkes et al., *PNAS* 1998 |
| Honey on wounds | Egyptian papyri (Edwin Smith, Ebers, about 1550 BC) prescribe honey, grease and lint. | Review, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7761083 |
| Germs and penicillin | Germ theory work from the 1860s; penicillin discovered 1928, first patient 1941. | Britannica |
| Social ties and survival | Strong social relationships linked to about 50% better survival odds; comparable to risks like smoking. | Holt-Lunstad, Smith & Layton, *PLoS Medicine* 2010 |
| Fever in lizards | Infected desert iguanas move to warmer spots, raising body temperature. | Kluger, Ringler & Anver, *Science* 1975 |
| Ant stitches | Ethnographic use of large ant mandibles to close wounds in Africa, South America and Asia. | Gudger, *JAMA* 1925 |
| Borneo cave art | East Kalimantan has some of the oldest known cave paintings. | Aubert et al., *Nature* 2018 |
