# Sources for "How Did Ancient Humans Keep Food Without Fridges?"

Checked on 2026-11-11 (web search of the original papers and university releases).
The script's wording follows the "safe to say" column.

| Claim in the film | Safe to say | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Qesem Cave bone "cans" | Qesem Cave, Israel, about 420,000–200,000 years ago. Chop marks on fallow deer leg bones fit removing skin that had dried, so bones were stored with skin on and cracked for marrow later. An experiment with 79 deer leg bones found marrow stayed good for up to 9 weeks in cool autumn conditions (about 13°C), and declined from about week 3 in warmer conditions. Researchers described the bones as used like "cans". | Blasco et al., *Science Advances* 2019, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6785254 |
| Norje Sunnansund fish | Southeastern Sweden, about 9,200 years ago. A long pit ringed by stake holes, packed with fish bones; about 80% roach. Interpreted as fermentation without salt; no pottery. At least 60 tonnes of fish caught at the site. Skins and burial are a likely reconstruction, not finds. | Boethius, *Journal of Archaeological Science* 2016; Lund University release, https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/article/signs-early-settlement-nordic-region-date-back-cradle-civilisation |
| Granaries at Dhra' | Dhra', near the Dead Sea, Jordan, about 11,300 years ago, about 1,000 years before domesticated crops in the region. Raised floors on wooden beams set into notched stones; air flow and rodent protection. Barley husk traces. | Kuijt & Finlayson, *PNAS* 2009, https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2009/06/090623150619.htm |
| Gilgal grain stash | About 260,000 wild barley and 120,000 wild oat grains found at Gilgal I. | Weiss et al., *Science* 2006 |
| Mezhyrich | Central Ukraine; mammoth-bone huts and large pits; a 2025 re-dating puts one structure at about 18,000 years ago. Pits as natural freezers is a hypothesis; some pits also held refuse, tools and burned bone. | Chu, Shydlovskyi & Maier, *Open Research Europe* 2025, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12639289/ ; Soffer 1985 |
| Hadza hunting and sharing | Average hunter makes a big kill on about one day in thirty. Large game is shared widely; in one dataset the hunter's household got about a tenth or less. Why they share is debated (status vs. family provisioning). Woodburn quote: "to preserve it and store it would be largely wasted effort: other people would simply demand meat when their own was finished and it would be wrong to refuse them." | Hawkes, O'Connell & Blurton Jones, *Human Nature* 2014, https://content.csbs.utah.edu/~hawkes/HawkesOConnellBLurtonJones14moreLessonsHadzaHunters.pdf ; Wood & Marlowe 2013; Woodburn |
| Hxaro | Ju/'hoansi delayed gift exchange that builds obligations; about 15 partners, 2–200 km away; average 4.4 months a year living with partners (1974 data). Gifts mostly beadwork, arrows, clothing, not food. | Wiessner 1982 (book chapter, Cambridge UP); Wiessner & Huang, *PNAS* 2022, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9564820 |
| Pemmican | Dried, pounded meat (traditionally bison) mixed with about equal melted fat, sometimes berries; Cree word; kept dry, months to years. | *Canadian Encyclopedia*, https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/pemmican |
| Drying and smoking science | Microbes need available water; drying stops growth but doesn't kill. Smoke deposits antimicrobial phenols and acids on the surface. Bacteria can double "in as little as 20 minutes" in the danger zone. | USDA FSIS danger-zone guidance; FDA water-activity guide, https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/inspection-technical-guides/water-activity-aw-foods |
| Stone Age drying evidence | Direct evidence is scarce. Kharaneh IV, Jordan, about 18,800–18,600 years ago: small post holes next to gazelle bones, tentatively read as a drying rack. | Spyrou et al., *Journal of Anthropological Archaeology* 2019 |
| Salt | Poiana Slatinei, Lunca, Romania; brine boiled into salt cakes; earliest phase 6050–5500 BC; "probably" the world's oldest salt works. Not evidence of salting food. | Weller & Dumitroaia, *Antiquity* 2005, https://www.antiquity.ac.uk/projgall/weller306/ |
| Kiviak | Present-day Inughuit tradition, northwest Greenland: hundreds of little auks in a sealskin, sewn shut, sealed with fat, under rocks for months. No archaeological date claimed. | https://www.anthropology-news.org/articles/catching-birds-in-the-high-arctic/ |
| Testart (storage and inequality) | Large-scale storage made settled, unequal hunter-gatherer societies possible (a necessary, not sufficient, condition). | Testart, *Current Anthropology* 1982 |

General knowledge used without a single citation: fresh meat is about three-quarters water; flies lay eggs on meat that can hatch within a day; Leeuwenhoek first saw bacteria in the 1670s; electric fridges became common in homes about a century ago; Homo sapiens is about 300,000 years old; humans carry more body fat than other apes and babies are born relatively fat; leopards cache kills in trees; spotted hyenas have been seen stashing food in water; acorn woodpeckers store tens of thousands of acorns in granary trees; honey resists spoilage because of its low water content; the Hadza and the greater honeyguide cooperate over honey; Norwegian stockfish; Andean chuño; yakhchāls in Persia; roughly a third of food is lost or wasted (FAO); community fridges.
