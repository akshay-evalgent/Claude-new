# Sources for "How Did Ancient Kids Grow Up Without Schools?"

Checked on 2026-11-12 against the original papers, museum pages and university releases. The script follows the "safe to say" wording.

| Claim in the film | Safe to say | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Hours in class | Compulsory primary and lower-secondary schooling averages about 7,600 hours across OECD countries. | OECD, *Education at a Glance* 2023, Indicator D1 |
| Compulsory school is recent | Prussia ordered rural children into school in 1763; Massachusetts passed the first US compulsory-attendance law in 1852; England made elementary school compulsory in 1880. Enforcement was patchy at first. | Encyclopedic summaries (Compulsory education) |
| Writing | Writing appeared in Mesopotamia more than 5,000 years ago. | Standard chronology |
| First baby, chimps vs foragers | Wild chimpanzees usually first give birth at around 13–15 (average about 14.9); women in foraging societies at around 19. | Walker, K.K. et al., *J. Human Evolution* 2018; Koster et al. 2020 |
| Brain energy | Around age 4, a child's brain uses glucose equal to about two thirds of the body's resting energy and over 40% of daily energy; body growth is slowest then. The trade-off is a hypothesis. | Kuzawa et al., *PNAS* 2014 |
| Words before school | Children know thousands of words by school age, around 10,000 by one well-known estimate. | Anglin, *Monographs of the SRCD* 1993 |
| Ju/'hoansi plants | More than 100 wild plants recognised as food; Richard Lee counted 105. | Lee, *The Dobe Ju/'hoansi* |
| Hunting peaks late | Records from more than 1,800 hunters at 40 sites: hunters most productive at about 30–35. | Koster et al., *Science Advances* 2020; Walker et al., *J. Human Evolution* 2002 |
| Termite fishing | At Gombe, young females learned termite fishing more than two years earlier than males (about 31 vs 58 months); daughters watched mothers more, sons played more. 14 young chimps. | Lonsdorf, Eberly & Pusey, *Nature* 2004 |
| Nut cracking | At Bossou, Guinea, young chimps start cracking nuts at about 3.5–5 years, steal kernels before that, and improve until about 11. | Inoue-Nakamura & Matsuzawa, *J. Comparative Psychology* 1997; Matsuzawa 2011 |
| Meerkat teaching | Helpers give pups dead or disabled scorpions (sting bitten off), then intact live ones; they nudge escaping scorpions back. | Thornton & McAuliffe, *Science* 2006 |
| Cooperative breeding | Humans as cooperative breeders (Hrdy). Efe babies average about 14 caretakers; at three weeks they spend about 40% of their time with someone other than their mother. | Hrdy, *Mothers and Others* 2009; Tronick, Morelli & Winn, *American Anthropologist* 1987 |
| Sibling care | In many societies older children do much of the carrying and minding of babies. | Weisner & Gallimore, *Current Anthropology* 1977 |
| White Sands trackway | More than 10,000 years ago, probably a young woman or teenager carried a toddler of about two for more than 1.5 km, setting the child down now and then, and returned hours later without the child; mammoth and giant sloth tracks cross it. | Bennett et al., *Quaternary Science Reviews* 2020 |
| Play | Anthropologists surveyed by Peter Gray (10 respondents, 7 cultures) said children were free to play "almost all day every day"; Efe boys played nearly all the time until 15–17. Mixed-age groups because of few same-age peers. | Gray, *American Journal of Play* 2009 |
| Hadza bows | Boys get their first small bows at about three and practise for years on targets, birds and small animals. | Marlowe, *The Hadza* 2010 (via secondary sources) |
| Hadza foraging | Children from about five gather baobab, berries and small tubers in groups, eat some on the spot and bring some home; girls bring more home than boys. | Crittenden et al., *Evolution and Human Behavior* 2013 |
| Aka infant teaching | Ten Aka infants aged 12–14 months: 169 teaching episodes in ten hours (about 11 per hour); infants were handed digging sticks, knives, machetes and axes and shown how to use them. | Hewlett & Roulette, *Royal Society Open Science* 2016 |
| Toys | Thule (Inuit ancestor) sites, about AD 1200: miniature harpoons, kayaks, sledges and dolls. A Laugerie-Basse bone disc (Magdalenian, 15,000+ years) with a deer on each face may have been a spinning toy. | Riede et al., *Evolutionary Anthropology* 2018 |
| Le Rozel | 257 Neanderthal footprints about 80,000 years old, a group of perhaps 10–13 people, mostly children and teenagers; youngest about two. | Duveau et al., *PNAS* 2019 |
| Rouffignac | Finger-flute widths compared with flutings by 124 modern pupils suggest makers aged about 2–5; high ceiling marks suggest children were probably held up. Magdalenian, 13,000+ years. | Sharpe & Van Gelder, *Antiquity* 2006 |
| Chauvet | A young person, perhaps a child, left footprints along about 70 m of passage and wiped a torch on the wall; charcoal about 26,000 years old. | French Ministry of Culture, Chauvet "Human trail" |
| San trackers in French caves | In 2013 three Ju/'hoan trackers read footprints in four French caves: at least 24 people, 10 children or teenagers; at Tuc d'Audoubert a man and a teenage boy carrying clay to the bison sculptures. Readings debated. | Pastoors & Lenssen-Erz (eds), *Reading Prehistoric Human Tracks* 2021; *Science News* 2015 |
| Handaxe learning | 26 beginners trained up to 90 hours; fast progress for about 30 hours, then a plateau; most stayed below expert quality. | Pargeter, Khreisheh & Stout, *J. Human Evolution* 2019 |
| Étiolles | About 15,000 years ago: master knappers worked the best flint at the central hearth; learners practised on second-rate blocks at the camp edges; their work ranges from clumsy to nearly competent. | French Ministry of Culture, Étiolles; Pigeot 1987 |
| Teaching with words | 184 volunteers learning Oldowan flaking: verbal teaching nearly doubled total quality versus working alone (23.6 vs 13.0); imitation added only about 20%. | Morgan et al., *Nature Communications* 2015 |
| Day and night talk | Ju/'hoan day talk: 34% complaints, 31% economic, fewer than 1 in 10 conversations with stories; 81% of long night conversations by the fire were stories. | Wiessner, *PNAS* 2014 |
| Agta storytellers | Skilled storytellers about twice as likely to be chosen as campmates; about half a child more surviving on average (correlational). | Smith et al., *Nature Communications* 2017 |
| Australian sea-level stories | Aboriginal stories from 21 coastal places appear to describe land drowned by the sea more than 7,000 years ago (estimated 7,250–13,070 years). | Nunn & Reid, *Australian Geographer* 2016 |
| Aboriginal memory method | 76 medical students; after about 20–30 minutes' training they were roughly three times more likely to recall a 20-item list perfectly. | Reser et al., *PLoS ONE* 2021 |
| Mau Piailug | In 1976 he navigated Hōkūleʻa about 4,000 km from Hawaiʻi to Tahiti without instruments; his grandfather began teaching him as a small child. | Polynesian Voyaging Society archive; *Soundings* |
| Sumerian schools | By about 4,000 years ago scribes trained in the edubba ("tablet house"). The literary text "Schooldays" (copies c. 1900–1600 BCE) describes a pupil beaten repeatedly, then his father hosting the teacher with gifts, after which the teacher praises him. | Kramer, *JAOS* 1949; ETCSL 5.1.1 |
| Out of school today | About 273 million children and young people are out of school. | UNESCO GEM Report, March 2026 |
| Peer teaching | Hunter-gatherer children learn mostly by watching, copying and playing; adults teach hard skills such as hunting. In Hadza and BaYaka camps, children taught each other more often than adults taught them. | Lew-Levy et al., *Human Nature* 2017; *Evolution and Human Behavior* 2020 |
