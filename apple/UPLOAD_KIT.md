## Facts used in the script (all checked)

| Line | Source |
|---|---|
| In 1976 he owned 10% of Apple | Wikipedia: Jobs and Wozniak "would each hold a 45% stake so that Wayne could receive a 10% stake to act as a tie-breaker" |
| He sold it for $800 | Wikipedia: "Wayne was later paid $800" for relinquishing his stake; Fortune: "cashed out just 12 days after Apple's inception for $800" |
| He met Jobs and Wozniak at Atari | Wikipedia: at Atari (1973 to 1976) he "met coworkers Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak" |
| At 41 he was the adult in the room | Wikipedia: he was 41 in 1976 (born May 17, 1934), the venture's "adult in the room" |
| The two Steves took 45% each; Wayne got 10% to break ties | Wikipedia (as above) |
| He wrote the contract and drew the first Apple logo | Wikipedia: "Wayne drafted the original partnership agreement" and "created the first illustrations of the Apple logo" |
| Jobs took on $15,000 in credit for the first big order | Wikipedia: Jobs "secured a $15,000 line of credit to purchase materials needed to fabricate Apple's first order placed by the Byte Shop" |
| In a partnership, every partner is on the hook for every debt | Wikipedia: "all members of a partnership are personally responsible for any debts incurred by any partner" |
| The Steves had almost nothing to lose; Wayne did | Wikipedia: Wayne "had already acquired a sizable amount of personal assets" (the Steves had few) |
| Twelve days later he took his name off | Wikipedia: "Twelve days after Wayne created the document that formally created Apple, he returned to the county registrar's office" |
| Later he took $1,500 more to give up any claim | Wikipedia: "one year later accepted additional payment $1,500" to forfeit future claims |
| Apple is worth over $5 trillion | MacRumors and 9to5Mac: first close above $5 trillion on Oct 14, 2026; 24/7 Wall St: above $5 trillion on Nov 10, 2026 |
| 10% is about $500 billion | Arithmetic: 10% of $5 trillion (a tenth of today's company, not a claim about his undiluted stake) |
| He kept his copy of the contract, signed by all three | Wikipedia; the 2011 Sotheby's lot was the contract signed by Jobs, Wozniak and Wayne |
| He sold that for $500 | Wikipedia: he sold his copy "for $500" |
| In 2011 it sold at auction for $1.6 million | MacRumors and Computerworld: Sotheby's, December 2011, $1,594,500 |
| He doesn't regret leaving Apple, just selling the paper | Wikipedia: in an April 2016 interview "his one regret was selling his copy of the original signed contract for $500"; Fortune (2026): no regrets about leaving |
